China chip policy · the argument, with references

Did Beijing's chip policy pull against China's own AI companies?

Yes, on direction, with five limits: four on how, and one on what Beijing meant by it. Every statement below carries a numbered reference to a saved source.

Written Nov 23, 2026 (UTC). Made public the same day. The figure that goes with it is in the same folder: figure/beijing_vs_labs.html.

Written by Claude, Anthropic's model. Anthropic has argued in public for controls on chip exports to China (that is from my general knowledge; no saved page is cited for it), so judge this note by its sources.

The claim tested

Palmer Luckey, in a clip posted on Nov 19, 2026: “China doesn't want NVIDIA to sell GPUs to Chinese companies.” 1 And next: “there are Chinese companies that want to buy Nvidia GPUs” 2. The first sentence is from a machine transcript of the clip (0:11 to 0:16). The second is from the text of the post that carries the clip; the post begins it with the word Like, and so does a second machine transcript. A published transcript of the whole session, at the DealBook Summit 2026, has both sentences in the same words, but ends the first with two more words, like this: “Chinese companies like this. There are Chinese companies that want to buy Nvidia GPUs” 3. That page does not say how its transcript was made. He goes on, by the machine transcript (0:18 to 0:36): “There's 600,000 H200 GPUs sitting in a port right now”, because “China will not give them an import permit”, “because they want to build an internal domestic processor industry, silicon industry, not build one that is dependent on the United States” 1. Nobody has checked any of this by ear.

The answer. From 2024 to 2026 the Chinese state's declared policy, and what its regulators did, pulled China's AI companies away from Nvidia chips. The companies went on using those chips, ordering them and asking for them. Five limits apply. First, Beijing was not one actor: its security and planning side pressed against the chips, its commerce and foreign-affairs side was more open to them. Second, the pressure came as public warnings and unpublished guidance, not as a published ban, and the seven state-media items found that only warn about Nvidia's chips all fall between Aug 1 and Sept 24, 2025; three later ones mix caution with openness to the H200. Third, some purchases were approved and some chips came in. Fourth, the companies had reasons of their own, and American curbs and conditions pushed too: Nvidia's reports since November 2025 say “we are already effectively foreclosed from the China market by U.S. export controls” 4, and of the 3,444,160 chips in the licence applications that Washington's summary of May 11, 2026 records, 1,888,950 (55%; the sum is mine) were in applications it returned without action; its table gives no reason, and the committee that released it says the summary does not show every application in full 56. Fifth, what Beijing meant by it is not settled: the US-China commission saw in the market regulator's move against Nvidia, which came as the regulator dropped an antitrust probe into Google, “China's willingness to both exert leverage and ease pressure as a negotiating tactic” 7, while the Trade Representative, asked whether China would let its companies buy the H200, said it was “gonna be a sovereign decision for China” (captions, not checked by ear, at 5:59 in the recording) 8. The direction of the policy stands under either reading.

1. The state's declared line: use home-made chips

1The leader. Xi Jinping said in 2016 that the internet's core technology is China's greatest weak point and that having core technology controlled by others is its greatest hidden danger: “互联网核心技术是我们最大的‘命门’，核心技术受制于人是我们最大的隐患” 9. In the same speech he said that an internet company, however large, whose core components depend heavily on foreign countries is like a house built on someone else's foundation: “那就好比在别人的墙基上砌房子” 10. In April 2025 he told the Politburo to concentrate forces on high-end chips: “集中力量攻克高端芯片、基础软件等核心技术” 11. In October 2025 the Central Committee's recommendations for the next five-year plan called for extraordinary measures, “采取超常规措施”, in integrated circuits and other fields 12. A search of the People's Daily database of his speeches and activities for Nvidia, its chief executive's name, the H20 and the H200 found nothing, while the same search finds Qualcomm, Microsoft and Intel in his texts; the database lacks at least one of his meetings of the period, so this is a strong negative, not a complete one (the searches, with their controls, are in tables/xi_searches.tsv).

2The rules. Notices of the finance and industry ministries, published on Dec 26, 2023, require Party and government bodies at township level and above, and the public institutions attached to them, to write into their purchase requirements for desktop computers and servers that the processor and the operating system meet the security-and-reliability evaluation: “应当将CPU、操作系统符合安全可靠测评要求纳入采购需求” 1314. The two evaluation centres' results of May 26, 2026, on one centre's own site, list nine AI training and inference chips that passed, every one from a Chinese maker 15; their results of Sept 20, 2026 add two more, HiSilicon's Ascend 950 and Kunlunxin's M100 16. Firms apply of their own accord, and firms and users choose whether to use the results: “测评结果由企业和用户自主选择使用” 17. Home-made products get a 20% price preference in government purchasing: “对本国产品的报价给予20%的价格扣除” 18. The test is where a product is made, not who owns its maker: state, private and foreign-funded firms “平等享受对本国产品的政府采购支持政策” (enjoy the purchasing support for home-made products equally), and regions and departments may not, in government purchasing, name a brand or restrict where a brand is registered or who owns it 18.

4The agencies and the state's media, in public. The Ministry of State Security warned in July 2025 that some chips, smart devices and software made abroad may have had backdoors planted on purpose when they were designed and made: “一些境外生产的芯片、智能设备或者软件可能在设计制造阶段就被故意预埋了‘后门’” 44. In May 2026 Li Chao, of the state planner's policy research office, said at its press conference that it was guiding home-made large models to do more to adapt to home-made computing chips: “指导国产大模型加大力度适配国产算力芯片” 45. State media went further and named the chip. After the cyberspace regulator summoned Nvidia, a People's Daily commentary of Aug 1, 2025 said that 'sick' chips must never be allowed on duty: “绝不能让‘带病’的芯片上岗” 46. Yuyuan Tantian, an account of the state broadcaster 47, wrote on Aug 10, 2025 that the H20 was neither green, nor advanced, nor safe, and: “作为消费者，我们当然可以选择，不买” (as consumers we can of course choose not to buy) 48. Of 42 state and Party media items on Nvidia chips found from July 2025 to November 2026, the seven that only warn about them all date from Aug 1 to Sept 24, 2025 4649. No later item found is a warning alone; three, of Dec 13 and 14, 2025 and Jan 7, 2026, mix caution with openness to the H200, the Economic Daily piece quoted below among them (the count and the reading of each item are in tables/state_media_items.tsv). An industry body had urged caution about American chips in general on Dec 3, 2024: the Internet Society of China called on Chinese companies to be prudent in choosing to buy them, “审慎选择采购美国芯片”, giving Washington's export controls as its ground, and in the same sentence to use chips made in China by domestic and foreign-funded firms alike, “积极使用内外资企业在华生产制造的芯片” 50. When Washington cleared the H200, a signed commentary in the Economic Daily said that Chinese users might buy it selectively but would not again pay for the whole lot, “中国用户可能会选择性购买H200芯片，但不会再全盘埋单”, and foresaw a market on two tracks, “‘多元采购+自主为主’的双轨模式” (buying from many sources, with home-made first) 51.

2. What the companies used and wanted: Nvidia

5A Shanghai government body says so. Shanghai's Commission of Economy and Informatization wrote, in a reply dated Apr 30, 2025 and published on its site on Nov 10, 2025, that in the city the internet companies' own computing clusters, which serve market demand, run mainly on Nvidia high-performance cards, “互联网企业自建算力集群，满足市场化企业需求，主要以英伟达高性能卡为主”, while the three big telecom operators mainly buy Huawei and other home-made clusters, “三大运营商主要购置华为等国产化智算集群”, to diversify the city's computing 52.

6The Party's journal, a chip maker and a model maker say why. An investigation by a joint study group of the Party journal Qiushi's economics desk and the research institute CCID, carried by the National Data Administration in May 2026, found that moving off Nvidia's software is costly and slow: “迁移成本高昂、适配周期漫长” 53. A Chinese maker of AI chips, Enflame, says in its final prospectus of Sept 8, 2026 that international makers led by Nvidia are ahead of home-made ones in hardware, software and systems, “均领先于国产厂商” 54. Its reply to the exchange's listing inquiry says that at the start of the period its accounts cover, when home-made AI chips were not yet mature, internet companies bought mainly international products for commercial reasons: “出于商业化考量，互联网行业客户采购主要以国际产品为主”; and that after the state's East Data, West Computing hub project began at the end of 2022, computing-centre projects became one of the main early sources of demand for home-made AI chips: “智算中心项目随之成为国产AI芯片初期主要的需求场景之一” 55. A model maker says the same of its rivals. iFlytek's record of its results meeting of Aug 21, 2026: other model makers commonly use Nvidia chips two generations ahead in performance, with a better ecosystem and more efficient training, “其他模型厂商普遍使用性能领先两代的英伟达芯片，其生态更完善，各类算法的训练与移植效率更高”. iFlytek itself is on the US Entity List, the same record says 56. Its report for the first half of 2026 says home-made computing “对比英伟达中端芯片H200尚存在明显差距” (still falls clearly short of Nvidia's mid-range H200 chip), though it says a model was trained wholly on it in April 57.

8The pushing back. The Financial Times, Aug 20, 2025: Chinese regulators had urged more use of home-made chips in recent years, “but tech giants from Alibaba to ByteDance argued that their AI development would be impaired without Nvidia's chips”; the paper adds that, by some close to the companies, “they have become more accepting of a switch, especially for” inference 64. Reuters's headline of Sept 4, 2025: “Chinese firms still want Nvidia chips despite government pressure not to buy, sources say” 65. Nori Chiou, an investment director at White Oak Capital Partners, quoted by Reuters in December 2025, said cloud companies were “lobbying the government to relax restrictions on a conditional basis” 66. On Jul 30, 2026 a company asked the commerce ministry, on its public message board, how to import H200 chips lawfully: “我司计划合规进口H200数据中心GPU” 67. The ministry's reply of Aug 19, 2026 speaks only of what exporters must do; it does not answer the question about imports. A Chinese trade site, Leiphone, Aug 31, 2025, quoted a salesman it did not name, who was still upbeat about the H20 market: “国内还是有需求的，但至于现在能不能采，什么时候采就不太清楚” (there is still demand at home, but whether one can buy now, and when, is not very clear). It also quoted a manager who had worked closely with Nvidia, under a name the site says is a pseudonym, Zheng Xing: there was hardly any demand for the H20, state firms and other government-led buyers would have none, and “BAT也在响应国家号召，不会再买新的H20” (Baidu, Alibaba and Tencent, too, are answering the state's call and will not buy new H20s) 68. Hanna Dohmen, a research analyst at Georgetown University's Center for Security and Emerging Technology (CSET), put the general point in her prepared statement for a hearing of the US-China commission, Feb 6, 2025: before the American controls “the Chinese government fought against the strong pull of commercial incentives”, and “Chinese consumers preferred to use the highest-performance chips designed by Nvidia and other foreign firms” 69.

3. What Beijing did about it

Points 10 to 12 rest on documents, filings and named officials; points 13 to 16 on press reports citing unnamed people. Point 16 also sets Washington's own table of licence applications, a document, beside those reports.

On the record

10Regulators acted against Nvidia by name. The market regulator announced on Dec 9, 2024 that it had opened an antitrust case: “市场监管总局依法对英伟达公司开展立案调查” 83. On Jul 31, 2025 the cyberspace regulator summoned Nvidia, “约谈了英伟达公司”, over the security risks of vulnerabilities and backdoors in the H20 chips it sells in China 84. On Sept 15, 2025 the market regulator announced a preliminary finding against Nvidia, and a further investigation: “市场监管总局依法决定对其实施进一步调查” 85. That notice cites the antimonopoly law and the regulator's 2020 conditions on Nvidia's purchase of Mellanox, and gives no facts. Nvidia's own account of the finding points the other way: “our compliance with applicable U.S. export controls, which required us to offer degraded products to the Chinese market, discriminated unfairly against customers in the China market” 86. By that account the case faults Nvidia for what it held back from Chinese customers, not for selling to them. The regulator's Party group, in the Party members' paper of Sept 24, 2026, counts the case as political work: “依法对英伟达、高通公司、杜邦中国集团立案调查，在重要时间节点上发布权威声音” (it opened cases against Nvidia, Qualcomm and DuPont China under the law, and spoke with authority at important moments) 87. Xinhua, in March 2026, had called the same enforcement even-handed: “坚持对各类企业一视同仁” 88. American bodies read the case as a lever in trade talks. The US-China commission's report of November 2025, citing Reuters and the Financial Times, says the finding came “in the midst of negotiations”, as the regulator dropped an inquiry into Google, “signaling China's willingness to both exert leverage and ease pressure as a negotiating tactic” 89. The White House said on Nov 1, 2025 that “China will terminate its various investigations targeting U.S. companies in the semiconductor supply chain, including its antitrust, anti-monopoly, and anti-dumping investigations” 90; the commerce ministry's account of the deal names none of China's investigations of American firms and, of such companies, says only “相关企业个案处理” (handling the cases of the companies concerned) 91. The Trade Representative's report of 2026 says that by the end of 2025 China “had terminated or suspended these investigations”, which “were being pursued for retaliatory purposes”; it names no company 92. No page read says the Nvidia case ended: the regulator's review of its 2025 work, posted Apr 10, 2026, still lists “对英伟达公司实施进一步调查” 93; its news list to Nov 23, 2026 has a suspension of the case against DuPont China and none for Nvidia 9495; and Nvidia's report of August 2026 still lists the finding among its risks 86.

6. What can be said, and how

  • Flat, on documents and filings. China's declared policy is to end dependence on foreign core technology and to raise the share of home-made chips (points 1 to 3). Its regulators opened a case against Nvidia and summoned it (point 10). Nvidia told securities regulators in November 2025 that the Chinese government “restricted customers from purchasing, importing, or using our data center products” (the annual report says “discouraged” 101), and, two sentences later, that “we are already effectively foreclosed from the China market by U.S. export controls” 4. Alibaba told them the cyberspace regulator asked companies to halt purchases of certain chips (point 11). A Shanghai government body says internet companies' clusters in the city run mainly on Nvidia (point 5). By May 11, 2026 Washington had approved 879,868 of Nvidia's and AMD's chips, none yet shipped, and returned 1,888,950 without action (section 4). Nvidia's revenue from customers based in China fell by about two thirds during 2025 and was, in the quarter ended Oct 25, 2026, the highest of the nine quarters reported on that basis; that is not a count of chips entering China (section 4).
  • In public, as warnings and urging, not as orders. The security ministry's warning, the state-media commentaries (found from August to September 2025), the state broadcaster's account saying buyers could choose not to buy the H20, and an industry body's call for caution (points 4 and 18). Nothing read ties the state-media texts to the guidance the press reports, and a Party paper also printed a scholar's advice to stock imported chips (point 17).
  • As reported, from unnamed people. Who gave the instructions to stop buying, and when; the warning about traitors; the customs checks; the 600,000 chips in Hong Kong; every number in the first table of section 4 except the United States government's count and Nvidia's own.
  • As others read it. That Beijing's acts were leverage in trade talks, or a sovereign choice with no bargain in them (point 21).
  • Not to be said. That China banned Nvidia chips: no published ban was found, and some chips came in. That an import permit was refused: no permit rule was found. That Beijing spoke with one voice: its commerce side and its security and planning side pulled apart (point 17). That the companies wanted only Nvidia: they also build and buy home-made chips. That the antitrust case shows Beijing wanted no Nvidia sales: by Nvidia's account the finding is that it offered Chinese customers degraded products. That the case was a bargaining move, or that it was not: both are readings (point 21). That only Beijing stood in the H200's way: Washington's conditions and a 25% tariff did too, and Washington returned without action 55% of the chips in the applications its summary of May 11, 2026 records. That Washington refused the chips it returned: the table gives no reason.

One sentence that survives all five limits. From 2024 the security and planning side of the Chinese state pressed China's AI companies, through unpublished guidance that the press reported, to buy home-made chips rather than Nvidia's, and from the summer of 2025 to stop ordering them, while the security ministry warned in public of backdoors in chips made abroad and state media warned about Nvidia's chips, most of all in August and September 2025; other parts of the state welcomed Nvidia and approved some purchases; the companies went on ordering Nvidia chips in the millions and, by the reports, had received a small part of them by late 2026; they were also turning to home-made chips, for reasons of their own and under American curbs; and whether Beijing meant its acts as lasting policy or as leverage in trade talks is read both ways.
