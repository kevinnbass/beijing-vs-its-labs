China · Nvidia’s H200 chip · Dec 2025 to Nov 2026

Reported ordered: more than 2 million H200 chips.
Reported delivered: about 10,000 each, to a few buyers.

Five press reports citing unnamed people, and two sums from one United States government table of licence applications. They are separate counts, not one tally: they do not add up, and they are not stages of one sale. They are set side by side by what they count, not in date order. Read with care

  • The bars count different things at different dates, so no bar is a share of another. Five rest on unnamed people; two are sums of a government table.
  • Washington’s table, “H200 License and Equivalent Application Status as of May 11, 2026”, gives the status of each licence application. The House Foreign Affairs Committee made it public on Nov 19, 2026; its summary cites “a BIS summary provided to the House Foreign Affairs Committee dated May 11, 2026”. BIS is the Commerce Department’s Bureau of Industry and Security. The table covers 3,444,160 chips in 54 applications: “Covered integrated circuits are the following products: NVIDIA’s H200 and AMD’s MI325X.” It does not say which chip a row is; bars 2 and 3 count the rows with Nvidia as exporter. An application names a data-centre operator or ultimate consignee; it is not an order. “Approved” in bar 2 is Washington’s licence; bar 4 is Beijing’s approval, by a press report.
  • The sums are ours, of the table’s rows as the project read them from its page images; the totals equal the figures the committee’s summary prints. Nvidia’s chips and AMD’s are not added in the bars. With AMD’s, the approved count is 879,868 in 21 applications and the returned count 1,888,950 in 9.
  • The 55% is a share of the chips in the applications the summary records, not of everything applied for. The committee’s example: “For example, NVIDIA applied on December 19, 2025, to export 445,000 H200s to Tencent”. The table’s only Tencent row of that date is for 75,000 chips, approved; neither document says whether it is that application. The share would be 49.5% if it is, counted at 445,000, and 48.6% if the 445,000 is a further application and was not returned without action (our arithmetic).
  • No approved application in the table is above 75,000 chips, and all five above that number were returned. On May 14, 2026 Reuters, by two people it did not name, reported a limit for each approved buyer: “each approved customer can purchase up to 75,000 chips”. It also wrote that “not a single delivery has been made so far”, and that the “path to a completed sale has been obstructed by a tangle of requirements on both sides”.
  • The approval Reuters reported on Jan 28, 2026 was not yet a purchase: a source said “customers were not yet converting the approvals to purchase orders”.
  • The possible total of July (fewer than 200,000) was, in the report’s words, “less than half of what the companies requested earlier this year”.
  • The report of Aug 18, 2026 names two buyers. A later report speaks of several: “Several major Chinese internet companies have each obtained about 10,000 H200 chips in the past few months” (Reuters, Nov 5, 2026).
  • Two later accounts say otherwise of the orders reported in bar 1. Nvidia’s chief executive, the day after the report of Beijing’s approvals, said “我认为中国政府仍在决策中，我们尚未收到采购订单” (I think the Chinese government is still deciding; we have not yet received purchase orders), as Voice of America’s Chinese service, Jan 29, 2026, renders him. TechWeb, as Sina carries it, reported on Jul 17, 2026, from industry insiders it did not name, that “国内AI厂商几乎未对英伟达H200芯片下达批量采购订单” (China’s AI firms had placed almost no bulk purchase orders for Nvidia’s H200). English in round brackets is ours, not an official translation.
  • Nvidia’s figures are in money, not in chips: a share of its revenue in the quarterly report, a sum of its sales on the call. Neither its call of Nov 17, 2026 nor Reuters’s report of it says how many of those chips crossed into the mainland.
  • No report here says how many H200 chips have entered the mainland. The Hong Kong report says small quantities were shipped by other routes, in its words, “without specifying how many”.
