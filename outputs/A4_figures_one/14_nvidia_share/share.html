Read with care

  • Not one series. The 95% is Reuters’s own sentence, with no date, no count and no source named. The 55% and 34% are IDC’s counts. The chart does not join the first to the others.
  • TrendForce’s figures are a third measure. For 2025 it gives all foreign suppliers together 34%, where IDC’s count, as Reuters reported it, gives Nvidia 55% and AMD 4%. The two firms do not measure the same thing, so neither is drawn on the other’s chart. TrendForce’s 2026 figures are a forecast made in June 2026.
  • The 2025 share has two sources. Reuters reported IDC’s count. Enflame, a Chinese maker of AI chips, gives the same 55% in its prospectus, from IDC’s data and its own sales. English in round brackets is ours, not an official translation.
  • Not like for like on the Chinese side. For 2025 the report gives all Chinese makers together (41%). For 2026 it names five, which add up to 47% (our addition), and leaves 19% not broken down (our subtraction). The report says: “Nine of the 11 Chinese chipmakers tracked by IDC increased shipments from a year earlier”. So 47% is the least the Chinese makers held, not the whole.
  • Huawei’s share in 2025 is not given as a percentage. The report says it led the Chinese makers, “shipping around 812,000 AI chips, roughly half of all domestically branded shipments”.
  • What the 34% counts is not fully known: “IDC did not specify which Nvidia products made up its 34% market share”.
  • Nvidia’s chief executive has put it lower. Reuters, May 14, 2026, reports him “saying its share of AI accelerators in China has effectively fallen to zero”.
  • AMD appears in the 2025 count only (4%). The 2026 report does not mention AMD, so nothing is drawn for it there.
  • A share of chips shipped in a period is not a share of chips in use.
