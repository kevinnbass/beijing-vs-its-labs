Did Beijing’s chip policy pull against China’s own AI companies? Yes on direction, with five limits.

On the record. China’s state planner says it is guiding home-made AI models to do more to adapt to home-made chips1. Nvidia told US securities regulators in November 2025 that China’s government “restricted customers from purchasing, importing, or using our data center products”2. Yet a Shanghai government body wrote that in the city the clusters internet companies build themselves run mainly on Nvidia cards3.

Reported, from unnamed people. Chinese companies ordered “more than 2 million H200 chips for 2026”4. In November 2026, about 600,000 were stored in Hong Kong while Beijing withheld approval for large-scale imports5.

Five limits. (1) Beijing was not one actor: its commerce minister said he hoped Nvidia would supply Chinese customers6. (2) No published ban was found in the 82 policy pages read: state media warned in public (of the 19 items found that only warn, 16 date from Jul 31 to Sept 24, 2025, and three are later), and the press reported “guidance rather than an outright ban”7. (3) Some chips were reported let in5. (4) American curbs pushed too: Nvidia calls itself “already effectively foreclosed from the China market by U.S. export controls”2; Washington returned without action 55% of the chips in the applications its summary of May 11, 2026 records8. (5) Beijing’s aim is read both ways: a US commission saw it willing to “exert leverage and ease pressure as a negotiating tactic”9; the Trade Representative called letting its companies buy “a sovereign decision for China” (captions, not checked by ear, at 5:59)10.
