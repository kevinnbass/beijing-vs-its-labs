China · home-made chip targets and what was in use · 2023 to 2026

Draft by Claude, Anthropic’s model.

On paper, half or more home‑made. In practice, Nvidia was still the biggest single supplier.

Targets that nine of China’s 31 provincial-level governments set for computing (eight for home-made chips, equipment or computing, one for what it calls independently controllable), beside what a Shanghai government body wrote and what a research firm counted. The two sides do not measure the same thing, so each has its own panel and its own scale. By the counts, Nvidia stayed the largest maker while its share fell.

No published national plan found in the 82 policy pages read sets a home-made share for computing centres. Two national texts among them hold a home-made percentage of other kinds: a 20% price preference for home-made goods where they compete with others in a government purchase, and an advisory committee’s roadmap of October 2015 that expected home-made products in several fields to reach stated shares of the home market by 2025, among them high-performance computers and servers at over 80% and mobile-terminal chips at 40% (guidance, not an instruction, in the words of the industry ministry’s vice minister at its launch). Bloomberg reported in June 2026, from unnamed people, a draft national plan with “at least 80 per cent of the core technology, AI chips included” from local suppliers; no such plan was found published. Source: National Development and Reform Commission and four other bodies, opinions of Dec 25, 2023.

Scale of the ten tracks: a share in that place, as its plan defines it. A goal, not a count. Of the other 22 provincial-level governments, 20 have a plan that was read and sets no share, one is known only from accounts of its plan (its issuer’s own among them), and for one no computing plan was found (tables/provinces_31.tsv).

Shanghai is on both sides of this figure 8 11

Its plan set the goal of over 50% home-made chips in new centres by 2025. Its commission’s reply of Apr 30, 2025 describes the clusters that internet companies run. A goal for new centres and a description of clusters in use are still two different things.

Read with care: what each one measures

Who says itWhat it measuresWhere and when
The targetsa provincial-level government, in a plana goal: a share of the chips, equipment or computing in that place. Three count home-made chips (Jiangsu and Shanghai in new centres, Tianjin in all); Guangdong, Inner Mongolia and Shandong home-made computing (Guangdong in what is newly added); Zhejiang home-made computing in one sector, its information and communications industry; Sichuan home-made equipment; Hebei software and hardware called independently controllable. Jiangsu’s plan marks its own target as expected, not binding; Shandong’s is known from a newspaper’s accountone government each; goals for 2025, 2026 and 2030, and a plan that runs to 2027

So the two sides cannot be set on one scale: a provincial or city target is not tested by national shipments, and neither number proves or disproves the other. The research firm’s count is of what was shipped, not of what any one buyer uses. English renderings of Chinese are ours, not official translations.
