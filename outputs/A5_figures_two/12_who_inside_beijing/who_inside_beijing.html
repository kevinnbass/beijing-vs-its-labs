Inside Beijing, security and planning bodies held Nvidia back. The commerce side welcomed it.

36 acts, each from a saved source, sorted by who acted and by the kind of act. The cyberspace regulator, the security ministry and the state planner appear only on the side that points away from Nvidia. The commerce ministry appears only on the side that points toward it: it welcomed Nvidia in public, spoke up for access and, by press reports, approved purchases. The antitrust case against Nvidia the company is set apart.

How one newspaper put the split

The Financial Times, Nov 13, 2026, in a passage that names no source: the commerce ministry “favoured imports”, while “the national security-focused officials within” the cyberspace regulator and the security ministry “viewed dependence on Nvidia as a strategic vulnerability”. “Over the past year, the latter position has gained ground.” The paper speaks of officials within those two bodies, not of the bodies as such.

And an American official

The US Trade Representative, on Bloomberg Television, video posted Nov 20, 2026 (captions, not checked by ear; 1:06 to 1:35): the Chinese side is “not as monolithic as people would have you believe”. In his “own analysis” there are some who think “we’d like to have access” and “others who think, well, we don’t want American chips anyway”. The video’s two caption tracks break the sentence in different places, so whether he puts the split inside the government needs a listen.

Read with care

  • Open marks rest on unnamed people. In the answers found, Beijing’s spokespeople confirmed none of them: “我不了解你提到的情况” (I am not aware of what you mention), said the foreign ministry on Aug 13, 2025; asked on Nov 9, 2026 about the chips stored in Hong Kong, it said “具体情况建议向中方主管部门询问” (for the details, ask the competent Chinese departments).
  • The marks are a selection, not a count. An act by several bodies is drawn once in each body’s row, with the same number. The grouping of the rows is ours. Eight of the named rows (each a body, a group of bodies or a person) have a mark on the side that points away from Nvidia and four on the side that points toward it; the industry ministry is on both.
  • The state-media marks are warnings, not orders. Of 75 state and Party media items on Nvidia chips found from Jul 2025 to Nov 2026, 16 of the 19 that only warn about them date from Jul 31, 2025 to Sept 24, 2025, and the three found later are in Global Times in 2026; seven of the 75 mix a warning with a welcome. The items after September 2025 are what two rounds of searches found, a sample and not a full count. The security ministry’s article names no maker.
  • No published order against buying Nvidia chips was found in the 82 policy pages read. The 2023 purchase rules bind Party and government bodies from township level up, the public institutions directly under Party committees and governments at those levels, and the departments’ public institutions that provide support to those bodies; other units may leave their requirement out. No published national plan found sets a home-made share for computing centres: every such share found is a province’s or a city’s (9 of 31 provincial-level governments were found to set one for home-made computing; one of them, Zhejiang’s, is for the computing of one sector, the province’s information and communications industry, by 2030, as a project’s target), and the national computing-network opinions of December 2023 set none. These shares are not drawn.
  • The chip at issue in these marks changed: the H20 (2024 to 2025), the RTX Pro 6000D (2025), the H200 (from December 2025). English renderings of Chinese are ours, not official translations.
