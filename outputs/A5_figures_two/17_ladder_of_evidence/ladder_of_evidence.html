What it shows about this claim

The declared line: end dependence on foreign core technology and use home-made chips. The purchase rules for state bodies, the provincial targets, the antitrust case and the summons. A Shanghai government body’s own statement that internet companies’ clusters run mainly on Nvidia. Not on this rung: Any order telling companies not to buy Nvidia chips. None was found in the 103 policy pages read. The commentaries warn and urge; none forbids.

Company filings and own notices

what companies told securities regulators and investors: filings, results releases, the company’s own transcript of a results call; and two universities’ own purchase notices

38sources

What it shows about this claim

Alibaba told the US securities regulator that the cyberspace regulator asked firms to halt purchases of certain chips. Nvidia told it that the Chinese government restricted, and later discouraged, customers’ purchases, and that its licensed sales were restricted; the same reports say it is foreclosed from the China market by US export controls. On its own results calls Nvidia said in May 2026 that it had no revenue yet from the licensed chips, and in November 2026 that it had sold $2.4 billion of licensed Hopper products to China in a quarter and was no longer shipping. Alibaba also says its own chips are in scaled production. Two universities posted notices to buy an Nvidia card in July 2026.

Not on this rung: Who gave the instruction, or how. Alibaba’s sentence does not name Nvidia. Nvidia gave no reason for stopping. A notice to buy is neither a purchase made nor a delivery.

Read with care

  • A rung says how a statement reached us, not whether it is true. A document can be wrong and an unnamed person right. A quotation that was found in a saved copy shows what the source says.
  • The rungs follow the argument’s own labels for its 209 sources; the same source sits on one rung only. The argument has eleven kinds of source; this figure puts its state documents and the published commentary of state media and an industry body on rung 1, filings and two universities’ notices on rung 2, officials, official records and one analyst’s testimony on rung 3, and reports of named people’s words and one newspaper’s account of a state document on rung 4. Rungs 1 to 4 hold exactly the kinds the argument counts as on the record. A named person’s words on rung 4 still pass through a reporter, a post or a transcript site.
  • What the count cannot weigh: one company filing may carry more than several press reports. The count of squares is not a score.
  • English renderings of Chinese are ours, not official translations.
