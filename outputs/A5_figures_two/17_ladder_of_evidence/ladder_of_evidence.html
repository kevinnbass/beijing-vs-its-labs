98sources

What it shows about this claim

Alibaba told the US securities regulator that the cyberspace regulator asked Chinese technology companies to halt purchases of certain chips. Nvidia told it that the Chinese government restricted, and later discouraged, customers’ purchases, and that its licensed sales were restricted; the same reports say it is effectively foreclosed from the China market by US export controls. On its own results calls Nvidia said in May 2026 that it had no revenue yet from the licensed chips, and in November 2026 that it had sold $2.4 billion of licensed Hopper products to China in a quarter and was no longer shipping. Alibaba also says its own chips are in scaled production. Listed Chinese firms told the Shenzhen exchange of purchases of cards named PRO 5500. Universities’ own notices of 2026 seek, buy or rent Nvidia cards, from two cards to six to eight eight-card servers; some central buyers’ notices of November 2026 ask for home-made computing. An award of April 2025 by the purchasing centre for central state bodies, for a university’s IT centre, names Nvidia H20 modules under a tender that allowed no imported products. None of the 32 pages of the carriers’ own read (notices, one filing and one news page) names Nvidia or an Nvidia model. Some ask for home-made chips by maker or by origin, or label their lots by software ecosystem; at least 10 of the 32 ask for nothing of the kind. One other notice held names Nvidia cards, a small one of 2024: a Jiangsu company that bears China Unicom’s name and publishes its tender on China Unicom’s procurement site (the notice names no owner) asks for cards it writes as NV RTX 3090, NV RTX 4090 and NV Tesla A100. Three other buyers’ notices of 2026 ask that AI chips meet the security-and-reliability evaluation: China Post’s Shandong branch (once results for such cards are out), Shanghai Pudong Development Bank, and a unit of China Southern Power Grid (for one lot).

Not on this rung: Who gave an instruction about Nvidia chips, or how it was given. Alibaba’s sentence names the cyberspace regulator but not Nvidia, and does not say how the request was made. Nvidia gave no reason for stopping. A notice to buy is not a purchase made, and a contract is not a delivery.
