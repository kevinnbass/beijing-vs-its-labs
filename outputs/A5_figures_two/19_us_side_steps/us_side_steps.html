Our sums of the table’s 54 rows; they match the committee’s own totals. Nvidia’s and AMD’s chips are added together, as in the table; a third exporter, Semsotai USA, has 32,850 chips in 4 applications, all still submitted. Applications returned without action hold 55% of the chips in the applications the summary records; they include every application above 75,000 chips (five) and every one whose ultimate location is Malaysia, Indonesia or Thailand (1,118,500 chips). The table gives no reasons. The committee says the summary does not show every application in full; for example, Nvidia applied on Dec 19, 2025 “to export 445,000 H200s to Tencent”. The table’s only Tencent row of that date is for 75,000, approved, and neither document says whether it is that application. If it is, counted at 445,000, the share in applications returned without action would be 49.5%; if the 445,000 is a further application and was not returned without action, 48.6% (our arithmetic).

The steps are in date order; the spacing is not to scale, and no bar is drawn because the numbers share no scale. A quarter’s figure sits at the date it was made public.

Read with care

  • Sold or shipped “to China” is not the same as arrived in the mainland. The Financial Times, citing people it did not name, reported in August that small batches “have been allowed to enter mainland China” and, by one of the people, that “Beijing wants them to keep most of these chips outside the mainland” (the paper’s own text, on Yahoo Finance). Where the chips sold later are now is not known.
  • The licence table is Washington’s own record, looking back. It gives each application’s status on May 11, 2026, and was made public on Nov 19, 2026 by the House Foreign Affairs Committee, by unanimous consent. An application names a data-centre operator or ultimate consignee; it is not an order, and an approval there is Washington’s licence, not Beijing’s.
  • This is the American side only. Why so little went out is a separate question: Nvidia’s filing says such sales “were restricted by the PRC government”, and the Commerce Secretary gave his understanding in these words: “Chinese central government has not let them as of yet buy the chips because they’re trying to keep their investment focused on their own domestic industry”.
  • The last quarter’s filing was absent from the SEC index. The SEC’s filing index, saved Nov 25, 2026, did not list a Form 10-Q for the quarter ended Oct 25, 2026. Its commentary on the quarter says: “We plan to file our 10-Q in the coming weeks.” The $2.4 billion is from its results call and that commentary.
  • Open marks rest on unnamed people. The two ceilings (75,000 and 100,000 a firm) come from two different reports. Washington’s table states no ceiling; no approved application in it is above 75,000.
  • The H200 is, in Reuters’s words, “Nvidia’s second-most powerful AI chip”. Nvidia’s board documentation lists the H20 and H200 under Hopper. BIS is the Commerce Department’s Bureau of Industry and Security; by the committee’s summary the approvals were BIS’s.
