### Sentence L3 · 0:10 to 0:16

"And what they don't understand is China doesn't want NVIDIA to sell GPUs to Chinese companies." [1] **Borne out on direction; too flat as worded.** This is the claim the project tested. On direction the record bears it out. Nvidia told US securities regulators, in its report signed Nov 19, 2025, that the Chinese government "restricted customers from purchasing, importing, or using our data center products" [5]. Alibaba told them: "In September 2025, the Cyberspace Administration of China requested Chinese technology companies to halt purchases of certain chips." [6] That sentence does not name Nvidia. As a flat statement it goes too far. The argument sets five limits on it. First, China was not one actor: Reuters reported on Jan 30, 2026, on purchases of the H200 by DeepSeek, ByteDance, Alibaba and Tencent, that "China's industry and commerce ministries have granted approvals for all four companies" [7]. Second, the pressure came as public warnings and unpublished guidance, not as a published ban: Bloomberg's report of September 2024 said "The policy has taken the form of guidance rather than an outright ban" [8]. Third, some purchases were approved and some chips came in (sentence L5). Fourth, the companies had reasons of their own, and American curbs and conditions pushed too: the same Nvidia report says "we are already effectively foreclosed from the China market by U.S. export controls" [5]. Fifth, what Beijing meant by it is not settled (sentence L5, the last part).

## How the two clips fit together

Pottinger blames Jensen Huang for China's AI models. Luckey says Beijing kept Nvidia's chips out. Both can hold, for different chips and years. Chinese companies did train models on Nvidia chips (sentences P4 and P12). By Washington's own table, none of the Nvidia chips it had approved by May 11, 2026 had shipped (sentence P12). On the H200 chips at issue in 2026, Nvidia's report for the quarter to Jul 26, 2026 says shipments under the H200 licences "account for less than 1% of Data Center revenue in our most recent quarter" [20]. On its results call for the quarter ended Oct 25, 2026 it said: "Q3 sales of licensed Hopper Data Center products to China was $2.4 billion", and then "we are no longer shipping licensed Hopper Data Center products to China" [43]. It gave no reason, and it did not say how many of those chips crossed into mainland China.
