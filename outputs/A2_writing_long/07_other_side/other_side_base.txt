## The case, in seven parts

The claim under test: Beijing's chip policy pulled against China's own AI companies, which wanted Nvidia's chips. The strongest reply is that there was a preference, not a conflict, and that part of what stood between the companies and Nvidia's chips was American.

1. **A vice premier and a minister welcomed Nvidia, and ministries were reported to approve purchases.** Vice Premier He Lifeng told Nvidia's chief executive on Apr 17, 2025 that Nvidia was welcome [cn-policy-he-welcomes]. The commerce minister told him on Jul 17, 2025 that he hoped Nvidia would supply Chinese customers [cn-policy-wang-nvidia]. Reuters, Jan 30, 2026, on Yahoo Finance, from unnamed people, on the H200: "China's industry and commerce ministries have granted approvals for all four companies" (DeepSeek, ByteDance, Alibaba and Tencent) [cn-ga31-sv1-reuters-deepseek-yahoo].

Washington's table of licence applications shows that by May 11, 2026 it had returned without action 55% of the chips in the applications its summary records. It gives no reason, and the House committee that released it says the summary does not show every application in full [cn-usdocs-hfac-decisions-20261119] [cn-usdocs-hfac-summary-20261119]. The person who told Reuters that Chinese firms pulled back after guidance from Beijing also said the shift "was partly triggered by changes on the U.S. side" [cn-acts-reuters-us-approvals-20260514-wayback].

## What this case cannot explain

- **Two filings.** Nvidia told the US securities regulator on Nov 19, 2025 that the Chinese government "restricted customers from purchasing, importing, or using our data center products" [man-ms-sec-10q-2025-10]. Alibaba told it: "In September 2025, the Cyberspace Administration of China requested Chinese technology companies to halt purchases of certain chips." [cn-clips-alibaba-20f] That sentence does not name Nvidia.
- **Orders of which only a small part arrived** (reported, from unnamed people). Reuters reported in December 2025 that Chinese companies "have placed orders for more than 2 million H200 chips for 2026" [cn-labs-h200-two-million-202512-relay1-wayback]; in August 2026, relaying the Financial Times, that ByteDance and Tencent had each received "about 10,000 H200 processors" [cn-tolerate-reutersaug26-relay1-wayback]; and in November 2026 that about 600,000 chips were stored in Hong Kong "as Beijing withholds approval for large-scale imports despite demand from technology firms" [cn-clips-evidence-1]. All three reports cite unnamed people.
!! cn-clips-evidence-1 | About 600,000 of Nvidia's advanced artificial intelligence chips are stored in Hong Kong awaiting shipment to China
- **Licences, and sales that stopped.** Nvidia, on its call of May 20, 2026, of chips Washington had licensed: "we have yet to generate any revenue, and we are uncertain whether any imports will be allowed into the country" [cn-o38-nvda-q1fy27-call-transcript-pdf]. On its call of Nov 17, 2026 it reported that "Q3 sales of licensed Hopper Data Center products to China was $2.4 billion", and said: "we are no longer shipping licensed Hopper Data Center products to China" [cn-o38-nvda-q3fy27-call-transcript-pdf]; Hopper is the family of chips that includes the H200 [cn-acts-reuters-halt-20261118-relay2].
!! cn-o38-nvda-q3fy27-call-transcript-pdf | All our statements are made as of today, November 17, 2026
!! cn-acts-reuters-halt-20261118-relay2 | The Hopper family includes the H200
!! cn-o38-nvda-q1fy27-call-transcript-pdf | Corrected Transcript 20-May-2026
!! cn-o38-nvda-q1fy27-call-transcript-pdf | While the US government has approved licenses for H200 to be shipped to China-based customers

Each part of that case is in the record, some as a reading or a company's own claim. Together they limit the claim: Beijing was not one actor; it warned in public and, by the press reports, gave unpublished guidance, and no published ban was found; some chips came in; the companies had reasons of their own and American curbs pushed too; and what Beijing meant is not settled. They do not show that nothing stood between the companies and the chips they ordered.
