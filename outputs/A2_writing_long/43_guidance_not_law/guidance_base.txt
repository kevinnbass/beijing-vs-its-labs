## Pressure without a rule

From 2024 the security and planning side of the Chinese state pressed China's AI companies to buy home-made chips rather than Nvidia's. It did so through guidance that was never published and, in August and September 2025, through warnings in state media. No published order was found in the 103 policy pages read. Five things in the record show how that works.

**1. The press reports call it guidance** (press reports citing unnamed people)

Bloomberg, Sept 27, 2024: regulators were discouraging purchases of Nvidia's H20 chip through what it called window guidance, "instructions without the force of law" [cn-factions-bloomberg-flexibility-202409-relay1].

## What guidance did here

It left no rule to cite, and by the reports it was tightened and eased within two weeks. An unnamed person told Reuters on Jan 14, 2026 that officials' wording "is basically a ban for now, though this might change in the future should things evolve" [cn-acts-reuters-customs-20260114]. On Jan 28 Reuters reported approvals "with conditions" for three companies [ms-bj-20260128-h200-nod-standard].
!! cn-acts-reuters-customs-20260114 | PUBLISHED: 2026-01-14
!! cn-acts-reuters-customs-20260114 | The wording from the officials is so severe that it
!! ms-bj-20260128-h200-nod-standard | PUBLISHED: 2026-01-28
So ban is the wrong word for what Beijing did. Nothing is the wrong word too: Nvidia told the US securities regulator on Nov 19, 2025 that the Chinese government "restricted customers from purchasing, importing, or using our data center products" [man-ms-sec-10q-2025-10].
