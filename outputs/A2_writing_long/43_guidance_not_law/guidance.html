China chip policy · how the pressure was applied

Guidance, not law

How Beijing steered China's AI companies away from Nvidia's chips without publishing an order, shown on this one case.

Pressure without a rule

From 2024 the security and planning side of the Chinese state pressed China's AI companies to buy home-made chips rather than Nvidia's. It did so through guidance that was never published and through warnings in state media, most of those found from Jul 31 to Sept 24, 2025. No published order was found in the 82 policy pages read. Five things in the record show how that works.

1. The press reports call it guidance (press reports citing unnamed people)

Bloomberg, Sept 27, 2024: regulators were discouraging purchases of Nvidia's H20 chip through what it called window guidance, “instructions without the force of law” 1. The Financial Times, Aug 20, 2025: in July the cyberspace regulator sent companies “an informal notice”, “instructing them to halt new orders for Nvidia's H20 chips” 2. Headlines do say ban. The same paper's “China bans tech companies from buying Nvidia's AI chips”, Sept 17, 2025, describes an order told to companies, by three unnamed people, not a published rule 3. Reuters's “China bans foreign AI chips from state-funded data centres, sources say”, Nov 5, 2025, describes guidance to new data-centre projects that took state money, by two unnamed people, not a published rule 4.

2. In public, state media warned, mostly from Jul 31 to Sept 24, 2025 (on the record)

Of 75 state-media items on Nvidia chips found from July 2025 to November 2026, 11 of the 14 that only warn about them date from Jul 31 to Sept 24, 2025 567; the three found later are in Global Times in 2026 (the items after September 2025 are what two rounds of searches found, a sample and not a full count). One, from an account of the state broadcaster on Aug 10, said of the H20: “作为消费者，我们当然可以选择，不买” (as consumers we can of course choose not to buy) 89. They warn and urge. None forbids.

3. Two published texts tell buyers to stop; neither is about Nvidia (on the record)

On May 21, 2023 the cyberspace regulator's review office published its conclusion on Micron: operators of critical information infrastructure should stop buying its products 1011. On Jun 22, 2026 the finance ministry barred government buyers from the products of 46 named American firms; Nvidia is not on the list 1213. For Nvidia the cyberspace regulator published a summons, on Jul 31, 2025, and no such conclusion that we found 14.

4. The written rules cover only the state's own buying (on the record)

Notices published on Dec 26, 2023 require Party and government bodies to write into their purchases of computers and servers that the processor and operating system meet a security evaluation. The same sentence adds: “其他单位可不在采购需求中提出此项要求” (other units may leave this requirement out) 1516. No published national plan found sets a home-made share for computing centres: every such share found is a province's or a city's, and the national computing-network opinions of December 2023 set none 17. Two national texts hold a home-made percentage of other kinds: the 20% price preference for home-made products in government purchasing 18, and an advisory committee's roadmap of October 2015, which expected home-made high-performance computers and servers to hold over 80% of the home market by 2025 19.

5. Asked in public, officials did not say (on the record)

Asked what Beijing was doing about Nvidia's chips, the foreign ministry's spokespeople sent reporters elsewhere at least four times from August 2025 to November 2026 20212223. On Nov 9, 2026: “具体情况建议向中方主管部门询问” (for specifics, I suggest you ask the competent Chinese departments) 23.

What guidance did here

It left no rule to cite, and by the reports it was tightened and eased within two weeks. An unnamed person told Reuters on Jan 14, 2026 that officials' wording “is basically a ban for now, though this might change in the future should things evolve” 24. On Jan 28 Reuters reported approvals “with conditions” for three companies 25. So ban is the wrong word for what Beijing did. Nothing is the wrong word too: Nvidia told the US securities regulator on Nov 19, 2025 that the Chinese government “restricted customers from purchasing, importing, or using our data center products” 26.
