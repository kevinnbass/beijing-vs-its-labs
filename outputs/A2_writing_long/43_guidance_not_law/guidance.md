# Guidance, not law

*How Beijing steered China's AI companies away from Nvidia's chips without publishing an order, shown on this one case.*

## Pressure without a rule

From 2024 the security and planning side of the Chinese state pressed China's AI companies to buy home-made chips rather than Nvidia's. It did so through guidance that was never published and, in August and September 2025, through warnings in state media. No published order was found in the 103 policy pages read. Five things in the record show how that works.

**1. The press reports call it guidance** (press reports citing unnamed people)

Bloomberg, Sept 27, 2024: regulators were discouraging purchases of Nvidia's H20 chip through what it called window guidance, "instructions without the force of law" [1]. The Financial Times, Aug 20, 2025: in July the cyberspace regulator sent companies "an informal notice", "instructing them to halt new orders for Nvidia's H20 chips" [2]. One headline says ban: the same paper's "China bans tech companies from buying Nvidia's AI chips", Sept 17, 2025. It describes an order told to companies, by three unnamed people, not a published rule [3].

**2. In public, state media warned, in August and September 2025** (on the record)

Of 42 state-media items on Nvidia chips found from July 2025 to November 2026, the seven that only warn about them all date from Aug 1 to Sept 24, 2025 [4] [5]. One, from an account of the state broadcaster on Aug 10, said of the H20: "作为消费者，我们当然可以选择，不买" (as consumers we can of course choose not to buy) [6] [7]. They warn and urge. None forbids.

**3. Two published texts tell buyers to stop; neither is about Nvidia** (on the record)

On May 21, 2023 the cyberspace regulator's review office published its conclusion on Micron: operators of critical information infrastructure should stop buying its products [8] [9]. On Jun 22, 2026 the finance ministry barred government buyers from the products of 46 named American firms; Nvidia is not on the list [10] [11]. For Nvidia the cyberspace regulator published a summons, on Jul 31, 2025, and no such conclusion that we found [12].

**4. The written rules cover only the state's own buying** (on the record)

Notices published on Dec 26, 2023 require Party and government bodies to write into their purchases of computers and servers that the processor and operating system meet a security evaluation. The same sentence adds: "其他单位可不在采购需求中提出此项要求" (other units may leave this requirement out) [13] [14]. No published national share for home-made chips was found: every percentage found in a published text is a province's or a city's, and the national computing-network opinions of December 2023 set none [15].

**5. Asked in public, officials did not say** (on the record)

Asked what Beijing was doing about Nvidia's chips, the foreign ministry's spokespeople sent reporters elsewhere at least four times from August 2025 to November 2026 [16] [17] [18] [19]. On Nov 9, 2026: "具体情况建议向中方主管部门询问" (for specifics, I suggest you ask the competent Chinese departments) [19].

## What guidance did here

It left no rule to cite, and by the reports it was tightened and eased within two weeks. An unnamed person told Reuters on Jan 14, 2026 that officials' wording "is basically a ban for now, though this might change in the future should things evolve" [20]. On Jan 28 Reuters reported approvals "with conditions" for three companies [21]. So ban is the wrong word for what Beijing did. Nothing is the wrong word too: Nvidia told the US securities regulator on Nov 19, 2025 that the Chinese government "restricted customers from purchasing, importing, or using our data center products" [22].
