## Between two governments

ByteDance, Alibaba, Tencent, Baidu and DeepSeek stood between two governments. Since 2022 Washington has restricted which Nvidia chips may be exported to China [1]. From 2024 the planning side of their own state and the industry ministry, and from 2025 its security side, pressed China's AI companies, through unpublished guidance that the press reported, to use home-made chips instead. Here is what the companies did, in five steps, in their own words where they spoke. Tencent's and Baidu's words come from a stock-data site's transcripts, whose speaker labels are unchecked.

### 1. They used Nvidia chips, and were reported to order more

A Shanghai government body wrote, in a reply dated Apr 30, 2025, that in the city internet companies' own computing clusters run mainly on Nvidia cards [2]. The orders are reported from unnamed people. In the first three months of 2025 Chinese companies including ByteDance, Alibaba and Tencent "have placed at least $16 billion in orders for" Nvidia's H20 chip [3]. Reuters reported on Dec 31, 2025 that Chinese companies "have placed orders for more than 2 million H200 chips for 2026" [4].

### 2. They spoke of the most advanced chips and of home-made ones

Tencent's president, Mar 18, 2026, naming no maker: "you actually want to have access to the most state-of-the-art, you know, training chips to the extent possible" [5]. A Baidu executive vice president, of its AI cloud group, May 18, 2026: "Domestic chips are still catching up with the most advanced global products in certain frontier training scenarios. Inference is an area where domestic chips can be highly relevant and competitive." [6] Another model maker, iFlytek, told investors on Aug 21, 2026, in an answer on how far home-made chips lag behind Nvidia's, what the others used: "其他模型厂商普遍使用性能领先两代的英伟达芯片" (other model makers commonly use Nvidia chips two generations ahead in performance) [7]. iFlytek itself is on the US Entity List, a list of foreign parties that are, in the Commerce Department's words, "subject to specific license requirements for the export, reexport, and transfer (in-country) of specified items" [8]; iFlytek's record says: "尤其是在讯飞被列入美国实体清单的背景下，更需要在全栈自主可控的国产芯片及配套硬件元器件供应上做好保障" (especially as iFlytek has been put on the US Entity List, it is all the more necessary to secure the supply of home-made chips and matching hardware components, independently controllable across the whole stack) [7].

### 3. They were reported to push back, and an unnamed company asked in public

The Financial Times, Aug 20, 2025: "tech giants from Alibaba to ByteDance argued that their AI development would be impaired without Nvidia's chips", though by some close to them "they have become more accepting of a switch" [9]. Nori Chiou of White Oak Capital Partners, quoted by Reuters in December 2025, said cloud companies were "lobbying the government to relax restrictions on a conditional basis" [10]. On the record the companies said less. Tencent's president, Aug 13, 2025: "we don't really have a definite answer on the import situation yet" [11]. On its call of May 13, 2026 a speaker put a shortage of chips down partly to "policy restrictions on certain, you know, foreign design GPUs being brought into China" [12]. The stock-data site labels the speaker as the chief strategy officer; a Chinese transcript, which says it was made by machine translation, puts the answer under the chief financial officer [13], so who spoke is not settled. The answer did not say whose policy. One company, which the message board does not name, asked in public. Its message of Jul 30, 2026 on the commerce ministry's message board asked how to import H200 chips lawfully; the reply of Aug 19 speaks only of exporters and export controls [14].

### 4. They reportedly trained abroad, and one lab was reported to go back to Nvidia for training

The Financial Times, November 2025, by two people it cites: Alibaba and ByteDance "are among the tech groups training their latest large language models (LLMs) in data centres across Southeast Asia", leasing that is, by the paper's account, "compliant with US export controls" [15]. The same paper, Aug 13, 2025, by three unnamed people: DeepSeek "was encouraged by authorities to adopt Huawei's Ascend processor rather than use Nvidia's systems", met "persistent technical issues", and was prompted to use "Nvidia chips for training and Huawei's for inference", while it "was still working with Huawei to make the model compatible with Ascend for inference" [16].

### 5. Baidu and Alibaba built chips of their own; Tencent and DeepSeek spoke of home-made ones

Baidu's chief executive said in April 2025, in Reuters' words, that it had switched on a cluster "comprising 30,000 of its self-developed, third generation P800 Kunlun chips" [17]. Alibaba's chief executive, May 13, 2026, gave a commercial reason for its own chips: "gross margins on mainstream foreign AI chips are very high, basically in the 60% to 80% range", and "the high margins on advanced foreign chips leave domestic chips with enormous headroom for cost-performance gains" [18]. Tencent's chief strategy officer, Nov 18, 2026: "that inference can be run very effectively on a surprisingly limited number of domestic chips" [19]. Of DeepSeek's later model, V4, Reuters wrote in April 2026: "Huawei said its chips were used in some of the V4's training process" [20]. Huawei Cloud's page of that day speaks of adapting and serving the model, and a post of that day signed Ascend CANN on the AtomGit open-source site, of a training reference implementation offered so that users can fine-tune quickly [21] [22]; neither says that Huawei's chips trained V4. DeepSeek's own report on V4 names no chip for its training, and says one of its schemes was validated "on both NVIDIA GPUs and HUAWEI Ascend NPUs platforms" [23]. A rival, iFlytek, told investors days later that V4's training was still done on Nvidia cards, a competitor's claim that nobody has checked [24].

## What the five steps add up to

The companies were neither simply held back by their state nor simply in step with it. The Financial Times put it in its own words in November 2025: training "requires a huge amount of computing power to process massive data sets, leading most Chinese groups to prefer Nvidia's advanced products for the task", while for inference, the everyday work of running a trained model, these companies were "increasingly turning to locally made Chinese chips" [15]. Two of the companies, Baidu and Alibaba, also build chips of their own (step 5). The pull to Nvidia's chips for training and the turn to home-made chips for inference are in the record at once.
