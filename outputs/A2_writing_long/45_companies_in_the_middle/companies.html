Between two governments

ByteDance, Alibaba, Tencent, Baidu and DeepSeek stood between two governments. Since 2022 Washington has restricted which Nvidia chips may be exported to China 1. From 2024 the planning side of their own state and the industry ministry, and from 2025 its security side, pressed China's AI companies, through unpublished guidance that the press reported, to use home-made chips instead. Here is what the companies did, in five steps, in their own words where they spoke. Tencent's and Baidu's words come from a stock-data site's transcripts, whose speaker labels are unchecked.

1. They ordered

A Shanghai government body wrote, in a reply dated Apr 30, 2025, that in the city internet companies' own computing clusters run mainly on Nvidia cards 2. The orders are reported from unnamed people. In the first three months of 2025 Chinese companies including ByteDance, Alibaba and Tencent “have placed at least $16 billion in orders for” Nvidia's H20 chip 3. By Dec 31, 2025 Chinese companies “have placed orders for more than 2 million H200 chips for 2026” 4.

2. They said why

Tencent's president, Mar 18, 2026, naming no maker: “you actually want to have access to the most state-of-the-art, you know, training chips to the extent possible” 5. A Baidu executive vice president, of its AI cloud group, May 18, 2026: “Domestic chips are still catching up with the most advanced global products in certain frontier training scenarios. Inference is an area where domestic chips can be highly relevant and competitive.” 6 Another model maker, iFlytek, told investors on Aug 21, 2026 what the others used: “其他模型厂商普遍使用性能领先两代的英伟达芯片” (other model makers commonly use Nvidia chips two generations ahead in performance) 7. iFlytek itself is on the US Entity List, and the same record says that this makes a secure supply of home-made chips all the more necessary for it 7.

3. They pushed back, and asked

The Financial Times, Aug 20, 2025: “tech giants from Alibaba to ByteDance argued that their AI development would be impaired without Nvidia's chips”, though by some close to them “they have become more accepting of a switch” 8. Nori Chiou of White Oak Capital Partners, quoted by Reuters in December 2025, said cloud companies were “lobbying the government to relax restrictions on a conditional basis” 9. On the record the companies said less. Tencent's president, Aug 13, 2025: “we don't really have a definite answer on the import situation yet” 10. On its call of May 13, 2026 a speaker put a shortage of chips down partly to “policy restrictions on certain, you know, foreign design GPUs being brought into China” 11. The stock-data site labels the speaker as the chief strategy officer; a Chinese transcript, which says it was made by machine translation, puts the answer under the chief financial officer 12, so who spoke is not settled. The answer did not say whose policy. One company, which the message board does not name, asked in public. Its message of Jul 30, 2026 on the commerce ministry's message board asked how to import H200 chips lawfully; the reply of Aug 19 speaks only of exporters and export controls 13.

4. They reportedly trained abroad, and one lab was reported to go back to Nvidia for training

The Financial Times, November 2025, by two people it cites: Alibaba and ByteDance “are among the tech groups training their latest large language models (LLMs) in data centres across Southeast Asia”, leasing that is, by the paper's account, “compliant with US export controls” 14. The same paper, Aug 13, 2025, by three unnamed people: DeepSeek “was encouraged by authorities to adopt Huawei's Ascend processor rather than use Nvidia's systems”, met “persistent technical issues”, and was prompted to use “Nvidia chips for training and Huawei's for inference”, while it “was still working with Huawei to make the model compatible with Ascend for inference” 15.

5. They built chips of their own and turned to other home-made chips

Baidu's chief executive, April 2025: a cluster “comprising 30,000 of its self-developed, third generation P800 Kunlun chips” 16. Alibaba's chief executive, May 13, 2026, gave a commercial reason for its own chips: “gross margins on mainstream foreign AI chips are very high, basically in the 60% to 80% range” 17. Tencent's chief strategy officer, Nov 18, 2026: “that inference can be run very effectively on a surprisingly limited number of domestic chips” 18. Of DeepSeek's later model, V4, Reuters wrote in April 2026: “Huawei said its chips were used in some of the V4's training process” 19. Huawei Cloud's page of that day speaks of adapting and serving the model, and a post of that day signed Ascend CANN on the AtomGit open-source site, of a training reference implementation offered so that users can fine-tune quickly 2021; neither says that Huawei's chips trained V4. DeepSeek's own report on V4 names no chip for its training, and says one of its schemes was validated “on both NVIDIA GPUs and HUAWEI Ascend NPUs platforms” 22. A rival, iFlytek, told investors days later that V4's training was still done on Nvidia cards, a competitor's claim that nobody has checked 23.

What the five steps add up to

The companies were neither simply held back by their state nor simply in step with it. The Financial Times put it in its own words in November 2025: training “requires a huge amount of computing power to process massive data sets, leading most Chinese groups to prefer Nvidia's advanced products for the task”, while for inference, the everyday work of running a trained model, these companies were “increasingly turning to locally made Chinese chips” 14. Some of those chips are their own (step 5). Both are in the record at once.
