China's chip policy pulled against its own AI companies

From 2024 to 2026 the Chinese state pressed its AI companies to move off Nvidia's chips, and, by the press reports, the companies kept ordering them. That holds on direction, with five limits.

The finding

From 2024 to 2026 the Chinese state pressed China's AI companies to move away from Nvidia's chips, and the companies kept reaching for them. That answers a question raised by a clip posted on Nov 19, 2026, in which Palmer Luckey said: “China doesn't want NVIDIA to sell GPUs to Chinese companies.” 1 And next: “there are Chinese companies that want to buy Nvidia GPUs” 2. The first sentence is from a machine transcript of the clip (0:11 to 0:16), the second from the text of the post that carries it. Nobody has checked either by ear. He is right on direction. Five limits follow at the end.

The declared line: use home-made chips

Xi Jinping said in 2016 that having core technology controlled by others is China's greatest hidden danger 3. Nine of China's 31 provincial-level governments were found to set a share for home-made computing, each by its plan's own measure: chips, equipment, hardware and software, or computing capacity; one of the nine, Zhejiang's, is for the computing of one sector. Jiangsu marks its own, 70% or more of chips in new computing centres by 2030, as indicative, not binding 4. In May 2026 the state planner (NDRC) said it was guiding home-made large models to do more to adapt to home-made chips 5.

What the companies used, and the orders reported

A Shanghai government body wrote, in a reply dated Apr 30, 2025 (published Nov 10, 2025), that in the city the internet companies' own computing clusters run mainly on Nvidia cards 6. The big companies' orders are reported from unnamed people: in the first three months of 2025 Chinese companies including ByteDance, Alibaba and Tencent “have placed at least $16 billion in orders for” Nvidia's H20 chip (The Information, through Reuters) 7.

What Beijing did

On the record

The cyberspace regulator summoned Nvidia on Jul 31, 2025 over backdoor risks in the H20 8. Nvidia told the US securities regulator on Nov 19, 2025 that the Chinese government “has encouraged customers to purchase from our China-based competitors and restricted customers from purchasing, importing, or using our data center products” 9. Alibaba told it: “In September 2025, the Cyberspace Administration of China requested Chinese technology companies to halt purchases of certain chips.” 10 That sentence does not name Nvidia.

Reported, from unnamed people

The Financial Times, Aug 20, 2025: in July the cyberspace regulator sent companies “an informal notice”, “instructing them to halt new orders for Nvidia's H20 chips” 11. Reuters, Jan 14, 2026: customs agents were told that H200 chips “are not permitted to enter China” 12.

The numbers

For the H200, which Reuters called Nvidia's second most powerful AI chip in May 2026, the counts are not one series 13. Washington's and Nvidia's are on the record; the rest come from unnamed people.

  • Reported: ordered by Chinese companies for 2026 (Reuters, Dec 31, 2025): “more than 2 million H200 chips” 14.
  • Applied for in Washington by May 11, 2026: 3,444,160 chips, Nvidia's H200 and AMD's MI325X together, in the table of licence applications from the Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) that the House Foreign Affairs Committee made public on Nov 19, 2026. An application is not an order 15161718.
  • Of those, licensed by Washington and not yet shipped: 879,868 (Nvidia's: 579,868). In applications returned without action: 1,888,950, or 55% of the chips BIS's summary records; no reason is given. The sums are ours 1516.
  • Reported: approved by Beijing, with conditions (Reuters, Jan 28, 2026): “more than 400,000 H200 chips in total” for ByteDance, Alibaba and Tencent 19.
  • Reported: under discussion in July 2026 (The Information, through Reuters, Jul 8, 2026): “Beijing is still determining the exact number of Nvidia chips to approve, and it could amount to fewer than 200,000 in total” 20.
  • Reported: delivered by August 2026 (Financial Times, through Reuters, Aug 18, 2026): “about 10,000 H200 processors” each for ByteDance and Tencent 21.
  • Reported: stored in Hong Kong, November 2026 (Reuters, Nov 5, 2026): about 600,000 22.
  • Nvidia, Nov 17, 2026, on the quarter to Oct 25: “Q3 sales of licensed Hopper Data Center products to China was $2.4 billion” (the Hopper family includes the H200). It said it had stopped shipping them and gave no reason 2324.

Five limits

1Beijing was not one actor. Vice Premier He Lifeng told Nvidia's chief executive in April 2025 that Nvidia was welcome 25. The Financial Times, Nov 13, 2026: the commerce ministry “favoured imports”, while security officials “viewed dependence on Nvidia as a strategic vulnerability” 26.

2Public warnings and unpublished guidance, not a published ban. No published order against buying Nvidia chips was found in the 82 policy pages read. Of 75 state-media items on Nvidia chips found from July 2025 to November 2026, 16 of the 19 that only warn about them date from Jul 31 to Sept 24, 2025 272829; the three found later are in Global Times in 2026, and the items after September 2025 are what two rounds of searches found, a sample and not a full count.

3Some purchases were approved, and some chips came in. Washington's approvals, on the record, and Beijing's, as reported, are listed above, and so are the reported deliveries and Nvidia's licensed sales to China; none of these says how many chips crossed into the mainland.

4The companies had reasons of their own, and American curbs and conditions pushed too. Alibaba says its chip unit “has brought proprietary GPUs into scaled production” 30. Nvidia's reports since November 2025 say: “we are already effectively foreclosed from the China market by U.S. export controls” 9.

5What Beijing meant by it is not settled. The US-China commission, November 2025, read an antitrust move against Nvidia, made as the regulator dropped a probe into Google, as “signaling China's willingness to both exert leverage and ease pressure as a negotiating tactic” 31. The US Trade Representative, asked whether China would let its companies buy the H200, said it was “gonna be a sovereign decision for China” (captions, not checked by ear, at 5:59) 32. The direction stands under either reading of what Beijing meant by it.

What survives

From 2024 the planning side of the Chinese state and the industry ministry, and from 2025 its security side, pressed China's AI companies, through unpublished guidance that the press reported, to buy home-made chips rather than Nvidia's. The companies kept ordering Nvidia chips, in the millions by the press reports, and by those reports had received a small part by late 2026. They were also turning to home-made chips, for their own reasons and under American curbs. Whether Beijing meant lasting policy or leverage is read both ways.
