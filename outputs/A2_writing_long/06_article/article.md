## The finding

From 2024 to 2026 the Chinese state pressed China's AI companies to move away from Nvidia's chips, and the companies kept reaching for them. That answers a question raised by a clip posted on Nov 19, 2026, in which Palmer Luckey said: "China doesn't want NVIDIA to sell GPUs to Chinese companies." [1] And next: "there are Chinese companies that want to buy Nvidia GPUs" [2]. The first sentence is from a machine transcript of the clip (0:11 to 0:16), the second from the text of the post that carries it. Nobody has checked either by ear. He is right on direction. Five limits follow at the end.

## The declared line: use home-made chips

Xi Jinping said in 2016 that having core technology controlled by others is China's greatest hidden danger [3]. Nine of China's 31 provincial-level governments were found to set a share for home-made computing, each by its plan's own measure: chips, equipment, hardware and software, or computing capacity; one of the nine, Zhejiang's, is for the computing of one sector. Jiangsu marks its own, 70% or more of chips in new computing centres by 2030, as indicative, not binding [4]. In May 2026 the state planner (NDRC) said it was guiding home-made large models to do more to adapt to home-made chips [5].

## What the companies used, and the orders reported

A Shanghai government body wrote, in a reply dated Apr 30, 2025 (published Nov 10, 2025), that in the city the internet companies' own computing clusters run mainly on Nvidia cards while the three big telecom carriers mainly buy home-made clusters [6]. Baidu's developer site says its cloud business expanded a cluster of home-made Kunlun P800 chips to 32,000 cards in April 2025 [7]. The big companies' orders are reported from unnamed people: in the first three months of 2025 Chinese companies including ByteDance, Alibaba and Tencent "have placed at least $16 billion in orders for" Nvidia's H20 chip (The Information, through Reuters) [8].

## What Beijing did

### On the record

The cyberspace regulator summoned Nvidia on Jul 31, 2025 over backdoor risks in the H20 [9]. Nvidia told the US securities regulator on Nov 19, 2025 that the Chinese government "has encouraged customers to purchase from our China-based competitors and restricted customers from purchasing, importing, or using our data center products" [10]. Alibaba told it: "In September 2025, the Cyberspace Administration of China requested Chinese technology companies to halt purchases of certain chips." [11] That sentence does not name Nvidia.

### Reported, from unnamed people

The Financial Times, Aug 20, 2025: in July the cyberspace regulator sent companies "an informal notice", "instructing them to halt new orders for Nvidia's H20 chips" [12]. Reuters, Jan 14, 2026: customs agents were told that H200 chips "are not permitted to enter China" [13].

## Five limits

**1.** **Beijing was not one actor.** Vice Premier He Lifeng told Nvidia's chief executive in April 2025 that Nvidia was welcome [26]. In Zhuhai, a project backed by city bodies offered computing on both home-made and Nvidia chips [27]. The Financial Times, Nov 13, 2026: the commerce ministry "favoured imports", while security officials "viewed dependence on Nvidia as a strategic vulnerability" [28].

**2.** **Public warnings and unpublished guidance, not a published ban.** No published order against buying Nvidia chips was found in the 82 policy pages read. Published local plans and the state planner's guidance still push toward home-made chips. Of 297 state and Party media items on Nvidia and its chips found from July 2025 to November 2026, 34 only warn about the chips, and 23 of those date from Jul 31 to Sept 24, 2025 [29] [30] [31]. Another 6 warnings date from Dec 9, 2025 to Jan 14, 2026, in coverage of the US easing for the H200 [32]. Searches and a scan of saved pages found the items after Sept 24, 2025. That later list is a sample of what these outlets printed.

**3.** **Some purchases were reported approved, and Nvidia reported licensed sales.** Reuters reported Beijing's approvals from people it did not name [20]. On Jan 29, 2026, Nvidia's chief executive said he thought the Chinese government was still deciding and Nvidia had not yet received purchase orders, as Voice of America's Chinese service renders him [33]. Nvidia reported licensed sales to China on its November results call [24]. Neither the reported approvals nor Nvidia's reported licensed sales say how many chips crossed into the mainland.

**4.** **The companies had reasons of their own, and American curbs and conditions pushed too.** Alibaba says its chip unit "has brought proprietary GPUs into scaled production" [34]. Nvidia's reports since November 2025 say: "we are already effectively foreclosed from the China market by U.S. export controls" [10].

**5.** **What Beijing meant by it is not settled.** The US-China commission, November 2025, read an antitrust move against Nvidia, made as the regulator dropped a probe into Google, as "signaling China's willingness to both exert leverage and ease pressure as a negotiating tactic" [35]. The US Trade Representative, asked whether China would let its companies buy the H200, said it was "gonna be a sovereign decision for China" (captions, not checked by ear, at 5:59) [36]. The direction stands under either reading of what Beijing meant by it.

### What survives

From 2024 the planning side of the Chinese state and the industry ministry, and from 2025 its security side, pressed China's AI companies, through unpublished guidance that the press reported, to buy home-made chips rather than Nvidia's. Reuters, citing people it did not name, reported orders for more than 2 million H200 chips for 2026 [15]. It also relayed the Financial Times report, from unnamed people, of deliveries of about 10,000 H200 chips each to two buyers [22]. The companies also bought and used home-made chips, for reasons of their own and under American curbs. Whether Beijing meant lasting policy or leverage is read both ways.
