# China's chip policy pulled against its own AI companies

*From 2024 to 2026 the Chinese state pressed its AI companies to move off Nvidia's chips, and the companies kept ordering them. That holds on direction, with five limits.*

## The finding

For two years the Chinese state pulled China's AI companies away from Nvidia's chips, and the companies kept reaching for them. That answers a question raised by a clip posted on Nov 19, 2026, in which Palmer Luckey said: "China doesn't want NVIDIA to sell GPUs to Chinese companies." [1] And next: "there are Chinese companies that want to buy Nvidia GPUs" [2]. The first sentence is from a machine transcript of the clip (0:11 to 0:16), the second from the text of the post that carries it. Nobody has checked either by ear. He is right on direction. Five limits follow at the end.

## The declared line: use home-made chips

Xi Jinping said in 2016 that having core technology controlled by others is China's greatest hidden danger [3]. Eight of China's 31 provincial-level governments were found to set a share for home-made computing hardware; Jiangsu marks its own, 70% or more of chips in new computing centres by 2030, as indicative, not binding [4]. In May 2026 the state planner (NDRC) said it was guiding home-made large models to do more to adapt to home-made chips [5].

## What the companies bought

A Shanghai government body wrote, in a reply dated Apr 30, 2025 (published Nov 10, 2025), that in the city the internet companies' own computing clusters run mainly on Nvidia cards [6]. The big companies' orders are reported from unnamed people: in the first three months of 2025 Chinese companies including ByteDance, Alibaba and Tencent "have placed at least $16 billion in orders for" Nvidia's H20 chip (The Information, through Reuters) [7].

## What Beijing did

### On the record

The cyberspace regulator summoned Nvidia on Jul 31, 2025 over backdoor risks in the H20 [8]. Nvidia told the US securities regulator on Nov 19, 2025 that the Chinese government "has encouraged customers to purchase from our China-based competitors and restricted customers from purchasing, importing, or using our data center products" [9]. Alibaba told it: "In September 2025, the Cyberspace Administration of China requested Chinese technology companies to halt purchases of certain chips." [10] That sentence does not name Nvidia.

### Reported, from unnamed people

The Financial Times, Aug 20, 2025: in July the cyberspace regulator sent companies "an informal notice", "instructing them to halt new orders for Nvidia's H20 chips" [11]. Reuters, Jan 14, 2026: customs agents were told that H200 chips "are not permitted to enter China" [12].

## The numbers

For the H200, Nvidia's second most powerful AI chip, the counts are not one series [13]. Washington's and Nvidia's are on the record; the rest come from unnamed people.

- Ordered for 2026: "more than 2 million H200 chips" (Reuters, Dec 31, 2025) [14].
- Applied for in Washington by May 11, 2026: 3,444,160 chips, Nvidia's H200 and AMD's MI325X together, in a Commerce Department table the House Foreign Affairs Committee made public on Nov 19, 2026. An application is not an order [15] [16] [17].
- Of those, licensed by Washington and not yet shipped: 879,868 (Nvidia's: 579,868). Returned without action: 1,888,950, or 55% of the chips in the applications the summary records; no reason is given. The sums are ours [15] [16].
- Approved by Beijing, with conditions: "more than 400,000 H200 chips in total" (Reuters, Jan 28, 2026) [18].
- Delivered by August 2026: "about 10,000 H200 processors" each for ByteDance and Tencent (Financial Times, through Reuters, Aug 18, 2026) [19].
- Waiting in Hong Kong: about 600,000 (Reuters, Nov 5, 2026) [20].
- Nvidia, Nov 17, 2026, on the quarter to Oct 25: "Q3 sales of licensed Hopper Data Center products to China was $2.4 billion" (the Hopper family includes the H200). It said it had stopped shipping them and gave no reason [21] [22].

## Five limits

**1.** **Beijing was not one actor.** Vice Premier He Lifeng told Nvidia's chief executive in April 2025 that Nvidia was welcome [23]. The Financial Times, Nov 13, 2026: the commerce ministry "favoured imports", while security officials "viewed dependence on Nvidia as a strategic vulnerability" [24].

**2.** **Public warnings and unpublished guidance, not a published ban.** No published order against buying Nvidia chips was found in the 103 policy pages read. Of 42 state-media items on Nvidia chips found, the seven that only warn about them all date from Aug 1 to Sept 24, 2025 [25] [26].

**3.** **Some chips came in.** See the approvals, deliveries and sales listed above.

**4.** **The companies had reasons of their own, and American curbs and conditions pushed too.** Alibaba says its chip unit "has brought proprietary GPUs into scaled production" [27]. Nvidia's reports since November 2025 say: "we are already effectively foreclosed from the China market by U.S. export controls" [9].

**5.** **What Beijing meant by it is not settled.** The US-China commission, November 2025, read an antitrust move against Nvidia, made as the regulator dropped a probe into Google, as "signaling China's willingness to both exert leverage and ease pressure as a negotiating tactic" [28]. The US Trade Representative called the choice to buy "a sovereign decision for China" (captions, not checked by ear, at 5:59) [29]. The direction stands under either reading.

### What survives

From 2024 the security and planning side of the Chinese state pressed China's AI companies, through unpublished guidance that the press reported, to buy home-made chips rather than Nvidia's. The companies kept ordering Nvidia chips in the millions and, by the reports, had received a small part by late 2026. They were also turning to home-made chips, for their own reasons and under American curbs. Whether Beijing meant lasting policy or leverage is read both ways.
