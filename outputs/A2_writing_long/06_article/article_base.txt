## The finding

From 2024 to 2026 the Chinese state pressed China's AI companies to move away from Nvidia's chips, and the companies kept reaching for them.
That answers a question raised by a clip posted on Nov 19, 2026, in which Palmer Luckey said: "China doesn't want NVIDIA to sell GPUs to Chinese companies." [clip-luckey]
And next: "there are Chinese companies that want to buy Nvidia GPUs" [cnpol-x-overton-2123392702821580811].
The first sentence is from a machine transcript of the clip (0:11 to 0:16), the second from the text of the post that carries it. Nobody has checked either by ear.
He is right on direction. Five limits follow at the end.

## Five limits

1. **Beijing was not one actor.** Vice Premier He Lifeng told Nvidia's chief executive in April 2025 that Nvidia was welcome [cn-policy-he-welcomes]. The Financial Times, Nov 13, 2026: the commerce ministry "favoured imports", while security officials "viewed dependence on Nvidia as a strategic vulnerability" [man-e2596eab15f4].
!! cn-policy-he-welcomes | 欢迎包括英伟达公司在内的更多美资企业深耕中国市场
!! cn-policy-he-welcomes | 新华社北京4月17日电
!! man-e2596eab15f4 | NOV 13 2026
2. **Public warnings and unpublished guidance, not a published ban.** No published order against buying Nvidia chips was found in the 82 policy pages read. Of 75 state-media items on Nvidia chips found from July 2025 to November 2026, 16 of the 19 that only warn about them date from Jul 31 to Sept 24, 2025 [cn-ga02-gt-cac-summons-news-202507] [cn-acts-peoplesdaily-bjd-20250801] [cn-media-stdaily-security-forum]; the three found later are in Global Times in 2026, and the items after September 2025 are what two rounds of searches found, a sample and not a full count.
!! cn-acts-peoplesdaily-bjd-20250801 | 2025-08-01
!! cn-acts-peoplesdaily-bjd-20250801 | 绝不能让“带病”的芯片上岗
!! cn-media-stdaily-security-forum | 2025-09-24
3. **Some purchases were approved, and some chips came in.** Washington's approvals, on the record, and Beijing's, as reported, are listed above, and so are the reported deliveries and Nvidia's licensed sales to China; none of these says how many chips crossed into the mainland.
4. **The companies had reasons of their own, and American curbs and conditions pushed too.** Alibaba says its chip unit "has brought proprietary GPUs into scaled production" [cn-policy-alibaba-own-gpu]. Nvidia's reports since November 2025 say: "we are already effectively foreclosed from the China market by U.S. export controls" [man-ms-sec-10q-2025-10].

!! man-ms-sec-10q-2026-07 | already effectively foreclosed from the China market by U.S. export controls
5. **What Beijing meant by it is not settled.** The US-China commission, November 2025, read an antitrust move against Nvidia, made as the regulator dropped a probe into Google, as "signaling China's willingness to both exert leverage and ease pressure as a negotiating tactic" [cn-le03-uscc-2025-full]. The US Trade Representative, asked whether China would let its companies buy the H200, said it was "gonna be a sovereign decision for China" (captions, not checked by ear, at 5:59) [cn-le01-greer-bbg-20260515-subs-ttml]. The direction stands under either reading of what Beijing meant by it.

**What survives**

From 2024 the planning side of the Chinese state and the industry ministry, and from 2025 its security side, pressed China's AI companies, through unpublished guidance that the press reported, to buy home-made chips rather than Nvidia's. The companies kept ordering Nvidia chips, in the millions by the press reports, and by those reports had received a small part by late 2026. They were also turning to home-made chips, for their own reasons and under American curbs. Whether Beijing meant lasting policy or leverage is read both ways.
