## Two permissions

Reuters called the H200 Nvidia's second most powerful AI chip in May 2026 [1]. To reach a Chinese company it needed an export licence from Washington and, by the press reports, Beijing's leave to import; no published Chinese permit rule for these chips was found. Each step is marked on the record or reported (a press report citing unnamed people).

## Washington's steps

- **Dec 8, 2025. On the record.** The President posted that he had told Xi Jinping "the United States will allow NVIDIA to ship its H200 products to approved customers in China", and added: "President Xi responded positively!" [2] That is the President's account, not Xi's words.
- **Jan 15, 2026. On the record.** The Commerce Department's rule took effect. It moved licence applications for the H200 to China and Macau from a presumption of denial to case-by-case review, on conditions that include "independent, third-party testing in the United States" and shipments to China and Macau of at most half of the same product shipped for end use in the United States [3].
- **February 2026. On the record.** Nvidia's filing: from that month the US government "granted licenses that would allow us to ship small amounts of H200 products to specific China-based customers", with "a 25% tariff upon importation into the United States" [4].
- **May 11, 2026. On the record.** The table of licence applications for the H200 and AMD's comparable chip, from the Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security, made public by the House Foreign Affairs Committee on Nov 19, 2026: 879,868 chips in the 21 licensed applications (Nvidia's: 579,868 in 17), each application marked "Approved, not shipped"; 1,888,950 in applications returned without action, 55% of the chips in the applications the summary records. No reason is given. The sums are ours [5] [6] [7].

## Beijing's steps

- **Jan 14, 2026. Reported.** Reuters: customs agents were told the chips "are not permitted to enter China". Companies were "explicitly instructed not to purchase the chips unless necessary" [8].
- **Jan 28 and 30, 2026. Reported.** ByteDance, Alibaba and Tencent "have been approved to purchase more than 400,000 H200 chips in total" (Jan 28), and DeepSeek on Jan 30 (Reuters both days; the second report on Yahoo Finance); the conditions, left to the state planner (NDRC), were still being set [9] [10]. Nvidia's chief executive said on Jan 29, at Taipei's airport, as Voice of America's Chinese service renders him: "我认为中国政府仍在决策中，我们尚未收到采购订单，我们也没有收到新闻报道所说的相关信息" (I think the Chinese government is still deciding; we have not yet received purchase orders, and we have not received the information the news reports speak of) [11].
- **Mar 17, 2026. Reported.** Reuters: "Nvidia has won Beijing's approval to sell" the chip, though a Chinese company source "did not know if the Chinese government had given final approval" [12].
- **May 14, 2026. Reported.** Reuters: "not a single delivery has been made so far". By one person, "Chinese firms pulled back after guidance from Beijing", a shift that "was partly triggered by changes on the U.S. side" [1].
- **Aug 18, 2026. Reported.** The Financial Times, through Reuters: "ByteDance and Tencent have each received about 10,000 H200 processors in recent weeks"; the paper's own text, dated Aug 19, adds, by one of its people, that "Beijing wants them to keep most of these chips outside the mainland" [13] [14].
- **Nov 5, 2026. Reported.** Reuters: "About 600,000 of Nvidia's advanced artificial intelligence chips are stored in Hong Kong awaiting shipment to China", "as Beijing withholds approval for large-scale imports despite demand from technology firms" [15].
- **Nov 9, 2026. On the record.** Asked about that report, the foreign ministry's spokesperson said to ask the competent departments [16].

## What was said on the record

- **Apr 22, 2026.** The US Commerce Secretary, to a Senate subcommittee: the "Chinese central government has not let them as of yet buy the chips" [17]. He called it his understanding.
- **May 20, 2026.** Nvidia, on its results call, of the H200 licences Washington had approved: "we have yet to generate any revenue, and we are uncertain whether any imports will be allowed into the country" [18].
- **Aug 26, 2026.** Nvidia's quarterly filing: the licensed sales "were restricted by the PRC government" [4].
- **Nov 17, 2026.** Nvidia, on its call for the quarter to Oct 25: "Q3 sales of licensed Hopper Data Center products to China was $2.4 billion", and "we are no longer shipping licensed Hopper Data Center products to China" [19] [20]. The Hopper family includes the H200. Nvidia gave no reason.

## What the two lists show

Neither yes was whole. Washington's came with testing, a cap and a tariff, and its table shows more chips in returned applications than in licensed ones. Beijing's is known almost wholly from unnamed people: a hold, then a conditional yes; and, as outcomes rather than approvals, the small deliveries the Financial Times reported in August and the 600,000 chips Reuters reported in Hong Kong in November. What Beijing meant by it is not settled. Reuters wrote in January 2026 that it was unclear whether Beijing wanted an outright ban, "is still chewing over restrictions, or whether these measures could be used as a bargaining tactic in talks with Washington" [8]. The US Trade Representative, May 15, 2026: whether Chinese companies may buy is "a sovereign decision for China" (captions, not checked by ear, at 5:59) [21]. Either way, by the reports, the companies had received a small part of the chips they sought: about 10,000 each for ByteDance and Tencent by August 2026, where three companies had been reported approved in January to buy more than 400,000; none of the sources here says how many crossed into the mainland.
