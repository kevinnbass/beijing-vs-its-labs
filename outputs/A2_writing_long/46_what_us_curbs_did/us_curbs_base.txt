## Two capitals

The push on China's AI companies to move off Nvidia came from two capitals.
From 2024 the planning side of the Chinese state and the industry ministry, and from 2025 its security side, pressed them, through unpublished guidance that the press reported, to use home-made chips.
And since 2022 Washington has restricted which Nvidia chips may be exported to China at all [cn-clips-alibaba-20f].
!! cn-clips-alibaba-20f | since 2022, the U.S. government has implemented a series of measures restricting the export to China of advanced computing chips
This piece sets out what the sources say the second did.

**2023: two early turns**

Reuters reported on Nov 7, 2023, by people it did not name, that Baidu had ordered 1,600 Huawei AI chips that August, ahead of new US rules [cn-labs-baidu-ascend-2023-relay1].

!! cn-labs-baidu-ascend-2023-relay1 | two people familiar with the matter said

!! cn-labs-baidu-ascend-2023-relay1 | placed the order in August, ahead of widely anticipated new rules by the US government that in October tightened restrictions
An unnamed person told Reuters why: "They were ordering 910B chips to prepare for a future where they may no longer be able to purchase from Nvidia" [cn-labs-baidu-ascend-2023-relay1].
The same report calls the order "tiny relative to the thousands of chips top Chinese tech firms have historically ordered from Nvidia" [cn-labs-baidu-ascend-2023-relay1].
Eight days later Tencent's president told analysts, of the new curbs: "And we will also try to look for domestic source for these training chips." [cn-policy-tencent-us-curbs-relay1]

**What the seller and the buyers have said on the record**

Nvidia, in its reports since November 2025: "we are already effectively foreclosed from the China market by U.S. export controls" [man-ms-sec-10q-2025-10].

Alibaba's annual report: "We and our technology partners are forced to develop equivalent technologies or components, or obtain equivalent technologies or components from sources outside the United States." [cn-clips-alibaba-20f]
iFlytek, a model maker on the US Entity List (a list of foreign parties that are, in the Commerce Department's words, "subject to specific license requirements for the export, reexport, and transfer (in-country) of specified items" [ms-g17-x2-bis-d38864]), told investors on Aug 21, 2026 that as American controls tighten "全行业都会被倒逼转向国产算力" (the whole industry will be forced to turn to home-made computing), and of itself: "尤其是在讯飞被列入美国实体清单的背景下，更需要在全栈自主可控的国产芯片及配套硬件元器件供应上做好保障" (especially as iFlytek has been put on the US Entity List, it is all the more necessary to secure the supply of home-made chips and matching hardware components, independently controllable across the whole stack) [cn-le26-iflytek-ir-20260821].

**2026: Washington and the H200**

Washington's rule of Jan 15, 2026 moved licence applications for Nvidia's H200 chip to China and Macau from a presumption of denial to case-by-case review, on conditions that include "independent, third-party testing in the United States", and kept a presumption of denial for "exports to entities that are headquartered or have a parent company headquartered in Macau or a destination in Country Group D:5, including end-users located outside of destinations in Country Group D:5 or Macau" [man-ms-admin-bis-jan2026-rule-8ec187]. The director of the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy, Michael Kratsios, told a House subcommittee the day before: "if you're a large Chinese tech company and you want to build a data center in Malaysia, you cannot buy H200s to do that" [cn-usdocs-house-kratsios-20260114].

## What the sources do and do not say

One official American body weighs the two. The US-China commission's 2026 report says of the Chinese state: "U.S. and allied controls did not drive China's indigenization efforts, but they have added urgency in recent years." [cn-usdocs-uscc-2026-ch8]
!! cn-usdocs-uscc-2026-ch8 | For more than two decades, Beijing has been pursuing self-sufficiency
The Congressional Research Service, Sept 19, 2025, read Beijing's pressure over Nvidia's weaker H20 chip as a push for better ones: China "may use its purported security concerns about the H20 to press for technology disclosures and access to more advanced chips, such as Nvidia's B30 chip" [cn-usdocs-crs-r48642-20250919].
!! cn-usdocs-crs-r48642-20250919 | September 19, 2025
Some labs now claim to train on home-made chips. Zhipu said in January 2026 that its image model had completed "its full training cycle on Chinese-made chips"; InfoWorld, which reported the claim, adds that the company "did not say how many processors or how long it took to train its model" [cn-train-infoworld-zhipu-glm].
!! cn-train-infoworld-zhipu-glm | PUBLISHED: January 14, 2026

American curbs gave the companies a reason of their own to line up home-made supply, and Tencent and iFlytek have said so on the record; Alibaba's report speaks of developing equivalent technology or getting it from sources outside the United States. None of the sources cited here says the curbs alone did it; the commission says they added urgency to an effort Beijing already had.
