## Two capitals

The push on China's AI companies to move off Nvidia came from two capitals.
From 2024 the security and planning side of the Chinese state pressed them to use home-made chips.
And since 2022 Washington has restricted which Nvidia chips may be exported to China at all [cn-clips-alibaba-20f].
!! cn-clips-alibaba-20f | since 2022, the U.S. government has implemented a series of measures restricting the export to China of advanced computing chips
This piece sets out what the sources say the second did.

**2023: two early turns**

Reuters reported on Nov 7, 2023, by people it did not name, that Baidu had ordered 1,600 Huawei AI chips that August, ahead of new US rules [cn-labs-baidu-ascend-2023-relay1].
!! cn-labs-baidu-ascend-2023-relay1 | PUBLISHED: 2023-11-07
!! cn-labs-baidu-ascend-2023-relay1 | two people familiar with the matter said
!! cn-labs-baidu-ascend-2023-relay1 | Baidu ordered 1,600 of Huawei Technologies' 910B Ascend AI chips
!! cn-labs-baidu-ascend-2023-relay1 | placed the order in August, ahead of widely anticipated new rules by the US government that in October tightened restrictions
An unnamed person told Reuters why: "They were ordering 910B chips to prepare for a future where they may no longer be able to purchase from Nvidia" [cn-labs-baidu-ascend-2023-relay1].
The same report calls the order "tiny relative to the thousands of chips top Chinese tech firms have historically ordered from Nvidia" [cn-labs-baidu-ascend-2023-relay1].
Eight days later Tencent's president told analysts, of the new curbs: "And we will also try to look for domestic source for these training chips." [cn-policy-tencent-us-curbs-relay1]

**2026: Washington and the H200**

Washington licensed Nvidia's H200 chip on condition of "independent, third-party testing in the United States", which brings "a 25% tariff upon importation into the United States" [man-ms-admin-bis-jan2026-rule-8ec187] [man-ms-sec-10q-2026-07].
!! man-ms-admin-bis-jan2026-rule-8ec187 | The effective date of this rule is January 15, 2026
!! man-ms-sec-10q-2026-07 | As a result, any H200s shipped under the new licensing program are subject to a 25% tariff
!! man-ms-sec-10q-2026-07 | The licenses require that the H200s go through an inspection process in the United States prior to any shipment to the customer
Washington's own table shows that by May 11, 2026 it had returned without action 55% of the chips in the licence applications its summary records. It gives no reason, and the House committee that released it says the summary does not show every application in full [cn-usdocs-hfac-decisions-20261119] [cn-usdocs-hfac-summary-20261119].

## What the sources do and do not say

One official American body weighs the two. The US-China commission's 2026 report says of the Chinese state: "U.S. and allied controls did not drive China's indigenization efforts, but they have added urgency in recent years." [cn-usdocs-uscc-2026-ch8]
!! cn-usdocs-uscc-2026-ch8 | For more than two decades, Beijing has been pursuing self-sufficiency
The Congressional Research Service, Sept 19, 2025, read Beijing's pressure over Nvidia's weaker H20 chip as a push for better ones: China "may use its purported security concerns about the H20 to press for technology disclosures and access to more advanced chips, such as Nvidia's B30 chip" [cn-usdocs-crs-r48642-20250919].
!! cn-usdocs-crs-r48642-20250919 | September 19, 2025
Under the curbs some labs now claim to train on home-made chips. Zhipu said in January 2026 that its image model had completed "its full training cycle on Chinese-made chips"; it "did not say how many processors or how long it took to train its model" [cn-train-infoworld-zhipu-glm].
!! cn-train-infoworld-zhipu-glm | PUBLISHED: January 14, 2026

American curbs gave the companies a reason of their own to line up home-made supply, and Alibaba, Tencent and iFlytek have said so on the record. None of the sources cited here says the curbs alone did it; the commission says they added urgency to an effort Beijing already had.
