## How to use this page

This page is for a reader who wants to check our English against the Chinese. Each row gives one passage: who issued it and when, the page's own words, and our English. The number after the source leads to the reference list, which gives the page's address and the name of the saved copy.

The 61 passages are a selection from the argument (ARGUMENT_referenced.md), from what it quotes of Chinese state bodies, the Party, state media and a few companies; they are not all of it. By a script's count they hold 74 of the argument's 254 Chinese quotations, some at greater length; by the kind of the source that holds each, the 180 left out are 35 from company filings, 41 from press reports (of words on the record or from unnamed people), 25 from purchase notices, 78 from state texts, state media and other public bodies, 71 of them from texts that this page does not show, and 1 from a machine-made transcript. The English is the argument's own wording wherever it gives one. A second reader went through the argument's Chinese quotations on Nov 23, 2026 and found faults in the English beside 34 of the 50 it then had; the argument was corrected, and this page takes the corrected wording. The quotations the argument gained on Nov 24, 2026 were not part of that review: passages 19 to 23, 35, 50 to 52, 57 and 59 to 61, the second quotation of passage 29 and the end of passage 32. Their English is the argument's own, or ours where it gives none (passages 51 and 52). On Nov 25, 2026 another second reader read all 61 passages of this page, those among them, and marked the English beside 14 as changing the sense and beside 22 as off by a shade. We read each finding against the Chinese and changed the English of 14 passages, here and in the argument. Neither second reader is a native reader of Chinese, and no native reader has checked this page.

Most passages are texts that a Chinese state body or the Party published, or an official's words on an official site. Some are not: state-media commentaries and an industry body's statement (section 3), company filings and two company chiefs' words as newspapers reported them (section 4), a company's question on a ministry's message board (passage 42), a scholar's advice printed by a Party paper (passage 48), a vice minister's words as the China Information Industry Network (中国信息产业网) reported them, on the site of a local office of his ministry (passage 23), and a state-owned group's news item (passage 51). Each row says which. None is a press report from unnamed people: Beijing's instructions to companies are reported that way and are not on this page.

A passage that is found shows what the page says. It does not show that the words were carried out.

## 2. Rules and targets on paper

These carry points 2 and 3. The purchase rule binds Party and government bodies at township level and above, and the public institutions attached to them, when they buy the computers it names. Passage 6 stands in the same sentence as passage 5: it lets every other buyer leave the rule out. Passages 10 to 17 are eight of the nine provincial-level governments found to set a share for home-made computing hardware; two are worded as aims (力争, strive to) and one is marked indicative. The ninth was found on Nov 25, 2026 and has no row of its own: Zhejiang's plan for its information and communications industry, posted on Jul 29, 2026, sets a target for that one sector, "到2030年，浙江信息通信行业算力规模达150EFLOPS，国产化算力占比超60%" (by 2030 the computing of Zhejiang's information and communications industry reaches 150 EFLOPS, with home-made computing over 60% of it) [cn-ga31-cndocs-52]. Passage 18 is Beijing city's plan, which sets an ability to build, not a share. Passage 19, Sichuan's, is one of the 4 local voucher rules that pay for home-made computing only, among the 91 counted in the project's voucher tables (the rules found by searches that went province by province and city by city, saved and read, not a census of every local government); the other three are Ningbo's, Huangyan's and the Changsha Economic and Technological Development Zone's. Passages 20 to 23 are national: the state planner backs home-made computing and vouchers; the national computing-network opinions set a share for green power, not for home-made chips; and the 2015 roadmap's forecast is advice, as the vice minister said at its launch. No published national plan found sets a home-made share for computing centres. The “two majors” of passage 20 are, in the words of a reply of Shenyang's science and technology bureau, "“两重”建设（国家重大战略实施和重点领域安全能力建设）" (the “two majors”: carrying out major national strategies, and building security capacity in key fields), which ultra-long special treasury bonds pay for [cn-ga31-shenyang-11].
