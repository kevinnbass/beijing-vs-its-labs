This page is for a reader who wants to check our English against the Chinese. Each row gives one passage: who issued it and when, the page's own words, and our English. The number after the source leads to the reference list, which gives the page's address and the name of the saved copy.

The 61 passages are a selection from the argument (ARGUMENT_referenced.md): 60 hold what it quotes of Chinese state bodies, the Party, state media and a few companies, and one, the ministry's reply of passage 43, is from a page the argument cites and describes without quoting it; they are not all of it. The English is the argument's own wording wherever it gives one. Two second readers have checked it, neither a native reader of Chinese; no native reader has checked this page. The first, on Nov 23, 2026, read the 50 Chinese quotations the argument then had and found faults in the English beside 34; the argument and this page take the corrected wording. The second, on Nov 25, 2026, read all 61 passages, among them those the argument gained on Nov 24, 2026, which the first did not read (passages 19 to 23, 35, 50 to 52, 57 and 59 to 61, the second quotation of passage 29 and the end of passage 32; the English of passages 51 and 52 is ours, as the argument gives none). ## 2. Rules and targets on paper

These carry points 2 and 3. The purchase rule binds Party and government bodies at township level and above, the public institutions directly under Party committees and governments at those levels, and the departments' public institutions that support those bodies, when they buy the computers it names. Passage 6 stands in the same sentence as passage 5: it lets every other buyer leave the rule out. Passages 10 to 17 are eight of the nine provincial-level governments found to set a share for home-made computing, each by its plan's own measure: chips, equipment, hardware and software, or computing capacity; two are worded as aims (力争, strive to) and one is marked indicative. The ninth was found on Nov 25, 2026 and has no row of its own: Zhejiang's plan for its information and communications industry, posted on Jul 29, 2026, sets a target for that one sector, "到2030年，浙江信息通信行业算力规模达150EFLOPS，国产化算力占比超60%" (by 2030 the computing of Zhejiang's information and communications industry reaches 150 EFLOPS, with home-made computing over 60% of it) [5]. Passage 19, Sichuan's, is one of the 4 local voucher rules that pay for home-made computing only, among the 91 counted in the project's voucher tables (the rules found by searches that went province by province and city by city, saved and read, not a census of every local government); the other three are Ningbo's, Huangyan's and the Changsha Economic and Technological Development Zone's. The “two majors” of passage 20 are, in the words of a reply of Shenyang's science and technology bureau, "“两重”建设（国家重大战略实施和重点领域安全能力建设）" (the “two majors”: carrying out major national strategies, and building security capacity in key fields), which ultra-long special treasury bonds pay for [6].
