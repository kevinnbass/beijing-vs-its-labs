How to use this page

This page is for a reader who wants to check our English against the Chinese. Each row gives one passage: who issued it and when, the page's own words, and our English. The number after the source leads to the reference list, which gives the page's address and the name of the saved copy.

The 61 passages are a selection from the argument (ARGUMENT_referenced.md): 60 hold what it quotes of Chinese state bodies, the Party, state media and a few companies, and one, the ministry's reply of passage 43, is from a page the argument cites and describes without quoting it; they are not all of it. By a script's count they hold 74 of the argument's 381 Chinese quotations (a quotation the argument gives twice is counted twice), some at greater length; by the kind of the source that holds each, the 307 left out are 66 from company filings, 75 from press reports (of words on the record or from unnamed people), 42 from purchase notices, 123 from state texts, state media and other public bodies, 116 of them from texts that this page does not show, and 1 from a machine-made transcript. 2. Rules and targets on paper

These carry points 2 and 3. The purchase rule binds Party and government bodies at township level and above, the public institutions directly under Party committees and governments at those levels, and the departments' public institutions that support those bodies, when they buy the computers it names. Passage 6 stands in the same sentence as passage 5: it lets every other buyer leave the rule out. Passages 10 to 17 are eight of the nine provincial-level governments found to set a share for home-made computing, each by its plan's own measure: chips, equipment, hardware and software, or computing capacity; two are worded as aims (力争, strive to) and one is marked indicative. The ninth was found on Nov 25, 2026 and has no row of its own: Zhejiang's plan for its information and communications industry, posted on Jul 29, 2026, sets a target for that one sector, “到2030年，浙江信息通信行业算力规模达150EFLOPS，国产化算力占比超60%” (by 2030 the computing of Zhejiang's information and communications industry reaches 150 EFLOPS, with home-made computing over 60% of it) 5. EFLOPS (exaflops) is the unit in which the plan's table measures the scale of computing power. Passage 18 is Beijing city's plan, which sets an ability to build, not a share. Passage 19, Sichuan's, is one of the 4 local voucher rules that pay for home-made computing only, among the 93 counted in the project's voucher tables (the rules found by searches that went province by province and city by city, saved and read, not a census of every local government); the other three are Ningbo's, Huangyan's and the Changsha Economic and Technological Development Zone's. Passages 20 to 23 are national: the state planner backs home-made computing and vouchers; the national computing-network opinions set a share for green power, not for home-made chips; and the 2015 roadmap's forecast is advice, as the vice minister said at its launch. No published national plan found sets a home-made share for computing centres. The “two majors” of passage 20 are, in the words of a reply of Shenyang's science and technology bureau, “‘两重’建设（国家重大战略实施和重点领域安全能力建设）” (the “two majors”: carrying out major national strategies, and building security capacity in key fields), which ultra-long special treasury bonds pay for 6.
