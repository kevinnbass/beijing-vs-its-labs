Who is who in the chip-policy story

The bodies and people named in the record, by row (a few rows hold two that acted together): what each is, and what it did here. Beijing was not one actor, so the Chinese state is split into the side that restricted and the side that welcomed.

Draft by Claude, Anthropic's model. Written Nov 23, 2026 (UTC).

What a body is comes from a saved page, in that page's words, or is marked (general knowledge). English in round brackets after Chinese is ours, not an official translation.

Words from the two clips are machine transcripts, and words from videos are caption tracks (automatic, or of a maker the page does not state); each is marked with the time. Nobody has checked them by ear.

  • 1 a filled number: a document, a filing, a named person on the record, or a published commentary of state media or an industry body
  • 2 an open number: a press report citing unnamed people, a research firm's count, or captions and machine transcripts nobody has checked by ear

How to read the rows

The question behind this page: did Beijing's chip policy pull against what China's own AI companies wanted? The answer of the argument (ARGUMENT_referenced.md) is yes on direction, with five limits. One limit is that Beijing was not one actor; another is that what Beijing meant by its acts is not settled. This page shows who the actors were.

Two kinds of statement stand in the last column and are kept apart. On the record means a published document, a filing, or a named person's words. Reported means a press report that cites unnamed people. What Beijing told companies in private, which Chinese bodies approved what, and what China's customs did are all reported, not published policy.

Provinces, cities and districts that pay vouchersLocal governments that pay part of the bill for rented or bought computing (general knowledge for the description).On the record: counting one rule in force for each local government whose text is saved in a form a script could check and which states a share of the bill, 7 of the 91 found by searches that went province by province and city by city, saved and read (not a census of every local government), pay more for home-made computing and still pay for other computing, 4 (Sichuan's, Ningbo's, Huangyan's and the Changsha Economic and Technological Development Zone's) pay for home-made computing only, and 80 state no higher rate for home-made computing: 79 pay one rate whatever the chip, and Hangzhou's pays 30% or 20% by a type of computing facility that it does not name. These classes look at the payment only: 9 of the 80 give home-made computing something by another provision of the same rule, such as money to build or run home-made computing facilities (a count by script over the project's voucher tables, tables/local_vouchers_classes.tsv; the argument's point 3 gives the same count). What this page does not show

  • It does not rank the actors or say who decided. Who gave which instruction, and when, is reported from unnamed people.
  • It does not settle what Beijing meant by its acts: American bodies read them as leverage in trade talks or as a sovereign choice (the argument's fifth limit).
  • It is not a list of everyone. It leaves out most people and bodies that the argument does not use; a few rows come from the project's other records (Matt Pottinger, the second speaker, and Jeffrey Kessler among them).
  • Where a row says (general knowledge), no saved page was used for that description.
  • Neither clip, nor any caption track, has been checked by ear.
