# Who else says it, and who narrows it

## What this page is for

Palmer Luckey said in a clip that China does not want Nvidia to sell chips to Chinese companies, while Chinese companies want to buy them. This page lists who else has said the same, who has said it only in part, who has said something that narrows or cuts against it, and how American bodies read what Beijing meant by it.

Four things to keep in mind. The American officials each give it as their own understanding, sense or analysis. No published instruction was found in the 82 policy pages read, and Beijing's spokespeople would not confirm one. American bodies read Beijing's conduct mostly two ways, as leverage in trade talks and as a sovereign choice, and the argument leaves that open (its fifth limit). And the analysts give opinions: they are sorted here by what they say, not by whether they are right.

## 4. What Beijing meant: leverage, or a sovereign choice

The argument's fifth limit: what Beijing meant by its acts is not settled. No Chinese document found states a motive for the unpublished guidance to companies, or ties an act against Nvidia to the trade talks. Beijing's own texts give purposes for its public acts: the cyberspace regulator summoned Nvidia "为维护中国用户网络安全、数据安全" (to safeguard Chinese users' cyber and data security) [cn-policy-cac-h20-original]; the market regulator's Party group says its cases against Nvidia, Qualcomm and DuPont China "有力维护国家利益" (forcefully safeguarded national interests) [cn-media-party-samr-september]; and the regulator's annual report for 2025 lists the further investigation of Nvidia among cases that served "维护公平竞争" (to protect fair competition) [cn-ga15-samr-antitrust-annual-2025-pdf].

## Read with care

- **Saying it is not showing it.** The officials in section 1 give their understanding, sense or analysis. What shows the conflict is in the argument: the documents, the filings and the reports.
- **The Chinese side has not confirmed it.** No published instruction against buying Nvidia chips was found in the 82 policy pages read, and the foreign ministry's spokespeople gave no answer either way.
- **Washington stood in the way too.** The House committee's table shows that 55% of the chips in the applications the summary records were returned without action, with no reason given; the American rule and a 25% tariff also bore on the H200 (section 3, and the argument's point 20).
- **What Beijing meant is read more than one way.** Section 4 sets the readings side by side. They are readings, not findings.
- **Captions and clips.** Words from captions and machine transcripts are marked, with the time; nobody has checked them by ear. Both clips also make claims about Nvidia's chief executive that are outside this page. Those are the speakers' allegations, and no saved source here establishes them.
- **Commentary is opinion.** An analyst who states the conflict adds no evidence that it exists.
- **Not here.** Press reports from unnamed people that state the conflict, such as Reuters's headline "Chinese firms still want Nvidia chips despite government pressure not to buy, sources say" [cn-policy-firms-want-nvidia], are in the argument and in TIER2_REPORTING.md.
