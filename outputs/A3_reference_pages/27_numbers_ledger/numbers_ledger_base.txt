## How to read a row

The first column gives the number and what it counts. The second gives the source's own words, who said it and when, and **on whose word** it rests. The third gives later or related numbers, often of other things or for other groups, and says how they differ, or that none is held.

**On whose word** is one of seven things:

- **Document**: a text a government body published.
- **Filing**: a company's report to a securities regulator or a stock exchange, its results, or its own published statement.
- **Named person**: an official or executive on the record, sometimes as the press reported the words.
- **Unnamed people**: a press report that cites people it does not name.
- **Research firm**: a research firm's or broker's count, as the press or a company reported it.
- **Records**: public tender or procurement records, as a news agency read them.
- **Outlet's account**: a named outlet's account of an official act, such as a government press conference.

Two more labels appear: **the outlet's own words**, where a report states a number without saying whose it is, and **ours**, for a number this project made.

A date is the date of the act where the page gives one, otherwise the date of the report. A number that is found shows what the source says, not that it is true.

Read the 55% with the committee's own caution. Some applications are "not fully reflected in the May 11, 2026, summary document"; "For example, NVIDIA applied on December 19, 2025, to export 445,000 H200s to Tencent" [cn-usdocs-hfac-summary-20261119]. The table's only Tencent row of that date is an Nvidia application for 75,000 chips, approved; neither document says whether that row is the application of 445,000. So the 55% is a share of the table, not of everything applied for: the returned share would be 49.5% if that row is the application, counted at 445,000, and 48.6% if the 445,000 is a further application and was not returned without action (ours). The table gives no reason for any return; RWA is the export bureau's own short form: "If your original application was returned without action (RWA)" [cn-le04-bis-ear-748]. An application names a data-centre operator or ultimate consignee; it is not an order, and an approval here is Washington's licence, not Beijing's.

## Read with care

- **Most chip counts rest on unnamed people.** In section 1 the figures that do not are Washington's table and its committee's release, the American rule's 50% cap, and Nvidia's own (three from its filings, two from its results calls). The under secretary's statement that very few had been exported is on the record, but it is not a figure.
- **The steps are not one series.** Ordered, cleared, approved and delivered come from different reports, for different groups of companies, at different dates. Do not subtract one from another. An application in Washington's table names a data-centre operator or ultimate consignee; it is not an order, and approved there means an American licence, not a Chinese one.
- **The shares are not one series.** The 95% is a news agency's description and the chief executive's starting point; the 55% and 34% are IDC's counts of AI accelerator cards; TrendForce's 34% and 21% are another firm's measure, of every foreign supplier together.
- **Revenue is not chips.** Nvidia's China line is revenue by where its direct customers have their headquarters, for every product.
- **A target on paper is not what was done.** Section 6 shows what provinces and cities set, not what was installed.
- **Money and chips are different units.** Where a row gives both for one purchase, both are of those cards only (the RTX PRO 5500 orders Reuters reported on Oct 20, 2026; the university's two H200 cards); the row of the centres in Gansu and Shaanxi gives a chip count for one project and a cost for another; and the RTX PRO 6000D's price is one reported in September 2025, not the price of any order here. Do not turn the other rows from one unit into the other.
