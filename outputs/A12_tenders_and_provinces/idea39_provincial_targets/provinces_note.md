# Home-made computing targets by province: nine of 31 set a share

*Of China's 31 province-level governments, nine were found to set a share for home-made computing, each by its plan's own measure: chips, equipment, hardware and software, or computing capacity. The nine are Tianjin, Hebei, Inner Mongolia, Shanghai, Jiangsu, Zhejiang, Shandong, Guangdong, Sichuan; Zhejiang's is for the computing of one sector, its information and communications industry. 20 have a plan that was read and sets none. Tibet's plan is known only from accounts of it, its issuer's own among them, and for Xinjiang no computing plan was found. The nine shares run from 50% to 90%, count different things, and only Jiangsu's plan says whether its share binds: it marks it indicative, not binding. Of the nine, 5 rest on a saved official page (Tianjin, Shanghai, Jiangsu, Zhejiang, Guangdong; Shanghai for its later figure, its 50% being held as carried by a business daily), 2 on a saved Internet Archive capture of an official page (Hebei, Inner Mongolia), 1 on a saved copy on another site (Sichuan) and 1 on a provincial daily's account (Shandong). Local computing vouchers: of the 91 rules found by searches that went province by province and city by city, saved and read (not a census), 7 pay more for home-made computing and still pay for other computing, 4 pay for home-made computing only, and 80 state no higher rate for home-made computing: 79 pay one rate whatever the chip, and Hangzhou's plan pays 30% or 20% by a type of computing facility that it does not name.*

## The question

The project's argument says, in its point 3, how many of China's 31 province-level governments set a share for home-made computing, each by its plan's own measure (chips, equipment, hardware and software, or computing capacity): nine, by the count made here. This note asks what each of the 31 has set, and gives a row for every government: the nine with a share, the 20 whose plan was read and sets none, and Tibet and Xinjiang. A province-level government here means one of the 22 provinces, 5 autonomous regions and 4 municipalities of the mainland. The note also lists the local computing vouchers found, which pay part of a firm's bill for rented or bought computing.

No national target is given in this note; this work did not look for one. In the national computing-network opinions of December 2023, the words 国产 (home-made) and 自主可控 (independently controllable) do not stand at all, while 安全可靠 (secure and reliable) stands 4 times, so the search could see a hit [1].

## The answer in short

**1.** **Nine governments set a share.** Jiangsu: 50% by 2025 and 70% or more by 2030, of chips in newly built computing centres [2]. Shanghai: over 50% of chips in newly built intelligent-computing centres by 2025 [3], and its opinions of 2025 aim, for 2027, at over 70% of its intelligent computing being independently controllable [4]. Tianjin: over 60% of chips in all the city's computing centres by 2026 [5]. Guangdong: 70% of newly added computing by the end of 2025, an aim [6]. Hebei: 60% or more of newly added basic computing software and hardware, by 2025 [7]. Sichuan: over 50% of computing equipment, an aim [8]. Inner Mongolia: 50% or more of computing by 2025, among the goals for the region's hub of the national computing network [9]. Shandong: 90% of computing by 2025, in a 2022 plan known only from the provincial daily's account [10]. Zhejiang: over 60% of the computing of one sector, its information and communications industry, by 2030, the target of a project in that sector's five-year plan, posted Jul 29, 2026 [11]. Table 1 gives each in its document's own words.

**2.** **The nine do not count the same thing.** Jiangsu and Shanghai's 2024 plan count chips in newly built centres. Tianjin counts chips in all the city's centres. Guangdong counts newly added computing, Sichuan computing equipment, Inner Mongolia and Shandong computing. Hebei counts newly added basic computing software and hardware together. Zhejiang counts the computing of one sector, its information and communications industry. Shanghai's 2025 document counts independently controllable computing. So the numbers cannot be added or averaged.

**3.** **Only Jiangsu's plan says whether its share binds.** Its table of indicators marks the row 预期性 (indicative), not 约束性 (binding) [2]. In Sichuan's and Guangdong's plans the share is worded with 力争 (strive to). In Shanghai's plan of 2024 that word opens the sentence before the listed goals, not the sentence that sets the 50%; its text of 2025 has the word in the sentence with its 70%. Tianjin, Hebei and Inner Mongolia state it flat, with neither word. So does Zhejiang's sentence, which is the target of a project (工程目标) and stands outside its plan's table of indicators; that table marks one line binding, energy efficiency, and has no line for home-made computing [11]. Shandong's plan is known only from a newspaper's account, so its label cannot be told. In no page read is a home-made share marked binding. The second column of table 1 gives this row by row.

**4.** **20 governments have a plan that was read and sets no share** (Beijing, Shanxi, Liaoning, Jilin, Heilongjiang, Anhui, Fujian, Jiangxi, Henan, Hubei, Hunan, Guangxi, Hainan, Chongqing, Guizhou, Yunnan, Shaanxi, Gansu, Qinghai, Ningxia). For 9 of them the document read is a computing plan or computing measures (Beijing, Liaoning, Fujian, Jiangxi, Henan, Hubei, Hunan, Chongqing, Qinghai; Liaoning's computing plan is known only from the provincial daily's account, and its AI plan is read in full). For the other 11 the project's table of the 31 governments (tables/provinces_31.tsv) classes the document read as a plan or measures on artificial intelligence or the digital economy, or as a reward (Shanxi, Jilin, Heilongjiang, Anhui, Guangxi, Hainan, Guizhou, Yunnan, Shaanxi, Gansu, Ningxia); for those, no share in the page read is not no share in the province. Hainan's AI plan, one of the 20, was published abridged, "（此件公开发布，此文有删减）" (this document is published with cuts), so a clause left out of it cannot be ruled out [12]. They are in table 2.

**5.** **What some of them say instead.** Beijing aims, by 2027, at the ability to build intelligent-computing centres that are 100% independently controllable, "具备100%自主可控智算中心建设能力": an ability to build, not a share of use [13]. Guizhou aims at a national rank, "国产智算能力位居全国前列" [14]. Henan encourages intelligent-computing and supercomputing centres to take independently controllable routes such as Ascend and Hygon, "鼓励智算、超算中心采用昇腾、海光等自主可控技术路线", with no number [15]. Chongqing's 2026 plan sets shares only for green power, "确保新建数据中心绿电占比超过80%", and, in its table of indicators, for the uptake of new smart terminals and agents, "新一代智能终端、智能体等普及率" [16]. Shaanxi's computing item sets no share; on building it says "按需建设算力基础设施" (build computing infrastructure as needed) [17]. Zhejiang, which is in table 1 for its sector plan of 2026, has AI measures of 2025 that set no share: they urge government departments, computing centres and businesses to buy or use home-made software and hardware, "倡导政府部门、算力中心、经营主体采购或应用国产软硬件", and set voucher rates (below) [18].

**6.** **Tibet and Xinjiang.** Tibet's plan for 2024 to 2026 is known only from accounts of it, a trade newspaper's and its issuer's own news item; the newspaper's account gives a share for intelligent computing, "智算算力占比达到60%", and none for home-made hardware [19]. For Xinjiang no regional computing or AI plan was found; the region's 2024 plan for the economy, read in its place, supports computing centres and sets no home-made share [20]. Table 3.

**7.** **Rewards, not targets.** Two provinces have offered rewards to data centres whose basic software and hardware are 90% or more home-made: a one-off reward of up to 5 million yuan for new or rebuilt centres in Shanxi, "对基础软硬件实现国产化90%以上的新建、改建数据中心，给予最高不超过500万元的一次性奖励" [21], and a reward of up to 10 million yuan in Ningxia, by its industry department's account of a regional policy, "对基础软硬件实现国产化率90%以上的数据中心，给予企业最高不超过1000万元奖励" [22], a policy the region repealed by its decision 宁政规发〔2025〕5号, signed Nov 27, 2025 [23]. Table 4.

**8.** **Local vouchers: 7 of 91 pay more for home-made computing, 4 pay for it only.** Counting one rule in force for each local government whose text is saved in a form the checker reads and which states a share of the bill for rented or bought computing, 7 of the 91 found by searches that went province by province and city by city, saved and read, pay more for home-made computing and still pay for other computing (Binjiang district of Hangzhou, Wenzhou city, Zhejiang province, Chengdu city, Hubei province, Wuhan city, Longwan district and Wenzhou Bay New Area (Wenzhou)), 4 pay for home-made computing only (Sichuan province, Ningbo city, Huangyan district (Taizhou), Changsha Economic and Technological Development Zone), and 80 state no higher rate for home-made computing: 79 pay one rate whatever the chip, and 1, Hangzhou's plan of 2025, pays 30% or 20% by a type of computing facility that it does not name. They are a count of what those searches found, not a census of every local government. The rate counted is the general one, for an ordinary applicant: a higher rate for a named kind of firm, whatever the chip, does not change a rule's class (in tables 6 and 7 it is in the row's note). Shanghai's rule, counted at one rate, for one, pays up to 30% from the city and lets the city and its districts together pay up to 100% of the rent, for a year at most, for early-stage and small firms: "市区协同给予最多1年、最高100%的租金补贴" [24]. Beijing's E-Town, a development zone and not the city, dropped its tier: its rule of Nov 26, 2024 repeals its rule of March 2024, which paid up to 40% for home-made and up to 30% for other computing [25], and pays 30% for all [26]. Binjiang district of Hangzhou added a subsidy for other computing only with its rule of May 2025, by its bureau's account [27]. The class looks at that one payment only: 9 of the 80 also give home-made computing something by another provision of the same rule, and none of the 7 or the 4 does (below). Tables 6 and 7 give the 7 and the 4; the others are listed after them.

## Method

- **The 31 rows.** Most of the documents were read on Nov 23, 2026; later searches of the project, up to Nov 25, 2026, read more, and the project's table of the 31 governments (tables/provinces_31.tsv) sorts them. Every quotation is checked by script against its saved copy, and the build stops if any government's group here differs from that table, or if the counts of nine, 20, one and one change.
- **Searches and saving.** The searches of Nov 23, 2026 are listed in search_log.txt; later searches are not listed here. Internet Archive captures are saved with their capture dates; a capture shows the page as the Archive held it on that date. Three PDFs were saved by hand in a browser after the sites refused automated requests or could not be reached.
- **Damaged text layers.** The hand-saved PDFs of Qinghai's 2024 and 2026 plans and of Hunan's 2025 plan have text layers that drop characters. Nothing is quoted from them. Qinghai's and Hunan's rows quote the Archive captures of the governments' own web pages, and the one check made in a damaged PDF, of Qinghai's table of indicators, which is an image in the capture, is made against a reading of the page images: 3 strings checked in damaged PDFs, each found in the reading of the page images (transcription.txt); a planted false string was not.
- **How a document was judged to set no share.** For each of the 30 documents in tables 2, 3 and 5, a script listed every place where a home-made word (国产, 自主可控, 安全可控, 安全可靠, 自主化) stands within 60 characters of a percent sign: 11 places in all, in near_percent.txt, each read. None sets a share of home-made hardware. They are Beijing's ability to build, Shanxi's reward, two rates of rack use (Henan, Fujian), a rate for public data (Guangxi), and a rate already reached for chip-making equipment (Hunan). Where a row says a word is not in the page, a script found it 0 times and found a control word in the same page (absent.txt).
- **What each row rests on.** Every row begins with one of five marks. **Saved official page**: a copy in the project's store of a page on the issuing body's own site or its government's own site. **Saved by hand from an official page**: a PDF of such a page, saved by hand in a browser. **Saved Archive capture**: a copy in the project's store of an Internet Archive capture of an official page. **Saved copy on another site**: the document's text on any other site, such as another government's, a state platform, a news site or a policy site. **A newspaper's account**: a saved page in which a newspaper describes the document; the document's own text is not held. Of the 43 documents in the tables, 16 are saved official pages, 1 saved by hand, 9 saved Archive captures, 13 saved copies on other sites, and 4 newspapers' accounts.
- **Binding or indicative.** Chinese plans often mark each target 约束性 (binding) or 预期性 (expected, that is, indicative); that this is common practice is my general knowledge, not a saved source. A script counted both words in the 10 pages of table 1. Both stand in two plans, each in its table of indicators: Jiangsu's (预期性 15 times, one of them on its home-made share; 约束性 once, on the energy-efficiency row) and Zhejiang's (预期性 17 times, 约束性 once, on the energy-efficiency row; the table has no home-made line, and the share stands outside it). Neither stands in the other 8 pages, so for those the column says the share is not marked binding or indicative and gives the page's own wording where it bears on the point. A share not so marked is not thereby binding, nor thereby not binding: the page does not say. Hunan's and Qinghai's plans, which set no home-made share, each mark one target binding, energy efficiency [28] [29].
- **Kinds of document.** Whether the document read for a government without a share is a computing plan is taken from the project's table of the 31 governments (tables/provinces_31.tsv, its column kind_of_document), and counted by script.
- **Dates.** Each date is the one the page gives, and the page's own date string was checked by the script. Jiangsu's saved PDF carries no date of issue, so the plan is not dated here.
- **Vouchers.** The rule for counting is the project's (tables/local_vouchers_classes.tsv, made by its script cn_voucher_classes.py from its two voucher tables): a local rule in force (not a draft, an application guide, a payout list, or a plan that is not in force), its official text or a full copy saved, its rate words found by the checker, stating a share of the bill for rented or bought computing; one rule per government, its rule in force (where a later rule in force replaces an earlier one, the later is counted: for Wuhan, the Data Bureau's measures of October 2026, not the city's measures of February 2025, though both are in force); the rate is the general one, for an ordinary applicant. The class is read from the rule's own words: a higher rate for 国产 (home-made), 自主 (independent) or 自主可控 (independently controllable) computing than for other computing; a rate for home-made computing and none for other; or no higher rate for home-made computing. The searches covered the 31 province-level governments, 24 named cities and new areas, and the cities, districts and zones on the lists of four searches that went city by city; other districts and counties were reached only as they turned up. Of the 91, 52 are read on an official page or file of the issuing government or body and 39 in another copy, and 38 give the payment another name than a voucher (a subsidy, a reward or support). The build runs that script and stops unless its result equals the published table row by row, and unless the rows of tables 6 and 7, with their rates and classes, equal the 7 and 4 counted rules of the first two classes (a control shows that a changed rate is seen); it lists the other 80 from the table. The rules' other provisions are counted by the project's script cn_voucher_other.py from its table tables/local_vouchers_other_provisions.tsv.

## Local vouchers for rented or bought computing

Cities, districts and provinces pay part of a firm's bill for rented or bought computing. The rule words name the kinds: 国产 (home-made), 自主 (independent) or 自主可控 (independently controllable) against 非国产, 非自主, 非自主可控 or 其他 (not home-made, not independent, not independently controllable, other); the rules that pay more say 自主 or 自主可控. Each rate below is the share of the bill the rule states for an ordinary applicant; most rules state it as a ceiling (不超过, not more than, or 最高, at most), and higher rates for named kinds of firm are in the row's note. One rule per government: its rule in force that states a share of the bill.

The other one is Hangzhou's: the city government's plan of 2025, in force from Jul 15, 2025, repealed its measures of July 2024, which paid up to 30% for home-made and up to 20% for other facilities, "使用国产算力设施开展模型训练、推理和应用的企业补贴比例不超过算力合同费用的30%，使用其他算力设施的企业补贴比例不超过算力合同费用的20%" [148], and pays "按照算力设施的不同类型给予30%或20%的分档补贴" (30% or 20% by the type of computing facility), without saying which type gets which [149].

**Other provisions of the same rules.** The class looks at the counted payment only. 9 of the 80 also give home-made computing something by another provision of the same rule: 6 money to build, buy or run home-made computing facilities or equipment (Shanghai [24], E-Town [26], Songjiang [125], Fuzhou [82], Hebei District (Tianjin) [88], Lingang [103]), 3 an award for adapting to, or building on, home-made chips or computing (Hebei District (Tianjin) [88], Guizhou [85], Shenzhen [123]), and 1 a condition on its suppliers (Beijing city [71]): it pays for renting the computing of admitted suppliers, each of which must be able to serve on home-made chips with a home-made software stack, at one rate whatever chip the service runs on. Without that condition and Hebei district of Tianjin's award for research built on Ascend and other independently controllable routes, they are 8. None of the 7 that pay more or of the 4 that pay for home-made computing only gives anything by another provision. Such a provision is a premium for home-made computing beside the payment; it does not change that the payment itself is open to other computing.

**Read and not counted, and why.** The project's two voucher tables hold 237 rows, each a rule or document considered for the count; 146 were read and not counted, for these reasons: 32 an earlier or narrower rule of a government whose rule in force is counted; 26 an application guide, not the rule; 22 it states no share of the bill; 19 no checkable text of the rule is saved; 18 not a computing voucher rule; 18 a repealed or expired rule; 7 a draft; 1 a rule since repealed or revised, of a government with no counted rule; 1 a public notice of a list, not the rule; 1 its copy does not show a rule in force (a plan with no date of effect, or rules whose page gives no date of effect and leaves their terms blank); 1 the share it states is a discount the cluster's operator gives, not a share of the bill the rule pays. Among them are Hangzhou's measures of July 2024 and E-Town's rule of March 2024 (repealed), Longgang district of Shenzhen's measures of December 2024 (a rule since repealed or revised), application guides, and drafts. The rows are in tables/local_vouchers.tsv and tables/local_vouchers_more.tsv, and the class or the reason of each is in tables/local_vouchers_classes.tsv.

## What was not found

- **Whether any target was met.** Jiangsu's first step, Shanghai's 50%, and the shares of Guangdong, Hebei, Inner Mongolia and Shandong were set for 2025. No page read says whether they were reached.
- **The plan's own text, in some cases.** Shandong's 2022 plan and Liaoning's 2024 computing plan are known here from the provincial dailies' accounts on government sites, Tibet's from a trade newspaper's. Shandong's later implementation plan for computing is known only from a drafting note.
- **A computing plan, for many governments.** For 11 of the 20 in table 2 the document read is not a computing plan, and for Xinjiang no plan was found.
- **The same kind of plan in other provinces.** Zhejiang's share stands in a five-year plan for one sector, written by its communications administration with its development and reform commission. Plans of that kind were then searched as a class for the other 30 governments (the project's table of these plans, tables/provincial_ict_plans.tsv): the plan for 2026 to 2030 was read in full for 10 and as a consultation draft for 2, only the plan for 2021 to 2025 for 7, and for 11 no plan text was found; none of the texts read sets a home-made share, so the nine remain a count of what was found, not a census.
- **Clauses on enforcement.** The share sentences are goals in plans. This work did not read the plans through for what follows if a goal is missed, and says nothing about it.
- **A census of vouchers.** The 91 rules counted are those found by searches that went province by province and city by city, saved and read; districts and counties not on the lists of the city-by-city searches were reached only as they turned up, and places not searched may have others.

## What a reader may and may not conclude

- **May.** That nine province-level governments were found to set a share for home-made computing, each by its plan's own measure (Shandong's known only from the provincial daily's account), one of them (Zhejiang) for one sector's computing, 5 of them on a saved official page (Shanghai's for its later figure, for 2027, only). That the shares run from 50% to 90%, with dates from 2025 to 2030, and count different things. That Jiangsu's plan marks its share indicative, not binding, and that no page read marks a home-made share binding. That 20 governments have a plan read that sets no share, some of them saying instead what they will build or encourage. That two provinces tied money to a home-made rate instead (Shanxi; Ningxia, whose policy has since been repealed). That of the 91 local voucher rules counted, rules found and not a census, 7 pay more for home-made computing, 4 pay for home-made computing only and 80 state no higher rate for home-made computing.
- **May not.** That the other governments have no target: for 11 of them the page read was not a computing plan. That any target was met. That a share without a label is binding, or that it is not. That a target binds a private company's purchases: the pages read set goals for a province's computing centres or capacity. That the shares can be compared, added or averaged. That there is a national target. That local governments in general pay less for other computing: most rules counted state no higher rate for home-made computing.
