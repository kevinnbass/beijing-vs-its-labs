# Home-made chip targets by province: eight of 31 set a share

*Of China's 31 province-level governments, eight were found to set a share for home-made computing hardware (Tianjin, Hebei, Inner Mongolia, Shanghai, Jiangsu, Shandong, Guangdong, Sichuan). 21 have a plan that was read and sets none. Tibet's plan is known only from a trade newspaper's account, and for Xinjiang no computing plan was found. The eight shares run from 50% to 90%, count different things, and only Jiangsu's plan says whether its share binds: it marks it indicative, not binding. Of the eight, 4 rest on a saved official page (Tianjin, Shanghai, Jiangsu, Guangdong; Shanghai for its later figure, its 50% being held as carried by a business daily), 2 on a saved Internet Archive capture of an official page (Hebei, Inner Mongolia), 1 on a saved copy on another site (Sichuan) and 1 on a provincial daily's account (Shandong). Local computing vouchers: of 15 rules found, 4 pay more for home-made computing and 11 pay one rate whatever the chip.*

Draft by Claude, Anthropic's model. Local; not published.

Written Nov 23, 2026 (UTC) for idea 39 of the chip-policy project, and brought into line the same day with the project's fourth edit of its core. Words between straight double quotes are a page's own words and were found in its saved copy by a script. English in round brackets is ours, not an official translation. Three PDFs saved by hand have damaged text layers; they are read here only from their page images.

## The question

The project's argument says, in its point 3, that eight of China's 31 province-level governments set a share for home-made computing hardware. Idea 39 asked what each of the 31 has set. This note gives a row for every government: the eight with a share, the 21 whose plan was read and sets none, and Tibet and Xinjiang. A province-level government here means one of the 22 provinces, 5 autonomous regions and 4 municipalities of the mainland. The note also lists the local computing vouchers found, which pay part of the bill for rented computing.

No national target is given in this note; this work did not look for one. In the national computing-network opinions of December 2023, the words 国产 (home-made) and 自主可控 (independently controllable) do not stand at all, while 安全可靠 (secure and reliable) stands 4 times, so the search could see a hit [1].

## The answer in short

**1.** **Eight governments set a share.** Jiangsu: 50% by 2025 and 70% or more by 2030, of chips in newly built computing centres [2]. Shanghai: over 50% of chips in newly built intelligent-computing centres by 2025 [3], and by 2027 over 70% of its intelligent computing to be independently controllable [4]. Tianjin: over 60% of chips in all the city's computing centres by 2026 [5]. Guangdong: 70% of newly added computing by the end of 2025 [6]. Hebei: 60% or more of newly added computing software and hardware, by 2025 [7]. Sichuan: over 50% of computing equipment [8]. Inner Mongolia: 50% or more of computing by 2025, among the goals for the region's hub of the national computing network [9]. Shandong: 90% of computing by 2025, in a 2022 plan known only from the provincial daily's account [10]. Table 1 gives each in its document's own words.

**2.** **The eight do not count the same thing.** Jiangsu and Shanghai's 2024 plan count chips in newly built centres. Tianjin counts chips in all the city's centres. Guangdong counts newly added computing, Sichuan computing equipment, Inner Mongolia and Shandong computing. Hebei counts newly added software and hardware together. Shanghai's 2025 document counts independently controllable computing. So the numbers cannot be added or averaged.

**3.** **Only Jiangsu's plan says whether its share binds.** Its table of indicators marks the row 预期性 (indicative), not 约束性 (binding) [2]. In Shanghai's two documents, Sichuan's and Guangdong's, the share stands under the word 力争 (strive to). Tianjin, Hebei and Inner Mongolia state it flat, with neither word. Shandong's plan is known only from a newspaper's account, so its label cannot be told. In no page read is a home-made share marked binding. The second column of table 1 gives this row by row.

**4.** **21 governments have a plan that was read and sets no share** (Beijing, Shanxi, Liaoning, Jilin, Heilongjiang, Zhejiang, Anhui, Fujian, Jiangxi, Henan, Hubei, Hunan, Guangxi, Hainan, Chongqing, Guizhou, Yunnan, Shaanxi, Gansu, Qinghai, Ningxia). For 9 of them the document read is a computing plan or computing measures (Beijing, Liaoning, Fujian, Jiangxi, Henan, Hubei, Hunan, Chongqing, Qinghai). For the other 12 the core's table classes the document read as a plan or measures on artificial intelligence or the digital economy, or as a reward (Shanxi, Jilin, Heilongjiang, Zhejiang, Anhui, Guangxi, Hainan, Guizhou, Yunnan, Shaanxi, Gansu, Ningxia); for those, no share in the page read is not no share in the province. They are in table 2.

**5.** **What some of them say instead.** Beijing aims, by 2027, at the ability to build computing centres that are 100% independently controllable, "具备100%自主可控智算中心建设能力": an ability to build, not a share of use [11]. Guizhou aims at a national rank, "国产智算能力位居全国前列" [12]. Henan encourages two home-made chip lines by name, "鼓励智算、超算中心采用昇腾、海光等自主可控技术路线", with no number [13]. Chongqing's 2026 plan sets a share only for green power, "确保新建数据中心绿电占比超过80%" [14]. Shaanxi's computing item sets no share; on building it says "按需建设算力基础设施" (build computing infrastructure as needed) [15]. Zhejiang's AI measures urge government departments, computing centres and businesses to buy or use home-made software and hardware, "倡导政府部门、算力中心、经营主体采购或应用国产软硬件", and set voucher rates (below) [16].

**6.** **Tibet and Xinjiang.** Tibet's plan for 2024 to 2026 is known only from a trade newspaper's account; the account gives a share for intelligent computing, "智算算力占比达到60%", and none for home-made hardware [17]. For Xinjiang no regional computing or AI plan was found; the region's 2024 plan for the economy, read in its place, supports computing centres and sets no home-made share [18]. Table 3.

**7.** **Rewards, not targets.** Two provinces reward data centres whose basic software and hardware are 90% or more home-made: a one-off reward of up to 5 million yuan for new or rebuilt centres in Shanxi, "对基础软硬件实现国产化90%以上的新建、改建数据中心，给予最高不超过500万元的一次性奖励" [19], and a reward of up to 10 million yuan in Ningxia, by its industry department's account of a regional policy, "对基础软硬件实现国产化率90%以上的数据中心，给予企业最高不超过1000万元奖励" [20]. Table 4.

**8.** **Local vouchers: 4 of 15 pay more for home-made computing.** Counting one rule in force for each local government whose text is saved in a form the checker reads and which states a share of the bill for rented computing, 4 of 15 pay more for home-made computing (Hangzhou city, Zhejiang province, Wenzhou city, Binjiang district of Hangzhou) and 11 pay one rate whatever the chip. Every rule counted also pays for other computing. These are the rules found, not a census. Beijing's E-Town, a development zone and not the city, dropped its tier: its rule of Nov 26, 2024 repeals its rule of March 2024 and pays 30% for all [21]. Binjiang district of Hangzhou began paying for other computing only in May 2025, by its bureau's account [22]. Tables 6 and 7.

## Method

- **The 31 rows.** This folder read most of the documents in rounds 1 to 3 (Nov 23, 2026, 05:30 to 07:22 UTC). Later agents of the project read more, and the core's table tables/provinces_31.tsv sorts the 31 governments. In round 4 this note took their saved ids and exact words and checked each quotation again by script. build.py stops if any government's group here differs from that table, or if the counts of eight, 21, one and one change.
- **Searches and saving.** Round 1's searches are listed in search_log.txt; the later agents' are in their own folders. Pages were saved with the project's fetcher. Internet Archive captures were asked through the machine's one queue and filed in the saved store by the project's session; a capture shows the page as the Archive held it on the capture's date. Three PDFs were saved by hand in the owner's browser after the sites refused this machine or could not be reached from it. No web request was made in round 4.
- **Damaged text layers.** The hand-saved PDFs of Qinghai's 2024 and 2026 plans and of Hunan's 2025 plan have text layers that drop characters. Nothing is quoted from them. Qinghai's and Hunan's rows quote the Archive captures of the governments' own web pages, and the one check made in a damaged PDF, of Qinghai's table of indicators, which is an image in the capture, is made against a reading of the page images: 3 strings checked in damaged PDFs, each found in the reading of the page images (transcription.txt); a planted false string was not.
- **How a document was judged to set no share.** For each of the 30 documents in tables 2, 3 and 5, a script listed every place where a home-made word (国产, 自主可控, 安全可控, 安全可靠, 自主化) stands within 60 characters of a percent sign: 11 places in all, in near_percent.txt, each read. None sets a share of home-made hardware. They are Beijing's ability to build, Shanxi's reward, two rates of rack use (Henan, Fujian), a rate for public data (Guangxi), and a rate already reached for chip-making equipment (Hunan). Where a row says a word is not in the page, a script found it 0 times and found a control word in the same page (absent.txt).
- **What each row rests on.** Every row begins with one of five marks. **Saved official page**: a copy in the project's store of a page on the issuing body's own site or its government's own site. **Saved by hand from an official page**: a PDF of such a page that the owner saved in his browser. **Saved Archive capture**: a copy in the project's store of an Internet Archive capture of an official page. **Saved copy on another site**: the document's text on any other site, such as another government's, a state platform, a news site or a policy site. **A newspaper's account**: a saved page in which a newspaper describes the document; the document's own text is not held. Of the 42 documents in the tables, 15 are saved official pages, 1 saved by hand, 9 saved Archive captures, 13 saved copies on other sites, and 4 newspapers' accounts.
- **Binding or indicative.** Chinese plans often mark each target 约束性 (binding) or 预期性 (expected, that is, indicative); that this is common practice is my general knowledge, not a saved source. A script counted both words in the 9 pages of table 1. Both stand only in Jiangsu's plan: 预期性 15 times, 约束性 once, on the energy-efficiency row. Neither stands in the other 8 pages, so for those the column says no label and gives the page's own wording where it bears on the point. No label does not mean binding and does not mean not binding: the page does not say. Hunan's and Qinghai's plans, which set no home-made share, each mark one target binding, energy efficiency [23] [24].
- **Kinds of document.** Whether the document read for a government without a share is a computing plan is taken from the core's table (its column kind_of_document), counted by build.py.
- **Dates.** Each date is the one the page gives, and the page's own date string was checked by the script. Jiangsu's saved PDF carries no date of issue, so the plan is not dated here.
- **Vouchers.** The rule for counting is the one the project's figure uses: a local rule in force (not a draft, a plan or an application guide), its official text or a full copy saved, its words found by the checker, stating a share of the bill for rented computing; one row per government, its latest such rule. build.py counts the rows of vouchers.txt and checks that each rate stands in the rule's quoted words.

## What was not found

- **Whether any target was met.** Jiangsu's first step, Shanghai's 50%, and the shares of Guangdong, Hebei, Inner Mongolia and Shandong were set for 2025. No page read says whether they were reached.
- **The plan's own text, in some cases.** Shandong's 2022 plan and Liaoning's 2024 computing plan are known here from the provincial dailies' accounts on government sites, Tibet's from a trade newspaper's. Shandong's later implementation plan for computing is known only from a drafting note.
- **A computing plan, for many governments.** For 12 of the 21 in table 2 the document read is not a computing plan, and for Xinjiang no plan was found.
- **Clauses on enforcement.** The share sentences are goals in plans. This work did not read the plans through for what follows if a goal is missed, and says nothing about it.
- **A census of vouchers.** The 15 rules counted are those found and saved; cities and districts not searched may have others.

## What a reader may and may not conclude

- **May.** That eight province-level governments have published a share for home-made computing hardware, 4 of them on a saved official page. That the shares run from 50% to 90%, with dates from 2025 to 2030, and count different things. That Jiangsu's plan marks its share indicative, not binding, and that no page read marks a home-made share binding. That 21 governments have a plan read that sets no share, some of them saying instead what they will build or encourage. That two provinces tie money to a home-made rate instead (Shanxi, Ningxia). That of 15 local voucher rules counted, 4 pay more for home-made computing and 11 pay one rate, and that every rule counted pays for other computing too.
- **May not.** That the other governments have no target: for 12 of them the page read was not a computing plan. That any target was met. That a share without a label is binding, or that it is not. That a target binds a private company's purchases: the pages read set goals for a province's computing centres or capacity. That the shares can be compared, added or averaged. That there is a national target. That local governments in general pay less for other computing: most rules counted pay one rate.

## The files

- provinces.txt: the table as typed, one line per document (made in round 4 by round4/make_rows.py from the round-3 file). provinces.tsv: the same with the address and saved id of every document. vouchers.txt and vouchers.tsv: the voucher rules counted. counts.tsv: governments by group and by kind of document. quotes.tsv: every string taken from a saved page. near_percent.txt and absent.txt: the two checks behind the words no share and not in the page. build.py rebuilds all of it and stops if a string is not found.
