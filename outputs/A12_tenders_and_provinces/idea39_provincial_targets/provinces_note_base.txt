# Home-made computing targets by province: nine of 31 set a share

@deck Of China's 31 province-level governments, nine were found to set a share for home-made computing, each by its plan's own measure: chips, equipment, hardware and software, or computing capacity. The nine are Tianjin, Hebei, Inner Mongolia, Shanghai, Jiangsu, Zhejiang, Shandong, Guangdong, Sichuan; Zhejiang's is for the computing of one sector, its information and communications industry. 20 have a plan that was read and sets none. Tibet's plan is known only from accounts of it, its issuer's own among them, and for Xinjiang no computing plan was found. The nine shares run from 50% to 90%, count different things, and only Jiangsu's plan says whether its share binds: it marks it indicative, not binding. Of the nine, 5 rest on a saved official page (Tianjin, Shanghai, Jiangsu, Zhejiang, Guangdong; Shanghai for its later figure, its 50% being held as carried by a business daily), 2 on a saved Internet Archive capture of an official page (Hebei, Inner Mongolia), 1 on a saved copy on another site (Sichuan) and 1 on a provincial daily's account (Shandong). Local computing vouchers: of the 91 rules found by searches that went province by province and city by city, saved and read (not a census), 7 pay more for home-made computing and still pay for other computing, 4 pay for home-made computing only, and 80 state no higher rate for home-made computing: 79 pay one rate whatever the chip, and Hangzhou's plan pays 30% or 20% by a type of computing facility that it does not name.

## The question

The project's argument says, in its point 3, how many of China's 31 province-level governments set a share for home-made computing, each by its plan's own measure (chips, equipment, hardware and software, or computing capacity): nine, by the count made here. This note asks what each of the 31 has set, and gives a row for every government: the nine with a share, the 20 whose plan was read and sets none, and Tibet and Xinjiang. A province-level government here means one of the 22 provinces, 5 autonomous regions and 4 municipalities of the mainland. The note also lists the local computing vouchers found, which pay part of a firm's bill for rented or bought computing.

No national target is given in this note; this work did not look for one. In the national computing-network opinions of December 2023, the words 国产 (home-made) and 自主可控 (independently controllable) do not stand at all, while 安全可靠 (secure and reliable) stands 4 times, so the search could see a hit [cn-policy-computing-network-1779].

## The answer in short

1. **Nine governments set a share.** Jiangsu: 50% by 2025 and 70% or more by 2030, of chips in newly built computing centres [cn-factions-jiangsu-plan]. Shanghai: over 50% of chips in newly built intelligent-computing centres by 2025 [cn-policy-shanghai-domestic-target], and its opinions of 2025 aim, for 2027, at over 70% of its intelligent computing being independently controllable [cn-factions-shanghai-cloud-20250324]. Tianjin: over 60% of chips in all the city's computing centres by 2026 [cn-o39-tianjin-plan-2024]. Guangdong: 70% of newly added computing by the end of 2025, an aim [cn-o39b-guangdong-yuesuan-2024]. Hebei: 60% or more of newly added basic computing software and hardware, by 2025 [cn-o39-hebei-opinion-2024-wayback]. Sichuan: over 50% of computing equipment, an aim [cn-factions-sichuan50]. Inner Mongolia: 50% or more of computing by 2025, among the goals for the region's hub of the national computing network [cn-o39-nmg-plan-pdf-wayback]. Shandong: 90% of computing by 2025, in a 2022 plan known only from the provincial daily's account [cn-o39-shandong-network-2022]. Zhejiang: over 60% of the computing of one sector, its information and communications industry, by 2030, the target of a project in that sector's five-year plan, posted Jul 29, 2026 [cn-ga31-cndocs-52]. 2. **The nine do not count the same thing.** Jiangsu and Shanghai's 2024 plan count chips in newly built centres. Tianjin counts chips in all the city's centres. Guangdong counts newly added computing, Sichuan computing equipment, Inner Mongolia and Shandong computing. Hebei counts newly added basic computing software and hardware together. Zhejiang counts the computing of one sector, its information and communications industry. Shanghai's 2025 document counts independently controllable computing. So the numbers cannot be added or averaged.
3. **Only Jiangsu's plan says whether its share binds.** Its table of indicators marks the row 预期性 (indicative), not 约束性 (binding) [cn-factions-jiangsu-plan]. In Sichuan's and Guangdong's plans the share is worded with 力争 (strive to). In Shanghai's plan of 2024 that word opens the sentence before the listed goals, not the sentence that sets the 50%; its text of 2025 has the word in the sentence with its 70%. Tianjin, Hebei and Inner Mongolia state it flat, with neither word. So does Zhejiang's sentence, which is the target of a project (工程目标) and stands outside its plan's table of indicators; that table marks one line binding, energy efficiency, and has no line for home-made computing [cn-ga31-cndocs-52]. Shandong's plan is known only from a newspaper's account, so its label cannot be told. In no page read is a home-made share marked binding. The second column of table 1 gives this row by row.

5. **What some of them say instead.** Beijing aims, by 2027, at the ability to build intelligent-computing centres that are 100% independently controllable, "具备100%自主可控智算中心建设能力": an ability to build, not a share of use [cn-factions-beijing-plan-20240424]. ## Method

- **The 31 rows.** Most of the documents were read on Nov 23, 2026; later searches of the project, up to Nov 25, 2026, read more, and the project's table of the 31 governments (tables/provinces_31.tsv) sorts them. Every quotation is checked by script against its saved copy, and the build stops if any government's group here differs from that table, or if the counts of nine, 20, one and one change.
- **Searches and saving.** The searches of Nov 23, 2026 are listed in search_log.txt; later searches are not listed here. Internet Archive captures are saved with their capture dates; a capture shows the page as the Archive held it on that date. Three PDFs were saved by hand in a browser after the sites refused automated requests or could not be reached.
- **Damaged text layers.** The hand-saved PDFs of Qinghai's 2024 and 2026 plans and of Hunan's 2025 plan have text layers that drop characters. Nothing is quoted from them. Qinghai's and Hunan's rows quote the Archive captures of the governments' own web pages, and the one check made in a damaged PDF, of Qinghai's table of indicators, which is an image in the capture, is made against a reading of the page images: 3 strings checked in damaged PDFs, each found in the reading of the page images (transcription.txt); a planted false string was not.
- **How a document was judged to set no share.** For each of the 30 documents in tables 2, 3 and 5, a script listed every place where a home-made word (国产, 自主可控, 安全可控, 安全可靠, 自主化) stands within 60 characters of a percent sign: 11 places in all, in near_percent.txt, each read. None sets a share of home-made hardware. They are Beijing's ability to build, Shanxi's reward, two rates of rack use (Henan, Fujian), a rate for public data (Guangxi), and a rate already reached for chip-making equipment (Hunan). Where a row says a word is not in the page, a script found it 0 times and found a control word in the same page (absent.txt).
- **What each row rests on.** Every row begins with one of five marks. **Saved official page**: a copy in the project's store of a page on the issuing body's own site or its government's own site. **Saved by hand from an official page**: a PDF of such a page, saved by hand in a browser. **Saved Archive capture**: a copy in the project's store of an Internet Archive capture of an official page. **Saved copy on another site**: the document's text on any other site, such as another government's, a state platform, a news site or a policy site. **A newspaper's account**: a saved page in which a newspaper describes the document; the document's own text is not held. Of the 43 documents in the tables, 16 are saved official pages, 1 saved by hand, 9 saved Archive captures, 13 saved copies on other sites, and 4 newspapers' accounts.
- **Binding or indicative.** Chinese plans often mark each target 约束性 (binding) or 预期性 (expected, that is, indicative); that this is common practice is my general knowledge, not a saved source. A script counted both words in the 10 pages of table 1. Both stand in two plans, each in its table of indicators: Jiangsu's (预期性 15 times, one of them on its home-made share; 约束性 once, on the energy-efficiency row) and Zhejiang's (预期性 17 times, 约束性 once, on the energy-efficiency row; the table has no home-made line, and the share stands outside it). Neither stands in the other 8 pages, so for those the column says the share is not marked binding or indicative and gives the page's own wording where it bears on the point. A share not so marked is not thereby binding, nor thereby not binding: the page does not say. Hunan's and Qinghai's plans, which set no home-made share, each mark one target binding, energy efficiency [cn-o39-hunan-green-compute-plan-2025-wayback] [man-cn-o39-qinghai-compute-15fyp-2026].
- **Kinds of document.** Whether the document read for a government without a share is a computing plan is taken from the project's table of the 31 governments (tables/provinces_31.tsv, its column kind_of_document), and counted by script.
- **Dates.** Each date is the one the page gives, and the page's own date string was checked by the script. Jiangsu's saved PDF carries no date of issue, so the plan is not dated here.
- **Vouchers.** The rule for counting is the project's (tables/local_vouchers_classes.tsv, made by its script cn_voucher_classes.py from its two voucher tables): a local rule in force (not a draft, an application guide, a payout list, or a plan that is not in force), its official text or a full copy saved, its rate words found by the checker, stating a share of the bill for rented or bought computing; one rule per government, its rule in force (where a later rule in force replaces an earlier one, the later is counted: for Wuhan, the Data Bureau's measures of October 2026, not the city's measures of February 2025, though both are in force); the rate is the general one, for an ordinary applicant. The class is read from the rule's own words: a higher rate for 国产 (home-made), 自主 (independent) or 自主可控 (independently controllable) computing than for other computing; a rate for home-made computing and none for other; or no higher rate for home-made computing. The searches covered the 31 province-level governments, 24 named cities and new areas, and the cities, districts and zones on the lists of four searches that went city by city; other districts and counties were reached only as they turned up. Of the 91, 52 are read on an official page or file of the issuing government or body and 39 in another copy, and 38 give the payment another name than a voucher (a subsidy, a reward or support). The build runs that script and stops unless its result equals the published table row by row, and unless the rows of tables 6 and 7, with their rates and classes, equal the 7 and 4 counted rules of the first two classes (a control shows that a changed rate is seen); it lists the other 80 from the table. The rules' other provisions are counted by the project's script cn_voucher_other.py from its table tables/local_vouchers_other_provisions.tsv.

## Local vouchers for rented or bought computing

Cities, districts and provinces pay part of a firm's bill for rented or bought computing. The rule words name the kinds: 国产 (home-made), 自主 (independent) or 自主可控 (independently controllable) against 非国产, 非自主, 非自主可控 or 其他 (not home-made, not independent, not independently controllable, other); the rules that pay more say 自主 or 自主可控. Each rate below is the share of the bill the rule states for an ordinary applicant; most rules state it as a ceiling (不超过, not more than, or 最高, at most), and higher rates for named kinds of firm are in the row's note. One rule per government: its rule in force that states a share of the bill.

## What was not found

- **Whether any target was met.** Jiangsu's first step, Shanghai's 50%, and the shares of Guangdong, Hebei, Inner Mongolia and Shandong were set for 2025. No page read says whether they were reached.
- **The plan's own text, in some cases.** Shandong's 2022 plan and Liaoning's 2024 computing plan are known here from the provincial dailies' accounts on government sites, Tibet's from a trade newspaper's. Shandong's later implementation plan for computing is known only from a drafting note.
- **A computing plan, for many governments.** For 11 of the 20 in table 2 the document read is not a computing plan, and for Xinjiang no plan was found.
- **The same kind of plan in other provinces.** Zhejiang's share stands in a five-year plan for one sector, written by its communications administration with its development and reform commission. Plans of that kind were then searched as a class for the other 30 governments (the project's table of these plans, tables/provincial_ict_plans.tsv): the plan for 2026 to 2030 was read in full for 10 and as a consultation draft for 2, only the plan for 2021 to 2025 for 7, and for 11 no plan text was found; none of the texts read sets a home-made share, so the nine remain a count of what was found, not a census.
- **Clauses on enforcement.** The share sentences are goals in plans. This work did not read the plans through for what follows if a goal is missed, and says nothing about it.
- **A census of vouchers.** The 91 rules counted are those found by searches that went province by province and city by city, saved and read; districts and counties not on the lists of the city-by-city searches were reached only as they turned up, and places not searched may have others.

## What a reader may and may not conclude

- **May.** That nine province-level governments were found to set a share for home-made computing, each by its plan's own measure (Shandong's known only from the provincial daily's account), one of them (Zhejiang) for one sector's computing, 5 of them on a saved official page (Shanghai's for its later figure, for 2027, only). That the shares run from 50% to 90%, with dates from 2025 to 2030, and count different things. That Jiangsu's plan marks its share indicative, not binding, and that no page read marks a home-made share binding. That 20 governments have a plan read that sets no share, some of them saying instead what they will build or encourage. That two provinces tied money to a home-made rate instead (Shanxi; Ningxia, whose policy has since been repealed). That of the 91 local voucher rules counted, rules found and not a census, 7 pay more for home-made computing, 4 pay for home-made computing only and 80 state no higher rate for home-made computing.
- **May not.** That the other governments have no target: for 11 of them the page read was not a computing plan. That any target was met. That a share without a label is binding, or that it is not. That a target binds a private company's purchases: the pages read set goals for a province's computing centres or capacity. That the shares can be compared, added or averaged. That there is a national target. That local governments in general pay less for other computing: most rules counted state no higher rate for home-made computing.

## The files

- provinces.txt: the table as typed, one line per document. provinces.tsv: the same with the address and saved id of every document. vouchers.txt and vouchers.tsv: the voucher rules counted that favour home-made computing (the others are listed from the project's table of classes). counts.tsv: governments by group and by kind of document. quotes.tsv: every string taken from a saved page. near_percent.txt and absent.txt: the two checks behind the words no share and not in the page. build.py rebuilds all of it and stops if a string is not found.
