# What purchase notices name: Nvidia cards and home-made machines, 2024 to 2026

*It is a sample found by search and proves nothing about proportions. 47 notices from Chinese universities and colleges, research institutes and one earthquake agency: 24 name Nvidia hardware, and 14 of those 24 are one university's (Wuhan University); 7 name or ask for home-made accelerator hardware (one of them, a Liaoning university's tender, asks only that the platform it buys support home-made chips it names, beside foreign ones); 16 name no accelerator maker or model. Later records add China Mobile's purchasing as the trade press reports it: the larger part, by value, of a 2025 purchase was for equipment of the CANN software ecosystem, and a tender of March 2026 asked for such equipment only (Huawei's CANN, as the trade site says of that tender). They also add two universities' notices of July 2026 to buy Nvidia's RTX PRO 6000D. A notice to buy is neither a purchase made nor a delivery.*

Draft by Claude, Anthropic's model.

## The question

How many published purchase notices of 2024 to 2026 name Nvidia hardware, and how many name home-made chips? A true census is not possible here. China's purchase notices sit on thousands of sites, many notices never name the chip, and the searches for the sample were made on one day. What follows is a bounded sample with a stated method and, apart from it, later records that later searches found.

## What the sample shows

**1.** **Universities' and institutes' own notices named Nvidia hardware in each of the three years.** The 24 notices in table 1 name the A100, A800, H100, H200, L40, L40S, L20, RTX A6000, RTX 4080 SUPER, RTX 4090, RTX 4090 D and RTX 5090. Most are small purchases, from a single card to a few servers. In the sample, the latest dated notice that names a chip of the A or H series is of Jun 30, 2025 (H200 cards, to rent); the three dated after it name the RTX 5090. Outside the sample, Wuhan University posted a bidding notice for A100 cards in July 2026 (see the later records), and Reuters reports a university's purchase record of October 2026 for two H200 chips (see the reports known before the sample).

**2.** **The largest purchases in the sample are of Huawei machines, and their titles say home-made.** Sun Yat-sen University awarded 23.99 million yuan for one Huawei cluster [1]. Wuhan University of Science and Technology awarded 20.02 million yuan for ten sets and other items, in a project whose name says ultra-long-term special treasury bonds [2]. Both are in table 2.

**3.** **In the sample, most award notices from the central government purchasing site do not name the chip.** Of the 14 award or result notices in the sample that come from that site (ccgp.gov.cn; its name does not make the buyers central bodies: they are the universities, colleges, institutes and agency named in the tables), 12 name no accelerator by maker or model (most give a server brand and model), 2 name a Huawei Atlas machine, and the number that name an Nvidia model is 0. A count of notices by chip therefore misses much of what is bought.

**4.** **The sample leans on one university.** 14 of the 24 notices in table 1 are Wuhan University's, whose purchasing site publishes small purchases with their full specification. That is a fact about one site, not about China.

## What a purchase record can and cannot show

- **Can show.** That a named buyer asked for, or chose, a named product, in a stated number, at a stated budget or price, on a stated date and under stated conditions, in its own words. A tender, purchase notice or purchase filing shows what was asked for. A result or award notice shows a contract decision; a result notice of a proposed award (拟成交) and a list of bid candidates (中标候选人) name the bidders proposed to win, before the award.
- **Cannot show.** That the goods were delivered, paid for or put to use. How imported chips reached China, or whether a sale met United States export rules: no notice says. What share of all purchases went to Nvidia or to home-made hardware: many notices name no chip, and the notices here were found by search. Whose chips are in machines that a tender describes only by a software ecosystem (CUDA-like, CANN), unless the page says. And a trade site's account of a tender is not the tender itself.

## Method

- **Start.** Five Reuters reports that rest on tender documents and purchase records, and one university's own notice, were known before the sample. They are set out in the section on reports known before the sample.
- **Searches.** 22 web searches on Nov 23, 2026, listed word for word in search_log.txt. Most were limited to the central government purchasing site (ccgp.gov.cn) or to universities' own sites (edu.cn). They asked for award notices (中标公告), result notices (成交公告) and purchase notices (采购公告) with Nvidia model names (H100, H200, H20, A100, A800, L40S, RTX 4090, RTX 5090) or with home-made names (昇腾 Ascend, Atlas, 寒武纪 Cambricon, 海光 Hygon, 昆仑芯 Kunlun, 鲲鹏 Kunpeng).
- **Saving.** Results that looked like a purchase of GPU or AI hardware were saved: 69 addresses tried, 64 saved, 5 not saved (4 answered not found, 1 could not be reached). Not every result was opened: the choice was made from titles and summaries. No search form on any purchasing site was used.
- **Sorting.** A notice goes in table 1 if it names Nvidia or an Nvidia model. It goes in table 2 if it names a home-made accelerator or a Huawei Atlas machine as the thing bought, asks for AI hardware under the word 国产 (home-made), as the Institute of Automation's tender does, or, as Liaoning's tender does, requires the platform bought to support home-made AI chips that it names, beside foreign ones. It goes in table 3 if the notice itself says GPU, AI, training or accelerated computing and names no accelerator by maker or model, even where it asks for GPU or NPU cards; most of these give a server brand and model. 17 saved pages are in no table: 6 whose notice does not itself say GPU, AI, training or accelerated computing, 3 dated before 2024, 3 that buy no accelerator, 3 that hold no usable content, and 2 that are not notices and are used further down. They are listed with reasons in left_out.txt.
- **Counting.** Every count of the sample is computed by script from the table of notices (notices.txt) or from its saved pages, and every count of the later records from the table named beside it. The year is the year of the date on the page; where the saved copy carries no date of its own, it is the year of the project number (Southern University of Science and Technology's and the Beijing Institute of Technology Zhuhai campus's notices) or of the purchase notice the award answers (Tsinghua University's Atlas award). The later records are not added to the counts: they were found another way.
- **Buyers.** Each buyer is described only by what its name says (a university, a vocational college, a research institute, an agency). No saved page says whether a buyer is a public or state body, so the note does not say it.
- **From my general knowledge, not from the notices.** Where a notice writes a model without Nvidia's name (L40, A6000, RTX 4090, RTX 4090 D, RTX 5090, H100), that these are Nvidia models is my knowledge; each such row says the maker is not written, except Nantong University's, whose notice names the card's board maker, 微星 (MSI, also my knowledge), and not Nvidia. That Huawei's Atlas machines run on Huawei's Ascend chips is also my knowledge; one notice writes the two names together [3].

## Later records, outside the sample

These are not in the sample or its count. They were found by later searches, and the project's table of purchase records (tables/procurement_records.tsv) lists them. Each is quoted here from the saved page.

- **China Mobile, 2025: an inference purchase in six lots.** C114, a telecoms trade site, reported on Aug 19, 2025 that China Mobile's tender for AI inference servers had six lots: "标包1、标包2、标包3和标包4均为类CUDA生态设备，总含税价超17亿元；标包5和标包6为CANN生态设备，总含税价约34亿元" (lots 1 to 4 are equipment of the CUDA-like kind, over 1.7 billion yuan with tax; lots 5 and 6 are equipment of the CANN kind, about 3.4 billion yuan) [55]. The page does not say whose chips the CUDA-like lots hold. Kunlunxin, a chip designer that calls itself an AI chip company China Mobile has invested in, "作为中国移动投资的人工智能芯片企业", says on its own site that in this purchase's CUDA-like section its products took the largest share in three lots, "在三个标包中均取得份额第一" [56]: a maker's own claim. China Mobile's own notices for this purchase are held only as a tender site carries them, with the candidates' names masked (see “What was not found”).
- **China Mobile, March 2026: a tender for CANN equipment only.** C114, Mar 17, 2026: "采购产品为人工智能超节点设备（CANN生态），采购规模约为6208卡（776套计算节点）。" (the product to be bought is AI supernode equipment, of the CANN ecosystem, about 6,208 cards in 776 computing nodes) [57]. ## Three details in the notices

- **Nvidia cards under a no-imports line.** Two notices that name Nvidia cards also refuse imports. The University of Chinese Academy of Sciences asked for servers with "8张Nvidia A800 /A100 80GB GPU" and wrote "本项目不接受进口" (this project does not accept imports) [20]. The notices do not say how a line refusing imports, or a home-made server brand, fits with the Nvidia cards they name.
- **An evaluated processor, an open accelerator.** A Liaoning university's tender requires processors that passed the security-and-reliability evaluation of China's information-security evaluation centre, "需通过中国信息安全测评中心安全可靠测评", and leaves the maker of the accelerators open: "配置不少于8颗GPU/NPU" (fit no fewer than 8 GPU or NPU chips) [36]. The 2023 purchase rule for general-purpose servers asks that evaluation of the processor (CPU) and of the operating system, and names no accelerator: "应当将CPU、操作系统符合安全可靠测评要求纳入采购需求" (the purchase requirement shall include that the CPU and the operating system meet the security-and-reliability evaluation) [70]. It binds Party and government bodies at township level and above, "乡镇以上党政机关", and two kinds of public institution tied to them, and says "其他单位可不在采购需求中提出此项要求" (other units may leave this requirement out) [70].
- **A buyer's reasons, in its own words.** Wuhan University chose an A100 card over an A800 offered at nearly the same price because "A100显卡比A800显卡性能更强大" (the A100 card performs better than the A800) [13].

## What a reader may and may not conclude

- **May.** That Chinese universities and research institutes published notices naming Nvidia hardware in 2024, 2025 and 2026, and that two universities posted notices in July 2026 to buy the RTX PRO 6000D: these are their own documents. That some of the largest purchases of AI hardware in the sample were made under project names that say home-made. That many award notices name no chip. That, by a trade site's accounts of its notices, China Mobile put about 3.4 of over 5 billion yuan of a 2025 purchase on CANN-ecosystem equipment, and asked in a March 2026 tender for such equipment only.
- **May not.** Any share, ratio or trend between Nvidia and home-made hardware: this is a sample found by search. That a notice to buy was a purchase made, or that a card named in a notice was delivered. How the cards reached China, or whether any sale met United States export rules: no notice says. Anything about China's AI companies: none of their purchases is in the sample. Anything about bodies whose purchases are not published.
