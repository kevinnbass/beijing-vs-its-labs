## The question

Idea 35 asked for a census of tenders: how many name Nvidia hardware and how many name home-made chips, from 2024 to 2026. A true census is not possible here. China's purchase notices sit on thousands of sites, many notices never name the chip, and the time was a few hours. What follows is a bounded sample with a stated method, and, apart from it, later records that another agent of the project found.

## What a purchase record can and cannot show

- **Can show.** That a named buyer asked for, or chose, a named product, in a stated number, at a stated budget or price, on a stated date and under stated conditions, in its own words. A tender, purchase notice or purchase filing shows what was asked for. A result or award notice, or a list of bid candidates, shows a contract decision.
- **Cannot show.** That the goods were delivered, paid for or put to use. How imported chips reached China, or whether a sale met United States export rules: no notice says. What share of all purchases went to Nvidia or to home-made hardware: many notices name no chip, and the notices here were found by search. Whose chips are in machines that a tender describes only by a software ecosystem (CUDA-like, CANN), unless the page says. And a trade site's account of a tender is not the tender itself.

## Method

- **Start.** The project held five Reuters reports that rest on tender documents and purchase records, and one university's own notice. They are set out in the section on what the project already held.
- **Searches.** {{N_SEARCHES}} web searches on Nov 23, 2026, listed word for word in search_log.txt. Most were limited to the central government purchasing site (ccgp.gov.cn) or to universities' own sites (edu.cn). They asked for award notices (中标公告), result notices (成交公告) and purchase notices (采购公告) with Nvidia model names (H100, H200, H20, A100, A800, L40S, RTX 4090, RTX 5090) or with home-made names (昇腾 Ascend, Atlas, 寒武纪 Cambricon, 海光 Hygon, 昆仑芯 Kunlun, 鲲鹏 Kunpeng).
- **Saving.** Results that looked like a purchase of GPU or AI hardware were saved with the project's fetcher: {{N_TRIED}} addresses tried, {{N_SAVED}} saved, {{N_FAILED}} not saved ({{N_404}} answered not found, {{N_NOCONN}} could not be reached). Not every result was opened: the choice was made from titles and summaries. No search form on any purchasing site was used.
- **Sorting.** A notice goes in table 1 if it names Nvidia or an Nvidia model. It goes in table 2 if it names a home-made accelerator or a Huawei Atlas machine, or asks for AI hardware under the word 国产 (home-made). It goes in table 3 if the notice itself says GPU, AI, training or accelerated computing and names no accelerator by maker or model; most of these give a server brand and model. {{N_LEFT}} saved pages are in no table: {{L_NOTAI}} whose notice does not itself say GPU, AI, training or accelerated computing, {{L_BEFORE}} dated before 2024, {{L_NOACC}} that buy no accelerator, {{L_NOCONTENT}} that hold no usable content, and {{L_ELSE}} that are not notices and are used further down. They are listed with reasons in left_out.txt.
- **Counting.** The counts come from a script (build.py) over the table file (notices.txt). The year is the year of the date on the page. The later records are not added to the counts: they were found another way.
- **Buyers.** Each buyer is described only by what its name says (a university, a vocational college, a research institute, an agency). No saved page says whether a buyer is a public or state body, so the note does not say it.
- **From my general knowledge, not from the notices.** Where a notice writes a model without its maker (L40, A6000, RTX 4090, RTX 5090, H100), that these are Nvidia models is my knowledge; each such row says the maker is not written. That Huawei's Atlas machines run on Huawei's Ascend chips is also my knowledge; one notice writes the two names together [cn-o35-buaa-50096].

## Three details in the notices

- **Nvidia cards under a no-imports line.** Two notices that name Nvidia cards also refuse imports. The University of Chinese Academy of Sciences asked for servers with "8张Nvidia A800 /A100 80GB GPU" and wrote "本项目不接受进口" (this project does not accept imports) [cn-o35-ccgp-ucas-a100-202409]. Southern University of Science and Technology awarded the purchase of a server with "8 块 VID Nvidia L40" under the line "是否接受进口 拒绝进口" (imports accepted or not: refused) [cn-o35-sustech-00064]. A third asks for a "国产服务器品牌" (home-made server brand) fitted with "NVDIA RTX A6000" cards [cn-o35-whu-75481]. The notices do not say how the two fit together.
- **An evaluated processor, an open accelerator.** A Liaoning university's tender requires processors that passed the security-and-reliability evaluation of China's information-security evaluation centre, "需通过中国信息安全测评中心安全可靠测评", and leaves the maker of the accelerators open: "配置不少于8颗GPU/NPU" (fit no fewer than 8 GPU or NPU chips) [cn-o35-ccgp-lnust-202511]. The 2023 purchase rule for general-purpose servers names the same two things and no accelerator: "应当将CPU、操作系统符合安全可靠测评要求纳入采购需求" (the purchase requirement shall include that the CPU and the operating system meet the security-and-reliability evaluation) [cn-policy-server-procurement-2023].
- **A buyer's reasons, in its own words.** Wuhan University chose an A100 card over an A800 offered at nearly the same price because "A100显卡比A800显卡性能更强大" (the A100 card performs better than the A800) [cn-o35-whu-6885].

## What a reader may and may not conclude

- **May.** That Chinese universities and research institutes published notices naming Nvidia hardware in 2024, 2025 and 2026, and that two universities posted notices in July 2026 to buy the RTX PRO 6000D: these are their own documents. That some of the largest purchases of AI hardware in the sample were made under project names that say home-made. That many award notices name no chip. That, by a trade site's accounts of its notices, China Mobile put about 3.4 of over 5 billion yuan of a 2025 purchase, and all of a March 2026 tender, on CANN-ecosystem equipment.
- **May not.** Any share, ratio or trend between Nvidia and home-made hardware: this is a sample found by search. That a notice to buy was a purchase made, or that a card named in a notice was delivered. How the cards reached China, or whether any sale met United States export rules: no notice says. Whose chips are in China Mobile's CUDA-like lots, beyond one chip maker's own claim to the largest share of three of them. Anything about China's AI companies: none of their purchases is in the sample. Anything about bodies whose purchases are not published.
