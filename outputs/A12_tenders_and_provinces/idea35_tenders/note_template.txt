## The question

How many published purchase notices of 2024 to 2026 name Nvidia hardware, and how many name home-made chips? A true census is not possible here. China's purchase notices sit on thousands of sites, many notices never name the chip, and the searches for the sample were made on one day. What follows is a bounded sample with a stated method and, apart from it, later records that later searches found.

## What a purchase record can and cannot show

- **Can show.** That a named buyer asked for, or chose, a named product, in a stated number, at a stated budget or price, on a stated date and under stated conditions, in its own words. A tender, purchase notice or purchase filing shows what was asked for. A result or award notice shows a contract decision; a result notice of a proposed award (拟成交) and a list of bid candidates (中标候选人) name the bidders proposed to win, before the award.
- **Cannot show.** That the goods were delivered, paid for or put to use. The notices give no shipping or import history and make no determination under United States export rules. What share of all purchases went to Nvidia or to home-made hardware: many notices name no chip, and the notices here were found by search. Whose chips are in machines that a tender describes only by a software ecosystem (CUDA-like, CANN), unless the page says. And a trade site's account of a tender is not the tender itself.

## Method

- **Start.** Before the Nov 23, 2026 sample searches, the project held reports on tender documents and project plans, and a notice from Beijing Institute of Technology. Northwestern Polytechnical University's own notice was added on Nov 24 and appears with the later records.
- **Searches.** {{N_SEARCHES}} web searches on Nov 23, 2026, listed word for word in search_log.txt. Most were limited to the central government purchasing site (ccgp.gov.cn) or to universities' own sites (edu.cn). They asked for award notices (中标公告), result notices (成交公告) and purchase notices (采购公告) with Nvidia model names (H100, H200, H20, A100, A800, L40S, RTX 4090, RTX 5090) or with home-made names (昇腾 Ascend, Atlas, 寒武纪 Cambricon, 海光 Hygon, 昆仑芯 Kunlun, 鲲鹏 Kunpeng).
- **Saving.** Results that looked like a purchase of GPU or AI hardware were saved: {{N_TRIED}} addresses tried, {{N_SAVED}} saved, {{N_FAILED}} not saved ({{N_404}} answered not found, {{N_NOCONN}} could not be reached). Not every result was opened: the choice was made from titles and summaries. No search form on any purchasing site was used.
- **Sorting.** A notice goes in table 1 if it names Nvidia or an Nvidia model. It goes in table 2 if it names a home-made accelerator or a Huawei Atlas machine as the thing bought, asks for AI hardware under the word 国产 (home-made), as the Institute of Automation's tender does, or, as Liaoning's tender does, requires the platform bought to support home-made AI chips that it names, beside foreign ones. It goes in table 3 if the notice itself says GPU, AI, training or accelerated computing and names no accelerator by maker or model, even where it asks for GPU or NPU cards; most of these give a server brand and model. {{N_LEFT}} saved pages are in no table: {{L_NOTAI}} whose notice does not itself say GPU, AI, training or accelerated computing, {{L_BEFORE}} dated before 2024, {{L_NOACC}} that buy no accelerator, {{L_NOCONTENT}} that hold no usable content, and {{L_ELSE}} that are not notices and are used further down. They are listed with reasons in left_out.txt.
- **Counting.** Every count of the sample is computed by script from the table of notices (notices.txt) or from its saved pages, and every count of the later records from the table named beside it. The year is the year of the date on the page; where the saved copy carries no date of its own, it is the year of the project number (Southern University of Science and Technology's and the Beijing Institute of Technology Zhuhai campus's notices) or of the purchase notice the award answers (Tsinghua University's Atlas award). The later records are not added to the counts: they were found another way.
- **Buyers.** Each buyer is described only by what its name says (a university, a vocational college, a research institute, an agency).
- **From my general knowledge, not from the notices.** Where a notice writes a model without Nvidia's name (L40, A6000, RTX 4090, RTX 4090 D, RTX 5090, H100), that these are Nvidia models is my knowledge; each such row says the maker is not written, except Nantong University's, whose notice names the card's board maker, 微星 (MSI, also my knowledge), and not Nvidia. That Huawei's Atlas machines run on Huawei's Ascend chips is also my knowledge; one notice writes the two names together [cn-o35-buaa-50096].

## Three details in the notices

- **Nvidia cards under a no-imports line.** Two notices that name Nvidia cards also refuse imports. The University of Chinese Academy of Sciences asked for servers with "8张Nvidia A800 /A100 80GB GPU" and wrote "本项目不接受进口" (this project does not accept imports) [cn-o35-ccgp-ucas-a100-202409]. Southern University of Science and Technology awarded the purchase of a server with "8 块 VID Nvidia L40" under the line "是否接受进口 拒绝进口" (imports accepted or not: refused) [cn-o35-sustech-00064]. Outside the sample, the Song County Public Security Bureau's December 2025 tender lists "至少8 块NVIDIA T4-16G 高性能运算卡" (at least eight high-performance NVIDIA T4-16G cards) in a server specification and states "是否接受进口产品：否" (imported products accepted: no). It lets bidders offer equipment of the same grade or better if it meets the technical and performance requirements [cn-ga31-evaltgov-2]. Another notice in the sample asks for a "国产服务器品牌" (home-made server brand) fitted with "NVDIA RTX A6000" cards [cn-o35-whu-75481]. The notices do not say how a line refusing imports, or a home-made server brand, fits with the Nvidia cards they name.

- **An evaluated processor, an open accelerator.** A Liaoning university's tender requires processors that passed the security-and-reliability evaluation of China's information-security evaluation centre, "需通过中国信息安全测评中心安全可靠测评", and leaves the maker of the accelerators open: "配置不少于8颗GPU/NPU" (fit no fewer than 8 GPU or NPU chips) [cn-o35-ccgp-lnust-202511]. The 2023 purchase rule for general-purpose servers asks that evaluation of the processor (CPU) and of the operating system, and names no accelerator: "应当将CPU、操作系统符合安全可靠测评要求纳入采购需求" (the purchase requirement shall include that the CPU and the operating system meet the security-and-reliability evaluation) [cn-policy-server-procurement-2023]. It binds Party and government bodies at township level and above, "乡镇以上党政机关", and two kinds of public institution tied to them, and says "其他单位可不在采购需求中提出此项要求" (other units may leave this requirement out) [cn-policy-server-procurement-2023].
- **A buyer's reasons, in its own words.** Wuhan University chose an A100 card over an A800 offered at nearly the same price because "A100显卡比A800显卡性能更强大" (the A100 card performs better than the A800) [cn-o35-whu-6885].

## What a reader may and may not conclude

- **May.** That Chinese universities and research institutes published notices naming Nvidia hardware in 2024, 2025 and 2026, and that two universities posted notices in July 2026 to buy the RTX PRO 6000D: these are their own documents. That some of the largest awards in the sample included Huawei machines under project names that say home-made. That many award notices name no chip. That, by a trade site's accounts of its notices, China Mobile put about 3.4 of over 5 billion yuan of a 2025 purchase on CANN-ecosystem equipment, and asked in a March 2026 tender for such equipment only.
- **May not.** Any share, ratio or trend between Nvidia and home-made hardware: this is a sample found by search. That a notice to buy was a purchase made, or that a card named in a notice was delivered. The notices give no account of shipping or of compliance with United States export rules. Whose chips are in China Mobile's CUDA-like lots, beyond one chip maker's own claim to the largest share of three of them. Anything about China's AI companies: none of their purchases is in the sample. Anything about bodies whose purchases are not published.

## The files

- notices.txt: the table as typed, one line per notice. notices.tsv: the same with the address and the saved id of every notice. counts.tsv: the count. quotes.tsv: every string taken from a page. left_out.txt: saved pages not used, with reasons. search_log.txt: the searches. refs_manual.tsv: references that are not sample notices. build.py remakes the tables, the counts and this note from these files, and stops if any string taken from a page is not found in that page's saved copy.
