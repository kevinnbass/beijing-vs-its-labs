## The question

Reuters reported on Nov 5, 2026: "About 600,000 of Nvidia's advanced artificial intelligence chips are stored in Hong Kong awaiting shipment to China", on the word of "two people familiar with the matter" [cn-clips-evidence-1]. The same report says: "Several major Chinese internet companies have each obtained about 10,000 H200 chips in the past few months" [cn-clips-evidence-1]. Both numbers rest on unnamed people. This note asks whether any public, official trade figure bears on how many such chips entered mainland China in 2025 and 2026. (Nov 5 is Reuters's dateline, which stands on the copy Yahoo carries; the copy quoted here is CNA's page, which shows only its own date, Nov 6, 2026.)

## The answer in short

- China's customs publishes monthly import values for integrated circuits, for computer processing units and for computer parts. No public table found here goes below those broad lines.
- No customs line can count Nvidia chips. Each line holds every maker's goods, imports are recorded by country of origin, and no saved page says under which line these chips are declared, in China or anywhere else.
- China's imports of computer parts (line 8473.30) were 82.2 billion US dollars in January to September 2026, against 18.9 billion in the same months of 2025, while their weight fell. The line can show that China paid far more for computer parts, and far more for each tonne of them. It cannot show what the parts were or who made them.
- Singapore's re-exports to mainland China under one line for computer units (line 84718010, control and adaptor units) were about 18 million Singapore dollars in January to June 2026 and about 3.0 billion in July to October 2026. These figures are a reading from Singapore's statistics service, kept on file with their hashes; they are not a saved source. The line can show that goods so declared left Singapore for the mainland. It cannot show what they were, who made them or who bought them.
- One mirror is tied to these chips by rule: licensed H200s must be inspected in the United States before they are shipped. US exports to mainland China, Hong Kong and Macau under the three headings an American proclamation names for such chips never passed 293 million dollars in a month to August 2026.
- Over January to August 2026 those exports came to 1,688.6 million dollars. That is room for a few tens of thousands of chips at most (about 62,500), but only if a chip is valued at about 27,000 dollars, a price Reuters reported from unnamed people, and only for goods the United States recorded as leaving for those three places under those three headings; the same figures would hold 600,000 chips only at about 2,800 dollars a chip. September and October 2026, the months that matter most, were not yet available on the one route open to us.
- Nvidia's own figures, on the record, are of the same order for the months that overlap: under 1% of 89.0 billion dollars in the quarter to Jul 26, 2026 [man-ms-sec-10q-2026-07]. Its 2.4 billion dollars of licensed sales to China falls in the quarter to Oct 25, 2026 [cn-o38-nvda-q3fy27-call-transcript-pdf], mostly after the months the trade figures cover. The ceiling and the 2.4 billion cannot be set against each other.
- These are different measures: different countries' records, tariff lines, currencies and months. This note sets them side by side and adds none of them together.

## 1. Which tariff heading these chips fall under

1. **A US document names three headings.** The President's proclamation of Jan 14, 2026, which put a 25% duty on certain advanced chips, defines the goods as products "classifiable in the provisions of the HTSUS enumerated in this subdivision" and lists three headings of the US tariff: 8471.50, 8471.80 and 8473.30 [cn-o34-us232-proclamation-fr-pi]. The goods must be a "logic integrated circuit, or an article that contains a logic integrated circuit" within set performance limits [cn-o34-us232-proclamation-fr-pi]. The White House's fact sheet says the duty falls on "certain advanced computing chips, such as the NVIDIA H200 and AMD MI325X" [cn-o34-us232-factsheet-wh]. Nvidia's quarterly report says a duty of the same rate reaches its licensed chips: "any H200s shipped under the new licensing program are subject to a 25% tariff upon importation into the United States" [man-ms-sec-10q-2026-07]. If that is the proclamation's duty, as the rate and the fact sheet indicate, the H200 enters the United States under one of those three headings. Which of the three is not stated.

## 4. Why these figures cannot show H200 imports

11. **The chips are a small part of a broad line.** Reuters gave the price in January 2026: H200 chips "priced at around $27,000 each" [cn-acts-reuters-customs-20260114]. That is a press figure from unnamed people. At that price 10,000 chips are 270 million dollars (our multiplication). That is 0.7% of the parts line in the third quarter of 2026, and 0.38% of one month's imports of integrated circuits. A delivery of that size cannot be seen.

12. **Imports are counted by origin, not by route.** The notes say: "The country of origin is recorded for imports." [cn-o34-gacc-explanatory-2024] Reuters says of the H200: "The chip is manufactured by Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company (TSMC)" [cn-labs-h200-lobby]. A chip made in Taiwan that arrives by way of Hong Kong or the United States would be counted as an import from Taiwan. No line shows goods arriving from Hong Kong as such. Table 16 bears this out: imports recorded as of Hong Kong origin were 12.5 million dollars under chapter 84 and 21.1 million under chapter 85 in September 2026 [cn-o34-gacc-t16-imports-bycountry-202609]. Hong Kong's own figures put its exports of computer parts (8473.30) to the mainland at 6.4 billion dollars in July 2026 alone [cn-o34-comtrade-m-202607].
!! cn-o34-gacc-t16-imports-bycountry-202609 | 12,545 114,827
!! cn-o34-gacc-t16-imports-bycountry-202609 | 21,088 164,608
!! cn-o34-comtrade-m-202607 | "primaryValue": 6449754920.706

13. **Chips held in Hong Kong are in no mainland figure.** Hong Kong's statistics office says: "The Hong Kong Special Administrative Region is a separate customs territory" [cn-le18-hk-csd-410-50031]. The mainland's statistics count goods when they enter its customs territory, and that includes "goods from abroad entering customs warehouses, bonded areas or special economic zones" [cn-o34-gacc-explanatory-2024]. So a chip counts as imported once it enters a bonded zone on the mainland, even before it reaches a buyer, and "Imports are recorded on the date when the goods are released from customs" [cn-o34-gacc-explanatory-2024].

14. **Some chips are reported to have come by other routes.** Reuters: "small quantities of chips had been shipped via intermediaries and indirect routes" [cn-clips-evidence-1]. A customs table cannot be expected to show such goods under their own name.

## 5. The mirror that does bear on it: US exports

15. **Licensed H200s must leave from the United States.** Nvidia told the US securities regulator: "The licenses require that the H200s go through an inspection process in the United States prior to any shipment to the customer." [man-ms-sec-10q-2026-07] The Commerce Department's rule of Jan 15, 2026 sets its new policy for "exports from the United States destined to China or Macau", says "the testing must be conducted in the customs territory of the United States", and for shipments from other countries says "the licensing policy remains a presumption of denial" [cn-o34-bis-rule-20260115]. The laboratory may test "a representative sampling of a batch" [cn-o34-bis-rule-20260115], but Nvidia's words above are that any H200 shipped under the programme is imported into the United States first. So every licensed H200 should appear in the United States' export figures, under the place the United States recorded it as leaving for.

16. **The route to the figures.** The US Census Bureau is the statistics office that publishes US trade figures. Its data service answered our request with: "A valid key must be included with each data API request." [cn-o34-census-exports-hs6-cn-hk-mo] A key needs a sign-up, so we did not use it. UN Comtrade's public preview gives the figures the United States reports, one month per request. We saved the 20 months from January 2025 to August 2026 for four headings. The answers carry code numbers only. 18. **The ceiling, and what it rests on.** The price used here is a press figure from unnamed people: "priced at around $27,000 each" [cn-acts-reuters-customs-20260114]. Suppose every dollar under those headings in January to August 2026 were an H200 at that price: 1,688.6 million dollars divided by 27,000 dollars is about 62,500 chips (our script's division; every step is in derived_figures.tsv). The ceiling moves with the price. The eight months would hold 600,000 chips only if a chip were declared at about 2,800 dollars, a tenth of the press price. It is also a loose ceiling: the same headings carried 151 million dollars a month in 2025, before any licence; Nvidia dates the first licences with the words "Beginning in February 2026" [man-ms-sec-10q-2026-07]. And it covers only goods the United States recorded as leaving for mainland China, Hong Kong or Macau. Goods recorded as leaving for another place first are outside it. Singapore is the one such place looked at here (point 24): with the United States' exports to Singapore under the same three headings added, 2,558.6 million dollars in the eight months, the same division gives about 157,300 chips (our arithmetic), again with every kind of goods counted as if it were an H200. At the press price 600,000 chips would be 16.2 billion dollars, 9.6 times everything the United States sent to the three places under these headings in those eight months.

Nvidia said on its results call of May 20, 2026: "While the US government has approved licenses for H200 to be shipped to China-based customers, we have yet to generate any revenue, and we are uncertain whether any imports will be allowed into the country." [cn-o38-nvda-q1fy27-call-transcript-pdf] Its report for the quarter to Jul 26, 2026 says: "Shipments of Data Center Hopper products to China during the second quarter of fiscal year 2027 were less than 1% of Data Center revenue", and "Data Center revenue was $89.0 billion" [man-ms-sec-10q-2026-07]. The two do not cover the same days: the customs figures run from January to August 2026, Nvidia's quarter from late April to Jul 26, 2026. ## 6. Other places' own figures

## What a reader may conclude

- No public customs table found here can confirm or refute that about 10,000 H200s reached each of several buyers, or any other count of these chips. Each line holds every maker's goods, and no saved page says under which line the chips are declared.
- To the end of August 2026 the United States' own export figures leave room for a few tens of thousands of licensed H200s at most (about 62,500), but only if a chip is valued at the press price of about 27,000 dollars, was declared under the three named headings, and was recorded as leaving for mainland China, Hong Kong or Macau. Nvidia's own report gives a bound of the same order for its quarter to Jul 26, 2026, at the same price, and an official called exports "minimal" on Jul 14, 2026 [man-a7d2f1a9f963].
- Nvidia's 2.4 billion dollars of licensed sales to China belongs to the quarter to Oct 25, 2026, mostly after the months the trade figures cover. The two cannot be set against each other.
- In January to September 2026 China imported 4.35 times as much computer parts by value as in the same months of 2025, and less by weight. That is a fact about a broad class of goods of every maker.
- Singapore recorded about 3.0 billion Singapore dollars of re-exports to mainland China under one line for computer units in July to October 2026, against about 18 million in the first half (a reading, not a saved source). That is a fact about goods under one line, of makers the tables do not name.

## What a reader may not conclude

- That the rise in computer parts is Nvidia chips, or H200s. The table does not say what it is.
- That Singapore's line is Nvidia's 2.4 billion dollars, or any number of H200s. The two figures are of different kinds, and Singapore's tables name no goods and no maker.
- That any two of these series can be added or chained. They are different countries' records, under different lines, in different currencies and months.
- That the 600,000 is wrong. That report is from November 2026 and is about Hong Kong; the US figures end in August 2026, and they would not show chips that reached Hong Kong some other way.
- That the ceiling is a count of chips. It is a sum of dollars divided by a press price, and it moves with that price.
- How many H200s entered mainland China. No figure found here states it.

## What would settle more

- The US figures for September and October 2026, when UN Comtrade or the Census Bureau's service has them. A step of billions of dollars to mainland China or Hong Kong under 8471.50, 8471.80 or 8473.30 would be the first official trace of large shipments.
- A hand query of China's customs service at stats.customs.gov.cn for the eight-digit lines under 8473.30 and 8471.50, by month and by origin. Even that would show a class of goods, not a product.
- Singapore's tables for November 2026 and after, and a saved copy of them: they would show whether the line stays high, not what the goods are.
- Nvidia's quarterly report for the quarter to Oct 25, 2026. On Nov 17, 2026 Nvidia said: "we plan to file our 10-Q in the coming weeks" [cn-o38-nvda-q3fy27-call-transcript-pdf]. The Securities and Exchange Commission's record of Nvidia's filings, fetched on Nov 23, 2026, lists no report for that quarter; the newest quarterly report in it is the one for the quarter to Jul 26, 2026 [cn-le05-sec-submissions-20261123].
