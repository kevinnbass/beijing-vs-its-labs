# Does any public record bear out Reuters's report of about 600,000 chips stored in Hong Kong?

@deck None was found, and none that refutes it. With a chip valued at the press price, Hong Kong's trade figures by heading (to July 2026) show no inflow of that size from the United States or under the line usually read as Taiwan, its monthly totals for office machines and computers (to September 2026) show no lasting change in the balance of trade, and Taiwan's exports to Hong Kong (to October 2026) are far smaller; none of these figures can pick out a chip. No government confirmation was found. Reuters's count rests on two people it does not name.

## What Reuters reported

Reuters, Nov 5, 2026: about 600,000 of Nvidia's H200 chips "are stored in Hong Kong awaiting shipment to mainland China, two people familiar with the matter said, as Beijing withholds approval for large-scale imports" [cn-clips-reuters-hongkong-yahoo]. Reuters calls this "The size of the stockpile of H200 chips, which has not previously been reported" [cn-clips-reuters-hongkong-yahoo]. The report does not say whose chips it counts, where in Hong Kong it places them, or how, if it is right, they came there. The Nov 5 dateline stands on the copy Yahoo carries; CNA's page of the same report shows only its own date, Nov 6, 2026.

Palmer Luckey gave the same number in a clip posted on Nov 19, 2026: "There's 600,000 H200 GPUs sitting in a port right now", because "China will not give them an import permit" [clip-luckey]. Those words are from a machine transcript of the clip (0:18 to 0:30); nobody has checked them by ear. Both phrases say more than the report his number matches. Reuters's words are "stored in Hong Kong", not in a port, and that Beijing "withholds approval for large-scale imports" [cn-clips-reuters-hongkong-yahoo]. The project's argument records that no permit rule for these chips was found in China's import lists read, and no decision naming them under the trade law's power to restrict goods outside those lists (its point 18).

## The answer in short

- No official figure or statement was found that confirms Reuters's count. Reuters asked Hong Kong's government and got no reply. China's foreign ministry, asked about the report, referred the question elsewhere.
- No second news outlet with sources of its own for the count was found. The pages that carry it relay Reuters.
- Hong Kong's own trade figures by tariff heading were available to July 2026. At the press price of about 27,000 dollars a chip the reported count would be worth about 16 billion dollars. From February to July 2026 the figures show no inflow of that size from the United States or under the partner line usually read as Taiwan, and under the headings an American proclamation names for such chips exports ran ahead of imports in every month; that cannot show whether a stock of any one product grew. One gap: by the statistics office's older guides, goods passing through on a through bill of lading or air waybill need no declaration, so they would be in no Hong Kong figure.
- For August and September 2026 Hong Kong's figures could be reached only as broad divisions. Imports of office machines and computers jumped in August 2026 and exports jumped in September. Over the two months together, exports ran ahead of imports by about as much as in two average months of the year, so the division's balance shows no lasting change to the end of September. It cannot say what came and went, or whether a stock of any one product grew, and October was not yet there.
- Taiwan's own figures run to October 2026. They show 4.69 billion US dollars of computers and computer parts sent to Hong Kong in ten months, every maker's goods together, far below what 600,000 chips would be worth at the press price. Its figure for integrated circuits is far too large to tell anything.
- Two statements on the record touch the report without confirming it: Nvidia says much of its product is warehoused in and distributed from Hong Kong, and it booked a charge for excess H200 inventory and purchase obligations in the half year to Jul 26, 2026. Neither says where any H200 stock is kept. Nvidia's own figure for licensed sales of Hopper data-centre products to China in the quarter to Oct 25, 2026 is 2.4 billion dollars; at the press price the reported 600,000 chips would be worth nearly seven times that.
- No customs line read here, in Hong Kong or elsewhere, can count Nvidia chips: each is a total for a kind of goods that names no maker, and no saved page says under which line an importer in China or Hong Kong declares these chips. Nvidia's own list gives only the codes Nvidia applies itself (point 4).

## 1. How large Reuters's count is

1. **About 16 billion US dollars.** Reuters gave a price in January 2026: H200 chips "priced at around $27,000 each" [cn-acts-reuters-customs-20260114]. That is a press figure from unnamed people. At that price 600,000 chips are 16.2 billion US dollars (our multiplication), or about 126 billion Hong Kong dollars at 7.8 to the US dollar (the rate is from my general knowledge of Hong Kong's peg, not from a saved page). Every comparison of the 600,000 with a dollar figure in this note rests on that price; the trade figures set beside one another do not.

2. **Far more than Nvidia says it sold to China under licence.** Nvidia's own figures are on the record. For the quarter to Jul 26, 2026: "Shipments of Data Center Hopper products to China during the second quarter of fiscal year 2027 were less than 1% of Data Center revenue", and "Data Center revenue was $89.0 billion" [man-ms-sec-10q-2026-07]. For the quarter to Oct 25, 2026: "Q3 sales of licensed Hopper Data Center products to China was $2.4 billion" [cn-o38-nvda-q3fy27-call-transcript-pdf]. Before those two quarters it had no revenue under the H200 licences: on May 20, 2026 Nvidia said of them "we have yet to generate any revenue" [cn-o38-nvda-q1fy27-call-transcript-pdf]. The two quarters together are under 3.3 billion dollars (our arithmetic). At the press price of 27,000 dollars a chip, 2.4 billion dollars would be about 89,000 chips, and 3.3 billion is about a fifth of what 600,000 chips would be worth (our divisions; price-based equivalents, not counts, since Hopper products may be more than H200s alone). So, at the press price, Nvidia's own figures for licensed Hopper shipments and sales to China to Oct 25, 2026 come to about a fifth of the reported count's value. The report does not say whether the chips it counts had been sold, and no saved page says whether Nvidia counts a delivery to Hong Kong as a shipment to China.

## 2. Would Hong Kong's trade figures show a stock of the size Reuters reported?

3. **Only as a change in broad totals, and with one gap.** No Hong Kong figure names a product or a maker, so the most its figures could show is a change in the totals under a heading. Hong Kong's statistics office says of its figures: "Import/export declaration is also required of Hong Kong's trade with Chinese Mainland, and statistics relating to this are included in Hong Kong's external merchandise trade statistics." [cn-le18-hk-csd-410-50031] So goods that leave Hong Kong for the mainland are counted as exports. Hong Kong's answers to a United Nations questionnaire on how it compiles trade figures say: "Hong Kong is a free port." [cn-o42-un-hk-trade-practice] They also say there are no bonded warehouses, that the general trade system is used, and that goods merely passing through are left out. The page is old: its date of release is Apr 7, 2003. If that still holds, goods brought into Hong Kong and stored there are counted as imports when they arrive and as exports when they leave. The gap is goods in passage. Hong Kong's statistics office lists "transhipment cargo consigned on a through bill of lading" or a through air waybill among the goods that need no declaration [cn-o42-hk-csd-declarations-guide]. Its slides for a United Nations workshop in December 2006 define the term as "any article that is brought into Hong Kong under a through bill of lading or a through air waybill from a place outside Hong Kong to another place outside Hong Kong", and say such goods are "exempted from declaration" [cn-o42-hk-csd-un-bangkok-slides]. Both documents are old, and whether the exemption still applies in this form was not established. Goods held in Hong Kong on through papers to the mainland would be in no Hong Kong trade figure. The report does not say on what papers, if any, the chips it counts would be held.

4. **Which headings.** A US proclamation of Jan 14, 2026 names three tariff headings for the advanced chips it covers: 8471.50, 8471.80 and 8473.30, which are units and parts of computers [cn-o34-us232-proclamation-fr-pi]. The White House's fact sheet names the H200 among those chips: "such as the NVIDIA H200 and AMD MI325X" [cn-o34-us232-factsheet-wh]. Nvidia's own list of the codes it applies itself, "provided for informational purposes only" [cn-o34-nvidia-export-page], gives 8471.80 for its HGX H200 parts, 8473.30 for its H200 NVL parts and 8471.50 for its DGX H200 systems [cn-ga11-nvidia-eccn-list-20261124]. Neither the proclamation nor the list says under which line an importer in Hong Kong declares the chips. The companion note on customs figures, which asks whether customs figures can count the H200 chips Reuters reported entering China, sets this out. Imports are also recorded by where the goods were made: Hong Kong answers "Country of origin." [cn-o42-un-hk-trade-practice] Reuters says of the H200: "The chip is manufactured by Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company (TSMC)" [cn-labs-h200-lobby]. A chip made in Taiwan and only inspected in the United States may be recorded as of Taiwan origin, so two partner lines are shown below: the United States, and the line usually read as Taiwan.

6. **From the United States or under Other Asia, nes: no inflow of that size, February to July 2026.** Reuters says "The United States barred exports of the H200 to China before" the permission of late 2025 [cn-clips-evidence-1]. The Commerce Department dates the President's announcement to "December 8, 2025" [cn-o34-bis-press-rule]. Nvidia dates the first licences with the words "Beginning in February 2026", and its report writes of these controls as covering "China (including Hong Kong and Macau)" [man-ms-sec-10q-2026-07]. So the months compared here are those from February 2026. From February to July 2026 Hong Kong's imports under these headings of United States origin and under Other Asia, nes together were 3.1 billion dollars (our sum), against the 16.2 billion dollars that 600,000 chips would be at the press price. The highest month of United States origin was March 2026, at 226.1 million dollars [cn-o34-comtrade-m-202603]. The large 2025 figure under Other Asia, nes in the table includes 4.1 billion dollars in March 2025 alone; that was before the H200 was allowed, and the figures do not say what the goods were. The United States' own export figures count something else, goods recorded as leaving the United States for Hong Kong, and they show no step of that size either: its exports to Hong Kong under the three headings came to 1,088.4 million dollars over the eight months January to August 2026 (our sum of eight monthly answers, the last of them [cn-o34-comtrade-m-202608]).

7. **Imports under these headings did rise, mostly from mainland China and South Korea.** The average month was 4.9 billion dollars in 2025 and 7.0 billion in January to July 2026. By origin, the average month went from 2.4 to 3.9 billion for mainland China, from 0.35 to 1.1 billion for South Korea and from 0.10 to 0.16 billion for the United States, and fell from 0.95 to 0.35 billion under Other Asia, nes (our averages, from the same monthly answers) [cn-o34-comtrade-m-202607].

8. **Exports ran ahead of imports in every month.** A stock building up under these headings would, other things equal, narrow the excess of exports over imports; other goods can offset it. Under these headings exports were larger in each of the 19 months, by 0.5 to 3.6 billion dollars a month [cn-o34-comtrade-m-202607]. The comparison is rough. The figures do not explain the gap, and it moves by a billion dollars or more from month to month. The totals can neither show nor rule out a stock of one product. Most of what left went to the mainland: of Hong Kong's total exports under the three headings, 65.9 billion dollars in January to July 2026, 48.1 billion went to the mainland (our sums). By heading, its exports to the mainland under 8473.30 were 16.46 billion dollars in January to July 2025 and 36.90 billion in January to July 2026; under 8471.80 they were 12.75 and 7.02 billion, and under 8471.50, 3.90 and 4.19 billion. These lines can show that computer parts and units of every maker went on from Hong Kong to the mainland in tens of billions of dollars. They cannot show whether any Nvidia chip was among them. Hong Kong's statistics office also publishes a quarterly table of selected products that gives parts and accessories of computer hardware on their own; it does not list the tariff codes it covers. In every quarter from January 2024 to June 2026 the total exports of these parts were larger than the imports, by 18,781 million Hong Kong dollars in the first quarter of 2026 and by 39,961 million in the second (our subtractions) [cn-le18-hk-csd-410-96001]. The third quarter of 2026 was not yet in the table on Nov 25, 2026.

11. **What that can and cannot tell.** At the press price, and at 7.8 Hong Kong dollars to the US dollar, 600,000 chips would be about 126 billion Hong Kong dollars: more than this division's imports in any month from January 2025 to September 2026. A stock building up inside the division would, other things equal, show as imports gaining on exports, though other goods can offset it. In August 2026 imports did gain: exports exceeded imports by 5,111 million Hong Kong dollars, against 28,302 million in July. In September the excess was 32,089 million. The two months together come to 37,200 million, about two average months of January to September 2026, at 19,270 million each (our arithmetic, from the monthly tables). So the narrowing of August was made up in September, and the division's balance shows no lasting change to the end of September 2026; it cannot show whether a stock of any one product grew. Over the whole of 2025 the average excess was 8,340 million a month: in 2026 exports ran further ahead of imports, not less. The division cannot say what the goods were. It holds every office machine and computer of every maker, the exports of a month are not the imports of that month, and goods held in passage are outside it (point 3). October 2026 was not in the tables. The office also warns: "The sums of the monthly figures may differ from the cumulative totals" [cn-le18-hk-csd-410-50031]. Hong Kong's statistics office offers a tool to build tables by single heading (read on its statistics page, not saved; that it reaches single headings is from my general knowledge). The tool did not open for this note (an HTTP 499 answer), so August and September 2026 are compared here only by broad divisions.

12. **What other places record as sent to Hong Kong.** Taiwan's finance ministry gives exports by destination for product groups, to October 2026 [cn-le18-tw-mof-exp-cnhk-groups]. Its exports to Hong Kong in January to October 2026 were 1.67 billion US dollars of computers (its group for computers holds all of heading 8471: the companion note on customs figures shows the test) and 3.02 billion of computer parts, 4.69 billion together; in July to October 2026, 2.13 billion together; in October alone, 604 million (our sums; each cell used was checked again in the saved page). That is every maker's goods, and far below the 16.2 billion dollars of point 1. Taiwan's group for integrated circuits is too large to tell anything: 60.4 billion dollars to Hong Kong in the ten months. Singapore's tables, which are a reading and not a saved source (the customs note says why), show re-exports to Hong Kong under one line for computer units, line 84718010, of about 23 million Singapore dollars in January to June 2026 and about 231 million in July to October; under the same line about 3.0 billion went to mainland China in those four months, not to Hong Kong. The United States' own exports to Hong Kong are in point 6. None of these shows an inflow of the size Reuters reported. None can rule one out: licensed chips must be inspected in the United States before they are shipped (point 17), the United States' figures here end in August 2026, and Hong Kong's own figures leave out goods in passage.

## 5. What else bears on it

**On the record**

16. **Nvidia says much of its product is warehoused in Hong Kong.** Nvidia told the US securities regulator that export controls may disrupt its supply chain "for a substantial portion of our products, which are warehoused in and distributed from Hong Kong" [man-ms-sec-10q-2026-07]. The filing gives no figure for what it holds there and names no product.

17. **Nvidia booked a charge for excess H200 inventory and purchase obligations.** The same report, for the quarter to Jul 26, 2026, says that "During the first half of fiscal year 2027, we incurred a $0.4 billion charge associated with H200 for excess inventory and purchase obligations" [man-ms-sec-10q-2026-07]; that half year ended on Jul 26, 2026. A charge is an accounting entry, not a count of chips held. It also says: "The licenses require that the H200s go through an inspection process in the United States prior to any shipment to the customer." [man-ms-sec-10q-2026-07] Neither sentence says where the stock was kept.

18. **Nvidia says it has stopped shipping, and gives no reason.** On its results call of Nov 17, 2026: "we are no longer shipping licensed Hopper Data Center products to China" [cn-o38-nvda-q3fy27-call-transcript-pdf]. Reuters wrote that Nvidia's then interim chief executive "did not explain why Nvidia had stopped shipping the processors or whether the halt would be permanent" [cn-acts-reuters-halt-20261118-relay2]. She told CNBC the same day that the licensed H200s "went to China within this last quarter", and: "we are no longer shipping H200 at all, let alone not shipping it to China" [cn-against2-kress-global-h200-halt-20261117]. That is from CNBC's own transcript, which it calls unofficial. Nvidia also made a statement, and two saved news pages carry its words. The Standard of Hong Kong, in a page stamped Nov 20, 2026, 04:57 Hong Kong time, has Nvidia say "in a statement" that the approvals were "subject to strict conditions and limited volumes" and that "despite receiving some licenses, we have since stopped shipping H200s to China" [cn-le04-standard-900000-20261120]. The Daily Caller, in a page stamped Nov 20, 2026, 20:29 UTC, about a day later, quotes an Nvidia spokesperson at more length (the page says the spokesperson spoke to the Daily Caller News Foundation): "The review and licensing process spanned more than half a year, with approvals subject to strict conditions and limited volumes." and "Despite receiving some licenses, we have already stopped shipping H200s to China." [cn-le04-dailycaller-20261120] Neither page says on which day the statement was made, and it gives no reason for the stop either. Of the quarter after, Nvidia's commentary says: "We are not assuming any Data Center compute revenue from China in our outlook." [cn-counter-b17-1] The words Hong Kong appear in neither the call's transcript nor the commentary (a word search; both do name China), so neither says where any H200 stock is held.

## What a reader may conclude

- The 600,000 is one report on the word of two unnamed people. Nothing official found here confirms it.
- From February to July 2026, Hong Kong's figures for the headings named for these chips show no inflow of that size of United States origin or under Other Asia, nes, with a chip valued at the press price of about 27,000 dollars. Its monthly divisions show no lasting change in the balance of computers traded to the end of September 2026, and Taiwan's figures show no flow of that size to Hong Kong to October 2026. None of these figures can pick out a chip.
- The United States' export figures to Hong Kong under those headings, which run to August 2026, show no step of that size either; its figures for September and October 2026 were not reached. Hong Kong's own figures leave out goods in passage on through papers (point 3), so they could not show goods held that way.
- By Nvidia's own account, its Hopper shipments and licensed sales to China in the two quarters to Oct 25, 2026 came to under 3.3 billion dollars [man-ms-sec-10q-2026-07] [cn-o38-nvda-q3fy27-call-transcript-pdf], and before them it had no revenue under the H200 licences [cn-o38-nvda-q1fy27-call-transcript-pdf]. At the press price that is about a fifth of what 600,000 chips would be worth.
- Nvidia says it warehouses much of its product in Hong Kong; it gives no figure for what it holds there and names no product.
- The Financial Times reported, as Reuters relayed it on Aug 18, 2026, that regulators told companies they could ship the chips to Hong Kong and use them there [cn-tolerate-reutersaug26-relay1-wayback]. That is a press report citing two unnamed people.

## What a reader may not conclude

- That the figure is confirmed, or that it is false. The months that could show it were not reached by heading here.
- Whose chips the report counts, whether they had been sold, or how, if the report is right, they came to Hong Kong. The report does not say, and no other source found here does.
- That the chips Reuters counts sit in a port, or that an import permit was refused. Those are Palmer Luckey's words, from a machine transcript nobody has checked by ear [clip-luckey]. Reuters's words are "stored in Hong Kong" and "withholds approval for large-scale imports" [cn-clips-reuters-hongkong-yahoo].
- That any trade figure here counts Nvidia chips, or that figures of different kinds can be added together.
