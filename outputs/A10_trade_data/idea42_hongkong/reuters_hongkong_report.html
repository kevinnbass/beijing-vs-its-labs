China chip policy · Reuters’s Hong Kong report

Does any public record bear out Reuters's report, from two people it did not name, on Nvidia H200 chips bound for China?

None was found, and none that refutes it. With a chip valued at the press price, Hong Kong's trade figures by heading (to July 2026) show no inflow of that size of United States origin or under the line usually read as Taiwan, its monthly totals for office machines and computers (to September 2026) show exports' lead over imports narrowing in August and largely made up in September, and Taiwan's exports of computers and computer parts to Hong Kong (to October 2026) are far smaller; none of these figures can pick out a chip. No government confirmation was found. Reuters's count rests on two people it does not name.

Draft by Claude, Anthropic's model. Written Nov 23, 2026; revised Nov 26, 2026 (UTC).

Anthropic has argued in public for controls on chip exports to China (that is from my general knowledge; no saved page is cited for it), so judge this note by its sources.

Words between quotation marks were checked against the saved copy of the source cited. Below, a heading is a six-digit code such as 8471.80 (the tariff's own word for it is subheading), and heading 8471 means every code that begins 8471. English in round brackets after Chinese is my rendering, not an official translation.

  • 1 a filled number: a document, a filing, a named person on the record, a published commentary, report or statement of state media, an industry body or a research institute, an institution's own notice, or a named outlet's account of a document
  • 2 an open number: a press report citing unnamed people, a research firm's, broker's or research group's count, or captions and machine transcripts nobody has checked by ear

What Reuters reported

Reuters, Nov 5, 2026: about 600,000 of Nvidia's H200 chips “are stored in Hong Kong awaiting shipment to mainland China, two people familiar with the matter said, as Beijing withholds approval for large-scale imports” 1. Reuters calls this “The size of the stockpile of H200 chips, which has not previously been reported” 1. The report does not say whose chips it counts, where in Hong Kong it places them, or how, if it is right, they came there. The Nov 5 dateline stands on the copy Yahoo carries; CNA's page of the same report shows only its own date, Nov 6, 2026.

Palmer Luckey gave the same number in a clip posted on Nov 19, 2026: “There's 600,000 H200 GPUs sitting in a port right now”, because “China will not give them an import permit” 2. Those words are from a machine transcript of the clip (0:18 to 0:30); nobody has checked them by ear. Both phrases say more than the report his number matches. Reuters's words are “stored in Hong Kong”, not in a port, and that Beijing “withholds approval for large-scale imports” 1. The project's argument records that no permit rule for these chips was found in China's import lists read, and no decision naming them under the trade law's power to restrict goods outside those lists (its point 18).

The answer in short

  • No official figure or statement was found that confirms Reuters's count. Reuters asked Hong Kong's government and got no reply. China's foreign ministry, asked about the report, referred the question elsewhere.
  • No second news outlet with sources of its own for the count was found. The pages that carry it relay Reuters.
  • Hong Kong's own trade figures by tariff heading were available to July 2026. At the press price of about 27,000 dollars a chip the reported count would be worth about 16 billion dollars. From February to July 2026 the figures show no inflow of that size of United States origin or under the partner line usually read as Taiwan, and under the headings an American proclamation names for such chips exports ran ahead of imports in every month; that cannot show whether a stock of any one product grew. One gap: by the statistics office's older guides, goods passing through on a through bill of lading or air waybill need no declaration; if that still applies, they would be in no Hong Kong figure.
  • For August and September 2026 Hong Kong's figures could be reached only as broad divisions. Imports of office machines and computers jumped in August 2026 and exports jumped in September. Over the two months together, exports ran ahead of imports by a little less than in two average months of the year, so August's narrowing was largely made up by the end of September. It cannot say what came and went, or whether a stock of any one product grew, and October was not yet there.
  • Taiwan's own figures run to October 2026. They show 4.69 billion US dollars of computers and computer parts sent to Hong Kong in ten months, every maker's goods together, far below what 600,000 chips would be worth at the press price. Its figure for integrated circuits is far too large to tell anything.
  • Two statements on the record touch the report without confirming it: Nvidia says much of its product is warehoused in and distributed from Hong Kong, and it booked a charge for excess H200 inventory and purchase obligations in the half year to Jul 26, 2026. Neither says where any H200 stock is kept. Nvidia's own figure for licensed sales of Hopper data-centre products to China in the quarter to Oct 25, 2026 is 2.4 billion dollars; at the press price the reported 600,000 chips would be worth nearly seven times that.
  • No customs line read here, in Hong Kong or elsewhere, can count Nvidia chips: each is a total for a kind of goods that names no maker, and no saved page says under which line an importer in China or Hong Kong declares these chips. Nvidia's own list gives only the codes Nvidia applies itself (point 4).

1. How large Reuters's count is

1About 16 billion US dollars. Reuters gave a price in January 2026: H200 chips “priced at around $27,000 each” 3. That is a press figure from unnamed people. At that price 600,000 chips are 16.2 billion US dollars (our multiplication), or about 126 billion Hong Kong dollars at 7.8 to the US dollar (the rate is from my general knowledge of Hong Kong's peg, not from a saved page). Every comparison of the 600,000 with a dollar figure in this note rests on that price; the trade figures set beside one another do not.

2Far more than Nvidia says it sold to China under licence. Nvidia's own figures are on the record. For the quarter to Jul 26, 2026: “Shipments of Data Center Hopper products to China during the second quarter of fiscal year 2027 were less than 1% of Data Center revenue”, and “Data Center revenue was $89.0 billion” 4. For the quarter to Oct 25, 2026: “Q3 sales of licensed Hopper Data Center products to China was $2.4 billion” 5. Before those two quarters it had no revenue under the H200 licences: on May 20, 2026 Nvidia said of them “we have yet to generate any revenue” 6. The two quarters together are under 3.3 billion dollars (our arithmetic). At the press price of 27,000 dollars a chip, 2.4 billion dollars would be about 89,000 chips, and 3.3 billion is about a fifth of what 600,000 chips would be worth (our divisions; price-based equivalents, not counts, since Hopper products may be more than H200s alone). So, at the press price, Nvidia's own figures for licensed Hopper shipments and sales to China to Oct 25, 2026 come to at most about a fifth of the reported count's value. The report does not say whether the chips it counts had been sold, and no saved page says whether Nvidia counts a delivery to Hong Kong as a shipment to China.

2. Would Hong Kong's trade figures show a stock of the size Reuters reported?

3Only as a change in broad totals, and with one gap. No Hong Kong figure names a product or a maker, so the most its figures could show is a change in the totals under a heading. Hong Kong's statistics office says of its figures: “Import/export declaration is also required of Hong Kong's trade with Chinese Mainland, and statistics relating to this are included in Hong Kong's external merchandise trade statistics.” 7 So goods that leave Hong Kong for the mainland are counted as exports. Hong Kong's answers to a United Nations questionnaire on how it compiles trade figures say: “Hong Kong is a free port.” 8 They also say there are no bonded warehouses, that the general trade system is used, and that goods merely passing through are left out. The page is old: its date of release is Apr 7, 2003. If that still holds, goods brought into Hong Kong and stored there are counted as imports when they arrive and as exports when they leave. The gap is goods in passage. Hong Kong's statistics office lists “transhipment cargo consigned on a through bill of lading” or a through air waybill among the goods that need no declaration 9. Its slides for a United Nations workshop in December 2006 define the term as “any article that is brought into Hong Kong under a through bill of lading or a through air waybill from a place outside Hong Kong to another place outside Hong Kong”, and say such goods are “exempted from declaration” 10. Both documents are old, and whether the exemption still applies in this form was not established. If it does, goods held in Hong Kong on through papers to the mainland would be in no Hong Kong trade figure. The report does not say on what papers, if any, the chips it counts would be held.

4Which headings. A US proclamation of Jan 14, 2026 names three tariff headings for the advanced chips it covers: 8471.50, 8471.80 and 8473.30, which are units and parts of computers 11. The White House's fact sheet names the H200 among those chips: “such as the NVIDIA H200 and AMD MI325X” 12. Nvidia's own list of the codes it applies itself, “provided for informational purposes only” 13, gives 8471.80 for its HGX H200 parts, 8473.30 for its H200 NVL parts and 8471.50 for its DGX H200 systems 14. Neither the proclamation nor the list says under which line an importer in Hong Kong declares the chips. The companion note on customs figures, which asks whether customs figures can count the H200 chips Reuters reported entering China, sets this out. Imports are also recorded by where the goods were made: Hong Kong answers “Country of origin.” 8 Reuters says of the H200: “The chip is manufactured by Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company (TSMC)” 15. A chip made in Taiwan and only inspected in the United States may be recorded as of Taiwan origin, so two partner lines are shown below: the United States, and the line usually read as Taiwan.

Reuters says “The United States barred exports of the H200 to China before” the permission of late 2025 18. The Commerce Department dates the President's announcement to “December 8, 2025” 19. Nvidia dates the first H200 licences with the words “Beginning in February 2026”, and its report writes of these controls as covering “China (including Hong Kong and Macau)” 4. So the months compared here are those from February 2026. From February to July 2026 Hong Kong's imports under these headings of United States origin and under Other Asia, nes together were 3.1 billion dollars (our sum), against the 16.2 billion dollars that 600,000 chips would be at the press price. The United States' own export figures count something else, goods recorded as leaving the United States for Hong Kong, and they show no step of that size either: its exports to Hong Kong under the three headings came to 1,088.4 million dollars over the eight months January to August 2026 (our sum of eight monthly answers, the last of them 21).

7Average monthly imports by origin. The average month was 4.9 billion dollars in 2025 and 7.0 billion in January to July 2026. By origin, average monthly imports rose from 2.4 to 3.9 billion for mainland China, from 0.35 to 1.1 billion for South Korea and from 0.10 to 0.16 billion for the United States, and fell from 0.95 to 0.34 billion under Other Asia, nes (our averages, from the same monthly answers) 22.

8Exports ran ahead of imports in every month. A stock building up under these headings would, other things equal, narrow the excess of exports over imports; other goods can offset it. Under these headings exports were larger in each of the 19 months, by 0.5 to 3.6 billion dollars a month 22. The comparison is rough. The figures do not explain the gap, and it moves by a billion dollars or more from month to month. The totals can neither show nor rule out a stock of one product. Most of what left went to the mainland: of Hong Kong's total exports under the three headings, 65.9 billion dollars in January to July 2026, 48.1 billion went to the mainland (our sums). By heading, its exports to the mainland under 8473.30 were 16.46 billion dollars in January to July 2025 and 36.90 billion in January to July 2026; under 8471.80 they were 12.75 and 7.02 billion, and under 8471.50, 3.90 and 4.19 billion. These lines can show that Hong Kong's exports of computer parts and units to the mainland ran to tens of billions of dollars. They do not name the makers, and they cannot show whether any Nvidia chip was among them. Hong Kong's statistics office also publishes a quarterly table of selected products that gives parts and accessories of computer hardware on their own; it does not list the tariff codes it covers. In every quarter from January 2024 to June 2026 the total exports of these parts were larger than the imports, by 18,781 million Hong Kong dollars in the first quarter of 2026 and by 39,961 million in the second (our subtractions) 23. The third quarter of 2026 was not yet in the table on Nov 25, 2026.

The heading holds the processors of every maker declared under it, and Nvidia's own list puts its H200 products under other headings (point 4), so the surplus says nothing about H200s.

11What that can and cannot tell. At the press price, and at 7.8 Hong Kong dollars to the US dollar, 600,000 chips would be about 126 billion Hong Kong dollars: more than this division's imports in any month from January 2025 to September 2026. In August 2026 imports did gain: exports exceeded imports by 5,111 million Hong Kong dollars, against 28,302 million in July. In September the excess was 32,089 million. 5. What else bears on it

On the record

16Nvidia says much of its product is warehoused in Hong Kong. Nvidia told the US securities regulator that export controls may disrupt its supply chain “for a substantial portion of our products, which are warehoused in and distributed from Hong Kong” 4. The filing gives no figure for what it holds there and names no product.

17Nvidia booked a charge for excess H200 inventory and purchase obligations. The same report, for the quarter to Jul 26, 2026, says that “During the first half of fiscal year 2027, we incurred a $0.4 billion charge associated with H200 for excess inventory and purchase obligations” 4; that half year ended on Jul 26, 2026. A charge is an accounting entry, not a count of chips held. It also says: “The licenses require that the H200s go through an inspection process in the United States prior to any shipment to the customer.” 4 Neither sentence says where the stock was kept.

18Nvidia says it has stopped shipping, and gives no reason. On its results call of Nov 17, 2026: “we are no longer shipping licensed Hopper Data Center products to China” 5. Reuters wrote that Nvidia's then interim chief executive “did not explain why Nvidia had stopped shipping the processors or whether the halt would be permanent” 28. She told CNBC the same day that the licensed H200s “went to China within this last quarter”, and: “we are no longer shipping H200 at all, let alone not shipping it to China” 29. That is from CNBC's own transcript, which it calls unofficial. Nvidia also made a statement, and two saved news pages carry its words. The Standard of Hong Kong, in a page stamped Nov 20, 2026, 04:57 Hong Kong time, has Nvidia say “in a statement” that the approvals were “subject to strict conditions and limited volumes” and that “despite receiving some licenses, we have since stopped shipping H200s to China” 30. The Daily Caller, in a page stamped Nov 20, 2026, 20:29 UTC, about a day later, quotes an Nvidia spokesperson at more length (the page says the spokesperson spoke to the Daily Caller News Foundation): “The review and licensing process spanned more than half a year, with approvals subject to strict conditions and limited volumes.” and “Despite receiving some licenses, we have already stopped shipping H200s to China.” 31 Neither page says on which day the statement was made, and it gives no reason for the stop either. Of the quarter after, Nvidia's commentary says: “We are not assuming any Data Center compute revenue from China in our outlook.” 32 The words Hong Kong appear in neither the call's transcript nor the commentary (a word search; both do name China), so neither says where any H200 stock is held.

Reported, from unnamed people

19The Financial Times, through Reuters, Aug 18, 2026. Reuters relayed the paper's report, which cited two people: “Beijing wants them to keep the hardware outside mainland China to support the growth of domestic chipmakers”, and “Chinese regulators have told companies they can ship the processors to Hong Kong, which operates outside mainland China's customs border, and use them there” 33. Reuters added that it “could not immediately verify the report”. The paper's own text, as Yahoo Finance Canada carries it, words the first point more narrowly, “Beijing wants them to keep most of these chips outside the mainland to support domestic chipmakers, one of the people said.”, and has more: Hong Kong “lacks enough data centres to house them”; “The US licences permit shipments of Nvidia's H200 chips to mainland China and Hong Kong.”; and “Nvidia has about half a million H200 chips in stock mainly for Chinese clients.” 34 That last sentence does not say the stock was in Hong Kong.

20A relay that went further than its source. A market-news site summarising that article wrote: “Total H200 stock held in this way is estimated at around 500,000 units” 35, meaning held in Hong Kong. The Financial Times text saved here does not say that. It should not be counted as a second source.

21Three stock estimates from unnamed sources. Reuters reported on Jan 14, 2026 that orders were “far exceeding Nvidia's inventory of 700,000 chips” 3. The Financial Times reported in August 2026 that Nvidia had about half a million H200s in stock 34. Reuters reported in November 2026 that about 600,000 H200s were stored in Hong Kong 1. All three reports cite people they did not name. The numbers are of similar size. No source says they describe the same stock.

What was not found

  • No statement by Hong Kong's government or customs on the chips Reuters reported.
  • No news outlet with its own sources for the 600,000. The pages surfaced in four searches relay Reuters, for example The Next Web: “The news agency cited two people familiar with the matter.” 36 Others were read in search results only and not saved.
  • No warehouse, cargo or port record that names the chips Reuters reported.
  • Hong Kong's figures by heading for August to October 2026 (its monthly divisions reach September), and the United States' for September and October 2026.
  • A customs line that could count H200 chips. Each line holds every maker's goods, and no saved page says under which line an importer in China or Hong Kong declares them.

What a reader may conclude

  • The 600,000 is one report on the word of two unnamed people. Nothing official found here confirms it.
  • From February to July 2026, Hong Kong's figures for the headings named for these chips show no inflow of that size of United States origin or under Other Asia, nes, with a chip valued at the press price of about 27,000 dollars. Its monthly divisions show exports' lead over imports of office machines and computers narrowing in August 2026 and largely made up in September, and Taiwan's figures for computers and computer parts show no flow of that size to Hong Kong to October 2026. None of these figures can pick out a chip.
  • The United States' export figures to Hong Kong under those headings, which run to August 2026, show no step of that size either; its figures for September and October 2026 were not reached. If the statistics office's older guides still apply (point 3), Hong Kong's own figures leave out goods in passage on through papers, so they could not show goods held that way.
  • By Nvidia's own account, its Hopper shipments and licensed sales to China in the two quarters to Oct 25, 2026 came to under 3.3 billion dollars 45, and before them it had no revenue under the H200 licences 6. At the press price that is at most about a fifth of what 600,000 chips would be worth.
  • Nvidia says it warehouses much of its product in Hong Kong; it gives no figure for what it holds there and names no product.
  • The Financial Times reported, as Reuters relayed it on Aug 18, 2026, that regulators told companies they could ship the chips to Hong Kong and use them there 33. That is a press report citing two unnamed people.

What a reader may not conclude

  • That the figure is confirmed, or that it is false. Hong Kong's figures by heading for the months after July 2026 were not reached here, and even they could show only a change in totals under a heading, not these chips.
  • Whose chips the report counts, whether they had been sold, or how, if the report is right, they came to Hong Kong. The report does not say, and no other source found here does.
  • That the chips Reuters counts sit in a port, or that an import permit was refused. Those are Palmer Luckey's words, from a machine transcript nobody has checked by ear 2. Reuters's words are “stored in Hong Kong” and “withholds approval for large-scale imports” 1.
  • That any trade figure here counts Nvidia chips, or that figures of different kinds can be added together.

What would settle more

  • Hong Kong's figures by heading for August to October 2026, and the United States' export figures for September and October 2026. Goods worth sixteen billion dollars, if declared under those headings and recorded for those places, would be large beside these figures; even then the figures would not name the goods or their maker.
  • Nvidia's quarterly report for the quarter to Oct 25, 2026: what it says about stock and write-offs. On Nov 17, 2026 Nvidia said: “we plan to file our 10-Q in the coming weeks” 5. The Securities and Exchange Commission's record of Nvidia's filings, fetched on Nov 23, 2026, lists no report for that quarter; the newest quarterly report in it is the one for the quarter to Jul 26, 2026 37. Nor did the record list one when fetched again on Nov 24, 2026 38, or on Nov 25, 2026 at 22:16 UTC 39.
