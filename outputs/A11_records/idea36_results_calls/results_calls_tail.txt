## Quote the calls, not the commission's appendix

The US-China commission's report of November 2026 prints two of the Tencent remarks above in its Appendix II [cn-usdocs-uscc-2026-ch8]. Its notes give Chinese transcripts of the two calls on Sina Technology as the source. So the appendix's English is a translation back from Chinese. The stock-data site's transcripts of the two calls are in English throughout (a script finds no mark of an untranslated passage in either), so the officers' own English words are on record there.

- **March 2026, Tencent's president.** The appendix: "If conditions allow us to purchase" [cn-usdocs-uscc-2026-ch8]. The call: "we would actually prefer to buy because then we don't necessarily need to pay the additional margin for leasing" [cn-o36-tencent-call-20260318-sa]. The sense is the same.
- **May 2026, its chief strategy officer.** The appendix has policy restrictions that "prevent some foreign-produced and" foreign-designed GPUs from entering the Chinese market [cn-usdocs-uscc-2026-ch8]. The call: "a combination of policy restrictions on certain, you know, foreign design GPUs being brought into China" [cn-o36-tencent-call-20260513-sa]. The appendix is the stronger: where he spoke of restrictions on GPUs being brought in, it has restrictions that prevent them from entering.

## What it changes for the argument

- **Point 6** of the argument now carries iFlytek's two lines: that other model makers commonly use Nvidia chips two generations ahead, and that home-made computing still falls short of the H200.
- **Point 9** now carries the Tencent president's words of Nov 18, 2026 on computing leased or built outside China, Meituan's claim for its model, and iFlytek's unchecked claim about DeepSeek's V4.
- **Point 11 now carries the nearest line to Beijing's side.** It is Tencent's "policy restrictions on certain, you know, foreign design GPUs being brought into China" [cn-o36-tencent-call-20260513-sa], which does not say whose policy. Points 13 to 16 gain nothing direct: Beijing's instructions stay as reported, and no call confirms them.
- **Point 19 now carries one on-the-record line from a buyer.** "higher-end imported GPUs which are now becoming available again" (Tencent, Mar 18, 2026) [cn-o36-tencent-call-20260318-sa] is a company saying on the record that it was buying imported high-end GPUs again. It names no chip and no number.
- **Point 20 could take the companies' own words.** The argument takes the Tencent president's words of November 2023 from a Reuters report. The first table has them from the call transcript, and has Alibaba's and Baidu's of the same month, Alibaba's from the company's own transcript.
- **A caution for November 2026.** Tencent and Alibaba both spoke of new export rules in the week Nvidia stopped shipping. A US rule was printed that week: its summary speaks of exports "to overseas data centers that remotely supply significant volumes of compute to companies headquartered in Country Group D:5 or Macau" [cn-o38-us-rule-fr-20261119-text]. Nvidia gave no reason for its stop. So this record shows neither Beijing nor the new US rule as the cause. The note on Nvidia's own documents (idea 38, in the folder idea38_nvidia_10q) sets it out.
- **The wish for the best chips is on the record, the wish for Nvidia by name is not.** Tencent's president wants "the most state-of-the-art" training chips [cn-o36-tencent-call-20260318-sa]. That the companies used and wanted Nvidia chips by name rests on other records (the argument's points 5 to 8). One of them is iFlytek's record of what its rivals use, in the second table here.
