## What Nvidia said, quarter by quarter

Each quarter below gives Nvidia's own words, from its commentary, its call transcripts and its last filed report; the one report by others quoted in this section, Reuters's, is named where it is quoted. Hopper is Nvidia's name for the chip generation; Reuters says "The Hopper family includes the H200" [9].

**1.** **First quarter of fiscal 2027, reported May 20, 2026: no Data Center Hopper products shipped to China.** Commentary: "No shipments of Data Center Hopper products to China occurred during the quarter, compared with $4.6 billion in the first quarter of fiscal year 2026." [10] On the call, the chief financial officer: "While the US government has approved licenses for H200 to be shipped to China-based customers, we have yet to generate any revenue, and we are uncertain whether any imports will be allowed into the country." [11]

**2.** **Quarter to Jul 26, 2026, reported Aug 26, 2026: under 1% of Data Center revenue.** Commentary: "Shipments of Data Center Hopper products to China during the quarter were less than 1% of Data Center revenue." [12] On the call, the chief financial officer: "In Q2, we shipped less than 1% of our total data center revenue in Hopper 200 products to customers based in China in accordance with the US government licenses."; and "given ongoing geopolitical uncertainty, there is no China data center compute revenue in our forward outlook" [13]. The Form 10-Q for that quarter says the United States granted licences for some H200 sales, "but such sales were restricted by the PRC government" [14].

**3.** **Quarter to Oct 25, 2026, reported Nov 17, 2026: $2.4 billion, then a stop.** Commentary: "Shipments of Data Center Hopper products to China during the quarter were $2.4 billion."; and for the quarter ahead: "We are not assuming any Data Center compute revenue from China in our outlook." [7] On the call, the then interim chief executive, who is also the chief financial officer: "Q3 sales of licensed Hopper Data Center products to China was $2.4 billion. Our guidance does not have any licensed Hopper Data Center products included, and we are no longer shipping licensed Hopper Data Center products to China." [15]

The outlook sentence in point 3 is about the next quarter, not the one reported. It stands under the heading "Outlook for the fourth quarter of fiscal 2027, which includes an additional week, is as follows:" [7], and the Form 8-K that carries the results has the same sentence: "We are not assuming any Data Center compute revenue from China in our outlook." [16] It does not say that no such revenue came in the quarter reported, which had $2.4 billion of licensed Hopper Data Center sales to China.

**The size of it.** The same commentary gives Data Center revenue for the quarter as "a record $107.6 billion" [7]. $2.4 billion is about 2.2% of that (the division is mine). The quarter before, the share was "less than 1%" [12].

**Reuters's report holds.** Reuters wrote that Nvidia "sold $2.4 billion worth of licensed Hopper-generation data centre products to China in the quarter ended October 25" [9]. Nvidia's transcript and commentary say the same, and the argument's point 19 cites Nvidia's own transcript for it.

## The stop: Nvidia gave no reason

- **No reason is given.** In the transcript of the call the word China comes twice, both times in the passage quoted above; the word H200 comes not at all (a script's count). Reuters noted the same: "She did not explain why Nvidia had stopped shipping the processors or whether the halt would be permanent." [9] The argument's point 19 says it too: Nvidia gave no reason. This note gives none either.

## What the same call said about export controls

- **On the call.** In the outlook of the prepared remarks: "New export controls were released by the U.S. Commerce Department this week. However, with demand far exceeding supply, we expect our performance to remain strong." [15] Asked about growth, the chief financial officer said: "we did look at an assessment in terms of the current export controls, and we're still going to spend some time studying those" [15]. An analyst on the call spoke of "the new BIS rules" [15]. The transcript does not name the rule.
- **The US rule of that week, in its own text.** I take the rule the call speaks of to be the one printed two days later: "Addressing Remote Access to Advanced Semiconductors", an interim final rule of the Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security, printed in the Federal Register of Nov 19, 2026: "This rule is effective November 19, 2026." [23] Its summary puts two things first: exports "to overseas data centers that remotely supply significant volumes of compute to companies headquartered in Country Group D:5 or Macau", and a new licence requirement for exports of "advanced semiconductors to Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, or Brazil", "countries which present a higher-than-normal risk of diversion", except for chips bound for companies headquartered, or whose ultimate parent company is headquartered, in the United States, "provided certain reporting requirements are met" [23]. China is in that group: the rule speaks of "Macau, the People's Republic of China (PRC), or other Country Group D:5 headquartered entities setting up cloud servers or data centers in other countries" [23]. I read the summary and the licensing section, not the whole rule, and this is not a legal reading.
