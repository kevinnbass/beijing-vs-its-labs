## What Nvidia said, quarter by quarter

All words below are Nvidia's, from its commentary, its call transcripts and its last filed report. Hopper is Nvidia's name for the chip generation; Reuters says "The Hopper family includes the H200" [cn-acts-reuters-halt-20261118-relay2].

1. **First quarter of fiscal 2027, reported May 20, 2026: nothing shipped.** Commentary: "No shipments of Data Center Hopper products to China occurred during the quarter, compared with $4.6 billion in the first quarter of fiscal year 2026." [cn-o38-nvda-q1fy27-cfo-commentary-pdf] On the call, the chief financial officer: "While the US government has approved licenses for H200 to be shipped to China-based customers, we have yet to generate any revenue, and we are uncertain whether any imports will be allowed into the country." [cn-o38-nvda-q1fy27-call-transcript-pdf]
!! cn-o38-nvda-q1fy27-call-transcript-pdf | 20-May-2026
2. **Quarter to Jul 26, 2026, reported Aug 26, 2026: under 1%.** Commentary: "Shipments of Data Center Hopper products to China during the quarter were less than 1% of Data Center revenue." [cn-o38-nvda-q2fy27-cfo-commentary-pdf] On the call, the chief financial officer: "In Q2, we shipped less than 1% of our total data center revenue in Hopper 200 products to customers based in China in accordance with the US government licenses."; and "given ongoing geopolitical uncertainty, there is no China data center compute revenue in our forward outlook" [cn-o38-nvda-q2fy27-call-transcript-pdf]. The Form 10-Q for that quarter says the United States granted licences for some H200 sales, "but such sales were restricted by the PRC government" [man-ms-sec-10q-2026-07].

## Why shipments stopped: Nvidia does not say

- **No reason is given.** In the transcript of the call the word China comes twice, both times in the passage quoted above; the word H200 comes not at all (a script's count). Reuters noted the same: "She did not explain why Nvidia had stopped shipping the processors or whether the halt would be permanent." [cn-acts-reuters-halt-20261118-relay2]
- **What stands near it on the call.** Later in the same prepared remarks, in the outlook: "New export controls were released by the U.S. Commerce Department this week. However, with demand far exceeding supply, we expect our performance to remain strong." [cn-o38-nvda-q3fy27-call-transcript-pdf] She did not tie this to the stop. Asked about growth, she said: "we did look at an assessment in terms of the current export controls, and we're still going to spend some time studying those" [cn-o38-nvda-q3fy27-call-transcript-pdf].
- **The US rule of that week, in its own text.** The transcript does not name the rule; an analyst on the call spoke of "the new BIS rules" [cn-o38-nvda-q3fy27-call-transcript-pdf]. I take it to be the one printed two days later. That rule is "Addressing Remote Access to Advanced Semiconductors", an interim final rule of the Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security, printed in the Federal Register of Nov 19, 2026: "This rule is effective November 19, 2026." [cn-o38-us-rule-fr-20261119-text] Its summary puts two things first: exports "to overseas data centers that remotely supply significant volumes of compute to companies headquartered in Country Group D:5 or Macau", and a new licence requirement for "Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, or Brazil" [cn-o38-us-rule-fr-20261119-text]. China is in that group: the rule speaks of "Macau, the People's Republic of China (PRC), or other Country Group D:5 headquartered entities setting up cloud servers or data centers in other countries" [cn-o38-us-rule-fr-20261119-text].

- **The rule does not name the H200.** The saved text of the rule does not hold the word H200 (a script's search; the word Brazil, a control, is found). Its text of the licensing section provides "a case-by-case license review policy" for chips under stated performance limits "when destined to end-users located in China or Macau" [cn-o38-us-rule-fr-20261119-text]. I read the summary and that section, not the whole rule, and this is not a legal reading.
- **The last filed report put the limit on Beijing's side.** For the quarter to Jul 26, 2026: "but such sales were restricted by the PRC government" [man-ms-sec-10q-2026-07]. Whether that still held in the quarter to Oct 25, 2026, when $2.4 billion was shipped, the saved documents do not say.

So the record holds three things side by side: a stop with no reason given; a new US rule the same week, whose summary puts data centres abroad and five other countries first; and an earlier filing that put the limit on the Chinese government. None of the saved documents says which of these, if any, caused the stop.

The end customer and shipping location may be different from our direct customers’ headquarters location." [cn-o38-nvda-q3fy27-supplemental-pdf]
