Each quarter below gives Nvidia's own words, from its commentary, its call transcripts and its last filed report; the one report by others quoted in this section, Reuters's, is named where it is quoted. 1. **First quarter of fiscal 2027, reported May 20, 2026: no Data Center Hopper products shipped to China.** Commentary: "No shipments of Data Center Hopper products to China occurred during the quarter, compared with $4.6 billion in the first quarter of fiscal year 2026." [cn-o38-nvda-q1fy27-cfo-commentary-pdf] On the call, the chief financial officer: "While the US government has approved licenses for H200 to be shipped to China-based customers, we have yet to generate any revenue, and we are uncertain whether any imports will be allowed into the country." 2. **Quarter to Jul 26, 2026, reported Aug 26, 2026: under 1% of Data Center revenue.** Commentary: "Shipments of Data Center Hopper products to China during the quarter were less than 1% of Data Center revenue." [cn-o38-nvda-q2fy27-cfo-commentary-pdf] On the call, the chief financial officer: "In Q2, we shipped less than 1% of our total data center revenue in Hopper 200 products to customers based in China in accordance with the US government licenses."; and "given ongoing geopolitical uncertainty, there is no China data center compute revenue in our forward outlook" [cn-o38-nvda-q2fy27-call-transcript-pdf]. ## What the same call said about export controls

- **On the call.** In the outlook of the prepared remarks: "New export controls were released by the U.S. Commerce Department this week. However, with demand far exceeding supply, we expect our performance to remain strong." [cn-o38-nvda-q3fy27-call-transcript-pdf] Asked about growth, the chief financial officer said: "we did look at an assessment in terms of the current export controls, and we're still going to spend some time studying those" [cn-o38-nvda-q3fy27-call-transcript-pdf]. An analyst on the call spoke of "the new BIS rules" [cn-o38-nvda-q3fy27-call-transcript-pdf]. The transcript does not name the rule.
- **The US rule of that week, in its own text.** I take the rule the call speaks of to be the one printed two days later: "Addressing Remote Access to Advanced Semiconductors", an interim final rule of the Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security, printed in the Federal Register of Nov 19, 2026: "This rule is effective November 19, 2026." [cn-o38-us-rule-fr-20261119-text] Its summary puts two things first: exports "to overseas data centers that remotely supply significant volumes of compute to companies headquartered in Country Group D:5 or Macau", and a new licence requirement for exports of "advanced semiconductors to Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, or Brazil", "countries which present a higher-than-normal risk of diversion", except for chips bound for companies headquartered, or whose ultimate parent company is headquartered, in the United States, "provided certain reporting requirements are met" [cn-o38-us-rule-fr-20261119-text]. China is in that group: the rule speaks of "Macau, the People's Republic of China (PRC), or other Country Group D:5 headquartered entities setting up cloud servers or data centers in other countries" [cn-o38-us-rule-fr-20261119-text]. I read the summary and the licensing section, not the whole rule, and this is not a legal reading.
