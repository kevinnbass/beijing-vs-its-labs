## The answer so far
- Yes on direction, with five limits. From 2024 to 2026 the Chinese state's declared policy, and what its regulators did, pressed China's AI companies to move away from Nvidia chips; the companies went on using those chips, ordering them and asking for them. The limits: Beijing was not one actor; the pressure came as public warnings and unpublished guidance, not as a published ban; some purchases were approved and some chips came in; the companies had reasons of their own, and American curbs and conditions pushed too; and what Beijing meant by it is not settled. That is the project's argument as revised on Nov 25, 2026, in short; the records below are its dated evidence.

## What would change the answer next
- Nvidia's 10-Q for the quarter ended Oct 25, 2026, and what it says of the Chinese government. The wording has changed from report to report: in November 2025 the government "restricted customers from purchasing, importing, or using our data center products" [man-ms-sec-10q-2025-10]; in the report of August 2026 the sales under the American licences "were restricted by the PRC government" [man-ms-sec-10q-2026-07]. Whether the next report keeps, drops or adds to those words is the next record on Nvidia's own side.
- Any published Chinese rule. No published Chinese order against buying Nvidia chips was found in the 82 policy pages read, and such an order could have been found: the 2023 review conclusion telling operators of critical information infrastructure to stop buying Micron's products is among those pages, "应停止采购美光公司产品" [cn-policy-micron-decision]. A published order, or a published permit rule for these imports, would change the second limit: so far the pressure is public warnings and unpublished guidance.
