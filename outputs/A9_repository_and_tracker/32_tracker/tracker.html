The answer so far

  • Yes on direction, with five limits. From 2024 to 2026 the Chinese state’s declared policy and its regulators’ acts pressed China’s AI companies to move away from Nvidia chips. The companies went on using those chips, ordering them and asking for them. They also bought, used and trained on home-made chips, by their own and their suppliers’ accounts. Beijing’s security and planning side pressed against Nvidia’s chips, while its commerce and foreign-affairs side, some local governments and units of telecom carriers were more open to them. The pressure came through public warnings and published measures that favour home-made chips. Reuters and the Financial Times reported unpublished guidance, citing people they did not name (Reuters, Nov 5, 2025, as carried by The Standard (Hong Kong): a report, from two unnamed people, on guidance about foreign AI chips in state-funded data centres; Financial Times, Sept 17, 2025 (three unnamed sources)). The search of policy pages found no published ban. Some purchases were reported approved, by press reports citing people they did not name (Reuters on The Standard (Jan 28, 2026)), and Nvidia’s own filings report licensed sales to China (Nvidia’s annual report for fiscal 2026; Nvidia's 10-Q, August 2026). The companies had reasons of their own to turn to home-made chips. The filings and other records name American curbs and conditions far more often than a Chinese act as a cause. What Beijing meant by its acts remains open. The records below give dated evidence for the argument as revised on Nov 26, 2026.

What would change the answer next

  • Nvidia’s 10-Q for the quarter ended Oct 25, 2026, and what it says of the Chinese government. The wording has changed from report to report: in November 2025 the government “restricted customers from purchasing, importing, or using our data center products” (Nvidia's 10-Q, November 2025); in the report of August 2026 the sales under the American licences “were restricted by the PRC government” (Nvidia's 10-Q, August 2026). Whether the next report keeps, drops or adds to those words is the next record on Nvidia’s own side.
  • Any published Chinese rule. The search of the 82 policy pages found no published Chinese order against buying Nvidia chips. It did find the 2023 review conclusion telling operators of critical information infrastructure to stop buying Micron’s products, “应停止采购美光公司产品” (the 2023 review conclusion). Public warnings and published measures favouring home-made chips are in the record. Reuters and the Financial Times reported unpublished guidance, citing people they did not name (Reuters, Nov 5, 2025, as carried by The Standard (Hong Kong): a report, from two unnamed people, on guidance about foreign AI chips in state-funded data centres; Financial Times, Sept 17, 2025 (three unnamed sources)). A published restriction or permit rule specific to Nvidia’s chips would change that account.
  • The end of the antitrust case. No page read says the Nvidia case ended: the market regulator’s review of its 2025 work, posted Apr 10, 2026, still lists “对英伟达公司实施进一步调查” (a further investigation of Nvidia) (the regulator's review of 2025), and its news list to Nov 23, 2026 has a suspension of the case against DuPont China, “市场监管总局暂停对杜邦中国集团有限公司的反垄断调查程序”, and none for Nvidia (the regulator's news list; the same list, newest page). By Nvidia’s account the preliminary finding is that “our compliance with applicable U.S. export controls, which required us to offer degraded products to the Chinese market, discriminated unfairly against customers in the China market” (Nvidia's 10-Q, August 2026). An end to the case would be a new act of the state; it would not by itself settle what Beijing meant, the fifth limit.

  • Beijing stands for the leaders and bodies of the Chinese state named in the records: Xi Jinping, Premier Li Qiang, Vice Premiers He Lifeng and Ding Xuexiang; the cyberspace regulator (Cyberspace Administration of China) and its review office; the state planner (National Development and Reform Commission, NDRC); the market regulator (State Administration for Market Regulation); the commerce, finance, industry and security ministries; customs; and the state’s evaluation centre for information security.   • The companies are the Chinese AI companies named in the records: Alibaba, Baidu, ByteDance, Tencent, DeepSeek, Zhipu, iFlytek and 360. A Shanghai government body’s account of what the city’s internet companies use stands in their row.
  • American officials appear where they speak of Beijing: the Commerce Secretary, Howard Lutnick, at a Senate hearing, and Jeffrey Kessler, under secretary of commerce for industry and security, at a House hearing.
  • The chips are Nvidia’s. The models named in the records include the H20, the RTX PRO 6000D and the H200. Home-made chips, in the records, are chips of Chinese makers, among them Huawei, Alibaba’s T-Head unit and Baidu; China’s rules for government purchases judge a product by where it is made, not by who owns its maker.
  • The four rows come from the figure: Beijing holds Nvidia back, Beijing is more open to Nvidia, The companies reach for Nvidia, The companies turn to home-made chips. Each row groups records by the direction they point. The heading of each record spells out what the source says. “Beijing is more open to Nvidia” includes welcomes from leaders and ministers, commerce ministry statements on American curbs, a draft rule on technology exports, press reports citing unnamed people on approvals and deliveries, a US official’s account of minimal H200 exports to China so far, and two universities’ buying notices. “Beijing holds Nvidia back” also includes acts and statements that name no Nvidia chip, among them the rules for what government bodies buy, Xi Jinping’s call to conquer high-end chips, the security ministry’s warning about chips made abroad and Alibaba’s filing on a halt to purchases of chips it does not name.   • The records are a selection made to show both sides, not a census. The number of records on a side is not a measure.
  • A quotation that is found shows what the source says, not that it is true.
  • The state of play is written on its date from the saved sources; it does not update itself.
