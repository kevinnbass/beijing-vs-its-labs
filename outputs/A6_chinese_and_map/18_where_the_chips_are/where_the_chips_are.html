In seven saved sources, most places named for Nvidia chips are outside mainland China, or are plans on paper

Twelve records from seven saved sources. Three press reports put chips outside the mainland: waiting in Hong Kong, or used in data centres in Southeast Asia and Japan. Two records on paper lie outside it too: a Japanese firm’s notice of a data centre moved to Chiba, and licence applications for consignees in Malaysia, Indonesia and Thailand that Washington returned without action. Six are plans, tenders and contracts on the mainland that name Nvidia chips. One says chips are on the mainland: a cluster that a city government said had entered operation.

Read with care

  • This map shows places that reports and records name. It does not show where chips are. A mark is what one source says about one place: it is not proof that chips are there, and the map is not a count. The seven sources read for it do not settle where the chips Nvidia has sold are now.
  • Open marks rest on unnamed people. Filled marks rest on documents or on a named company or government body, as the outlet cited reports them. The tender and investment papers themselves were not read for this map. Two filled marks rest on documents that are themselves saved: Datasection’s notice and the Commerce Department’s table.
  • A diamond is a plan, a tender, a contract or a licence application. It shows that a paper names Nvidia chips, not that chips arrived. Of the Gansu centre Reuters writes: “The documents do not establish whether the processors have been delivered.” The licence applications drawn in grey were returned without action: no licence was given.
  • Not on the map, because the report names no place: chips that Beijing let in. Reuters, Nov 5, 2026, citing one unnamed person: “Several major Chinese internet companies have each obtained about 10,000 H200 chips in the past few months”.
  • The chips are not all one kind. Hong Kong: H200. Japan: B200 in the Financial Times’s report, B300 in the firm’s notice. The licence table covers Nvidia’s H200 and AMD’s MI325X; the applications drawn name Nvidia as exporter. Plans and tenders: H100 and H200. The Jiangsu cluster: H800.
  • Both reports of capacity rented abroad say it was within the rules at the time: leasing data centres abroad is “compliant with US export controls” (Financial Times, Nov 27, 2025); the paper calls the arrangement in Japan “a legal but geopolitically fraught strategy” (Dec 20, 2025). Later, on Nov 16, 2026, the US Commerce Department released a rule “to address remote access to advanced super intelligence (SI) chips and servers”, aimed at “remote computing arrangements involving data centers in third countries”.
  • Not drawn, from the Reuters review of Nov 13, 2026: a cluster in Fujian. Reuters lists it with facilities “already using restricted Nvidia hardware”, but says only that a Shanghai-listed company’s venture “had built an H800 cluster” and that its services contract “ended early”. It does not say plainly that the cluster runs. Also not drawn: two small university purchases (three H100 processors and two H200 chips).
