China chip policy: idea 39, second pass

Home-made chip shares: the sixteen governments left open

Of the sixteen province-level governments the first note left open, one sets a share for home-made computing in a plan that was read: Guangdong, 70% of newly added computing by the end of 2025, as an aim. For thirteen more a document was read in its own text and sets no share; two of those pay a reward at 90% or more. Tibet's plan is known only from accounts of it, its issuer's own among them, and for Xinjiang no computing plan was found.

Draft by Claude, Anthropic's model.

Written Nov 23, 2026 (UTC) as a second pass on idea 39 of the chip-policy project, and brought up to date the same day (round 4) with pages that later agents saved, and on Nov 24, 2026 for Tibet's row. Words between straight double quotes are a page's own words and were found in its saved copy by a script. English in round brackets is ours, not an official translation. One document is marked read, not saved: it was read in an Internet Archive capture kept in this output's folder, so its words stand here without quotation marks. Three PDFs the owner saved by hand (Qinghai's two plans and Hunan's plan) have a damaged text layer. Nothing is quoted from that layer: their quotations stand in saved Internet Archive captures of the same pages, and the table rows read from their page images are typed, with page numbers, in a file named transcription.txt beside each saved copy. Four sources are accounts of a published plan and not the plan's text. The build script files them under its kind for a named news outlet's account; two are newspapers' accounts, one is state radio's as carried by a government site, and one is a portal article. Each label says which.

  • 1 a filled number: a document, a filing, a named person on the record, or a published commentary of state media or an industry body
  • 2 an open number: a press report citing unnamed people, a research firm's count, or captions and machine transcripts nobody has checked by ear

The question

The first note on idea 39 found seven province-level governments with a published share for home-made computing hardware. It did not reach eleven (Zhejiang, Chongqing, Anhui, Shaanxi, Shanxi, Jiangxi, Guangxi, Qinghai, Hainan, Xinjiang, Tibet). For five more (Guangdong, Jilin, Heilongjiang, Ningxia, Yunnan) the document it read was a plan for artificial intelligence or for the digital industry, not a computing plan. This note takes those sixteen. For each it asks one thing: does a plan of the government set a share of home-made chips, home-made computing or independently controllable computing?

The answer in short

1Guangdong sets a share. Its computing plan for 2024 and 2025 says: “力争到2025年底，新增国产化算力占比达到70%” 1 (strive for home-made computing to make up 70% of newly added computing by the end of 2025). The page is on the site of the plan's lead issuing body. It is an aim, and the page does not mark it binding or indicative. Guangdong is one of the eight governments, of 31, found to set a share; the argument's table gives each in its document's own words. They do not count the same thing, so the numbers cannot be added or averaged.

2Two governments pay a reward and set no target: Shanxi and Ningxia. Shanxi's 2024 measures give up to 5 million yuan to a new or rebuilt data centre whose basic software and hardware are 90% or more home-made 2. Ningxia's policy, by its industry department's account, gives up to 10 million yuan 3. Shanxi's computing plan proper was not read, and Ningxia's hub plan is known only from a newspaper's account.

3Four governments not reached before are now read, from saved copies. None sets a share. Chongqing's plan of June 2026 for an energy and computing hub sets a share only for green power, “确保新建数据中心绿电占比超过80%” 4 (make sure green power is over 80% in newly built data centres); the word 国产 (home-made) is not in the page. Zhejiang's AI measures of 2025, held as a copy on a policy site, appeal to buyers, “倡导政府部门、算力中心、经营主体采购或应用国产软硬件” 5 (advocate that government departments, computing centres and businesses buy or use home-made software and hardware), and set voucher rates. Shaanxi's AI Plus plan of November 2025, held as a Sohu copy that lacks items 6 and 7, has its computing item whole: “按需建设算力基础设施” 6 (build computing infrastructure as needed). Hainan's AI Plus plan of 2026, held as a copy on a policy site, says “构建自主可控算力底座” 7 (build an independently controllable computing base) and gives no number.

4Five more governments: a document was read in its own text and sets no share. Qinghai's two plans, of 2024 and 2026, and Jiangxi's two documents are about computing. Qinghai's 2026 plan tells computing centres to deploy independently controllable, secure and reliable equipment 8; Jiangxi's measures of December 2025 say the share of independently controllable equipment is to rise step by step, with no number (read, not saved). Anhui's, Jilin's and Guangxi's are wider plans, for digital infrastructure or the digital economy. With point 3 that makes nine governments in this group (Anhui, Jilin, Guangxi, Jiangxi, Qinghai, Chongqing, Zhejiang, Shaanxi, Hainan).

5Two governments: no computing plan was found, and the first note's document stands (Heilongjiang, Yunnan). Each is a plan for AI or for the digital industry and sets no share.

6Tibet: accounts only. The region's computing plan for 2024 to 2026 is known from two accounts of it: the Tibet Communications Administration's own news item on its site, Jun 25, 2024 9, and a trade newspaper's account 10. Both give its targets for 2026, “到2026年，算力规模达到100PFLOPS，智算算力占比达到60%” (computing reaches 100 PFLOPS, and intelligent computing 60% of it), and neither has any of the home-made words or the word 芯片 (chip). An account that is silent does not show that the plan is silent.

7Xinjiang: no computing plan was found. Agent L16 read four pages on the region's own sites instead. By L16's reading and this note's scan, the two cited here set no share for home-made hardware, and the one use of independently controllable in them is about a network protocol 11.

8All 31. With the first note's governments, the session's table (tables/provinces_31.tsv) now has eight that set a share, 21 with a plan read that sets none, one known only from accounts (Tibet) and one with no computing plan found (Xinjiang). The build script checks that each row of this note agrees with that table.

9Two more documents, outside the sixteen, were saved by hand and read: Gansu's and Hunan's. Neither sets a share. Hunan's plan has the figure 95%, but it is the present rate of home-made equipment for making chips, not a goal for computing 12. They are in table 2.

Method

  • Two rounds. Round 1, on the morning of Nov 23, 2026, made 84 requests: 44 web searches, 34 page saves and 6 calls to the Internet Archive through the machine's one queue, each listed in request_log.txt. Round 4, the same day, made none. It rests on pages saved since by two later agents (L16 and L25, whose ids begin cn-le16- and cn-le25-) and on Internet Archive captures the session filed (ids ending -wayback).
  • What each row rests on. Saved official page: a copy in the project's store of a page on the issuing body's own site or its government's own site. Saved Archive capture of the official page: a copy in the project's store of an Internet Archive capture of such a page. Saved copy on another site: a copy in the project's store of the document's own text on any other site. A press account saved from another site: a saved page that describes the plan; the plan's own text is not held. Archive capture kept in this folder: read, not saved: a capture kept in archive/ and not in the project's store.
  • No share in the page read: the check and its control. A script listed, in the 21 pages read in this pass or saved since, each place where a home-made word (国产, 自主可控, 安全可控, 安全可靠, 自主化, 信创) stands within 60 characters of a percent sign: six places in five pages, listed in near_percent.txt. Each was read. Two are the control, which shows the scan can see a share: Guangdong's 70% and Shanxi's reward at 90%. The other four are not shares of home-made hardware: a goal for intelligent computing (50%) beside the words for an independently controllable ecosystem, in Guangdong's plan; a rate for public data (80%) beside a heading on secure and reliable data infrastructure, in Guangxi's; a rate of clean-energy use (100%) beside the words secure and reliable, in Qinghai's 2024 plan; and Hunan's 95% for chip-making equipment. For the four governments newly read, every percentage in the page was read as well: Chongqing's are for green power and the take-up of intelligent terminals; Zhejiang's are rates and thresholds of vouchers, subsidies and loan support; Shaanxi's is the take-up of intelligent terminals; the copy of Hainan's plan has none outside the copying site's own advertising. The scan was not run on the three damaged PDFs, which were read page by page as images, nor on the first note's pages, which that note checked.
  • A word is not in a page: the check and its control. Where a row says a word is not in a page, the script counted it at zero and found a control word in the same page, so the count could have seen a hit: thirteen such checks, listed in absent_check.txt.
  • No plan was found: what that rests on. It is a statement about searches, not about the government. The searches and their controls are in the section after the tables.
  • Hand-saved PDFs. Every page of the four PDFs the owner saved was opened and read as an image. Gansu's has a sound text layer. For Qinghai's two and Hunan's, the saved captures of the same pages have sound text, and the script compared it with what was typed from the images: of 56 typed lines, 45 stand word for word in the captures (five of them table rows, once a category cell that the typing repeats is set aside). The other 11 are the table and one box of Qinghai's 2026 plan, which are images in the capture too and so rest on the page image alone. The comparison is listed in transcription_check.txt.
  • Dates. Each date is the one the page gives, and the page's own date string was checked by the script.
