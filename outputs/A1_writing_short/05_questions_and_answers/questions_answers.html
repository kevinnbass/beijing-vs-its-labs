China chip policy · ten questions and answers

Did China ban Nvidia? Questions and answers

Ten questions a newcomer asks. Each answer is at most three sentences and names its source.

Draft by Claude, Anthropic's model. Written Nov 23, 2026; revised Nov 26, 2026 (UTC).

Words between quotation marks were found in the saved copy by a script. English given for Chinese sources is ours, not an official translation.

Two kinds of source are kept apart; the key under this header says what a filled and an open reference number mean. No published Chinese order against buying Nvidia chips was found in the 82 policy pages read. What is known of Beijing's instructions to the companies comes from company filings and from press reports, and each answer says which.

The short answer: no published ban was found, but Beijing's policy pulled against what its own AI companies wanted, with five limits. Questions 1, 7, 8, 9 and 10 give four of the limits; question 5 gives the fifth.

  • 1 a filled number: a document, a filing, a named person on the record, a published commentary, report or statement of state media, an industry body or a research institute, an institution's own notice, or a named outlet's account of a document
  • 2 an open number: a press report citing unnamed people, a research firm's or research group's count, or captions and machine transcripts nobody has checked by ear

The ten questions

1Did China ban Nvidia chips? No published ban was found. None of the 82 Chinese policy pages read holds an order against buying Nvidia chips, and Bloomberg's report of Sept 27, 2024, from unnamed people, says: “The policy has taken the form of guidance rather than an outright ban” 1. Some chips also came in (question 8).

2Then what did Beijing do in public? Two regulators acted against Nvidia by name. The market regulator announced an antitrust case against it on Dec 9, 2024 and, on Sept 15, 2025, a preliminary finding against it and a further investigation, and the cyberspace regulator summoned it over security risks in its H20 chip on Jul 31, 2025 234. State media warned about Nvidia's chips too: of the 75 state and Party media items on Nvidia chips found from July 2025 to November 2026, 16 of the 19 that only warn about them date from Jul 31 to Sept 24, 2025: the first three on the day of the summons, the regulator's notice as the state broadcaster CCTV's site and Xinhua carried it and a Global Times report 5, then among others a signed piece of Aug 1 on People's Daily's commentary account 6, and last a Science and Technology Daily report of Sept 24 7; the three found later are in Global Times in 2026, two of them an industry analyst's words in news reports and one the paper's own unsigned commentary, and the items after September 2025 are what two rounds of searches found, a sample of what these outlets printed and not a full count.

3Has anyone said on the record that purchases were restricted? Yes: two companies, in reports filed with the US securities regulator. Alibaba's annual report says, without naming Nvidia: “In September 2025, the Cyberspace Administration of China requested Chinese technology companies to halt purchases of certain chips.” 8 Nvidia's report of November 2025 says the Chinese government “restricted customers from purchasing, importing, or using our data center products”, and its report of August 2026 that its licensed H200 sales “were restricted by the PRC government” 910.

4Do China's AI companies want Nvidia chips? Yes. A Shanghai government body wrote, in a reply dated Apr 30, 2025 (published Nov 10, 2025), that in the city internet companies' own computing clusters run mainly on Nvidia cards 11. Reuters reported on Dec 31, 2025, citing unnamed people, that Chinese technology companies “have placed orders for more than 2 million H200 chips for 2026” 12.

5Why would Beijing hold back chips its own companies want? Its declared policy is to end dependence on foreign core technology and to raise the share of home-made chips: in May 2026 Li Chao, of the state planner's policy research office, said it was guiding home-made large models to do more to adapt to home-made computing chips 13. But what Beijing meant by its acts against Nvidia is not settled, and no Chinese document found states a motive for the unpublished guidance to companies or ties an act against Nvidia to the trade talks, though Beijing's own texts give purposes for its public acts (security, national interests, fair competition). The US-China Economic and Security Review Commission, in its report to Congress, saw in the move against Nvidia of China's market regulator, the State Administration for Market Regulation, “China's willingness to both exert leverage and ease pressure as a negotiating tactic” 14, while the US Trade Representative, Jamieson Greer, asked whether China would let its companies buy the H200, said it was “gonna be a sovereign decision for China” (captions, not checked by ear, at 5:59) 15.

6What did Reuters report about 600,000 chips in Hong Kong? A number from one press report, not from a record. Reuters, Nov 5, 2026, citing two unnamed people: “About 600,000 of Nvidia's advanced artificial intelligence chips are stored in Hong Kong awaiting shipment to China” 16. Asked about that report on Nov 9, 2026, the foreign ministry's spokesman said to ask the departments in charge 17.

7Was an import permit refused? That says more than the record shows. No permit rule for these chips was found in the policy pages read, nor in China's lists of goods that need an import licence for 2024, 2025 and 2026 (the 2026 list: 18); Reuters's words are that Beijing “withholds approval for large-scale imports” 16. On Jul 30, 2026 a company asked the commerce ministry, on its public message board, whether importing H200 chips needs a special import licence; the ministry's reply of Aug 19, 2026 speaks only of exporters and export controls 19.

8Did any Nvidia chips get in? Some, by press reports citing unnamed people. Reuters, Jan 28, 2026: ByteDance, Alibaba and Tencent “have been approved to purchase more than 400,000 H200 chips in total” 20; the Financial Times, through Reuters, Aug 18, 2026: “Small batches of Nvidia's H200 chips, one of the company's most powerful AI chips, have been allowed to enter mainland China” 21. On the record, Nvidia's report for the quarter to Jul 26, 2026 says: “we have made a fraction of the allowed shipments under the USG's H200 licensing program” 10.

9Did the whole Chinese government take one side? No. Vice Premier He Lifeng told Nvidia's chief executive in April 2025 that more American firms, Nvidia among them, were welcome to put down deeper roots in China, and the commerce minister told him in July 2025 that he hoped multinationals, Nvidia among them, would give Chinese customers high-quality, reliable products and services 2223. The Financial Times reported on Nov 13, 2026, citing unnamed people, that the commerce ministry “favoured imports” while security officials “viewed dependence on Nvidia as a strategic vulnerability” 24.

10Was Beijing the only thing in the way? No: American curbs and conditions stood in the way too, and the companies had reasons of their own. Nvidia's reports since November 2025 say “we are already effectively foreclosed from the China market by U.S. export controls” 9, and in Washington's own table of licence applications, as it stood on May 11, 2026, every approval is marked “Approved, not shipped”, while Washington returned without action the applications for 55% of the chips its summary records, with no reason given 2526. The companies were also turning to home-made chips: Alibaba says its “chip design subsidiary T-Head has brought proprietary GPUs into scaled production” 27.

The answer in one sentence

From 2024 the planning side of the Chinese state and the industry ministry, and from 2025 its security side, pressed China's AI companies, through unpublished guidance that the press reported, to buy home-made chips rather than Nvidia's, and from the summer of 2025 to stop ordering them, while the security ministry warned in public of backdoors in chips made abroad and state media warned about Nvidia's chips, most of all in August and September 2025; other parts of the state welcomed Nvidia and, by the reports, approved some purchases; the companies went on ordering Nvidia chips, in the millions by the press reports, and by those reports had received a small part of them by late 2026; they were also turning to home-made chips, for reasons of their own and under American curbs; and whether Beijing meant its acts as lasting policy or as leverage in trade talks is read both ways.

What these answers leave out

  • Each answer gives one to six sources. The full argument has 21 points and 461 sources; it is the project's argument page, ARGUMENT_referenced.html.
  • Where the chips that Nvidia sold are now is not known.
  • The numbers in questions 4, 6 and 8 are press reports citing unnamed people. Nvidia's own words in question 8 give no number.
  • Question 2, the antitrust case. No saved page says the case has ended. By Nvidia's own account the finding is about what it held back from Chinese customers: “our compliance with applicable U.S. export controls, which required us to offer degraded products to the Chinese market, discriminated unfairly against customers in the China market” 10. The answer does not say what the case shows about Beijing's wishes; that is read more than one way (question 5).
  • Question 3. Nvidia's wording changed from report to report. Its annual report of February 2026 has “discouraged customers from purchasing, importing, or using our data center products” where the report of November 2025 has restricted 28.
  • Question 5. The Trade Representative's words are from a video's caption track of an interview posted on May 15, 2026. Nobody has checked them by ear.
  • Question 6 gives Reuters's own date for the Hong Kong report, Nov 5, 2026, which stands on Yahoo's copy of it, also saved. The copy the answer cites is CNA's page, which shows only its own date, Nov 6, 2026.
  • Question 8. On its results call for the quarter ended Oct 25, 2026 Nvidia said: “Q3 sales of licensed Hopper Data Center products to China was $2.4 billion”, and “we are no longer shipping licensed Hopper Data Center products to China” 29. It gave no reason, and it did not say how many of those chips crossed into the mainland.
  • Question 10. The 55% is a share of the chips in the applications that Washington's summary of May 11, 2026 records, Nvidia's H200 and AMD's MI325X together. The committee that made the table public says some applications are “not fully reflected in the May 11, 2026, summary document” 26, so it is not a share of everything applied for. An application names a data-centre operator or ultimate consignee; it is not an order, and an approval there is Washington's licence, not Beijing's.
