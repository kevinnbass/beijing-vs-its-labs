The posts

1Palmer Luckey, clip posted Nov 19, 2026 (0:11, machine transcript): “China doesn't want NVIDIA to sell GPUs to Chinese companies.” Then the post's text: “there are Chinese companies that want to buy Nvidia GPUs”. Not checked by ear. Right on direction, with five limits. 12

2The declared line: use home-made chips. At its press conference of May 22, 2026, Li Chao of China's state planner (NDRC) said it was guiding home-made large models to do more to adapt to home-made computing chips. Source: the planner's own record. English ours. 3

3What the companies used: Nvidia. A Shanghai government body wrote (reply dated Apr 30, 2025; published Nov 10, 2025) that in the city internet firms' own clusters run mainly on Nvidia cards, while the three big telecoms mainly buy Huawei's and other home-made clusters. 4

4What Beijing did. Alibaba's annual report to the SEC: “In September 2025, the Cyberspace Administration of China requested Chinese technology companies to halt purchases of certain chips.” The sentence does not name Nvidia. This is a filing, not an unnamed source. 5

5Limit 1: Beijing was not one actor. On Jul 17, 2025 China's commerce minister told Nvidia's chief executive he hoped multinationals, Nvidia among them, would give Chinese customers high-quality, reliable products and services. Source: the ministry's account; English ours. 6

6Limit 2: warnings and guidance, not a published ban. Bloomberg, Sept 27, 2024, citing unnamed people: “The policy has taken the form of guidance rather than an outright ban”. In 82 Chinese policy pages read, no published order against buying Nvidia chips was found. 7

7Limit 3: some were approved, some came in. Press reports from unnamed people: over 2 million H200s ordered for 2026, over 400,000 approved by Beijing with conditions, about 600,000 stored in Hong Kong (Reuters); about 10,000 each to ByteDance and Tencent (FT, via Reuters). 891011

8Limit 4: the companies had reasons of their own, and US export curbs pushed too. Tencent's president, on a results call in November 2023, after new US curbs: “we will also try to look for domestic source for these training chips” (as Reuters reported the call). 12

9Limit 5: what Beijing meant is unsettled. A US commission's report to Congress read the market regulator's Nvidia move “as a negotiating tactic”. The US Trade Representative, asked May 15, 2026 if China would let firms buy H200s: “a sovereign decision for China” (captions). 1314

10What survives: from 2024 China's planning side and industry ministry, and from 2025 its security side, pressed its AI firms off Nvidia chips, by guidance the press reported. The firms kept ordering and, by the reports, got a small part. What Beijing meant is not settled.

That post carries four reported numbers: three from Reuters reports of Dec 31, 2025, Jan 28, 2026 and Nov 5, 2026, and one from the Financial Times as Reuters relayed it on Aug 18, 2026. The copy the post cites for the report of Nov 5 is CNA's page, which shows only its own date, Nov 6, 2026; Reuters's dateline of Nov 5 stands on Yahoo's copy of the same report, also saved.
  • Washington's side is left out for length: the conditions it set, and its own table of licence applications, which shows chips approved and not shipped and others returned without action. The argument sets them out under its fourth limit.
  • Post 6 names one press report. The public warnings in state media, and the dates they fall between, are in the argument's points 4 and 18.
  • Post 10 is a shorter form of the argument's last sentence; its last words are limit 5's. It drops the public warnings, the parts of the state that welcomed Nvidia and, by the reports, approved some purchases, and the companies' own turn to home-made chips; posts 5 to 8 carry them.
  • No recording has been checked by ear: not the clip in post 1, and not the interview in post 9.
