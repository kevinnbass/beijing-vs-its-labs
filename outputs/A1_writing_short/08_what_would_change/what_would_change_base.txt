## The answer these would test

From 2024 to 2026 the Chinese state's declared policy, and what its regulators did, pressed China's AI companies to move away from Nvidia chips. The companies went on using those chips, ordering them and asking for them. Five limits apply. First, Beijing was not one actor. Second, the pressure came as public warnings and unpublished guidance, not as a published ban. Third, some purchases were approved and some chips came in. Fourth, the companies had reasons of their own, and American curbs and conditions pushed too. Fifth, what Beijing meant by it is not settled.

Where a document bears on one of the limits, its entry says which. The direction itself rests on documents already held (see the last section), and it stands under either reading of what Beijing meant by it.

**4. A Chinese text that says what Beijing meant by it**
- **What it would show.** Whether Beijing meant its acts against Nvidia as lasting policy or as leverage in trade talks. It bears on the fifth limit. It would not change the direction, which stands under either reading: what is open is what Beijing wanted from its acts, not which way they pulled the companies.
- **Where it would be.** A Chinese readout of the leaders' exchanges or of the trade talks would be on the foreign ministry's or the commerce ministry's site, or with the state news agency. The American written readouts would be on the White House's or the Trade Representative's site.
- **Looked for.** By the argument's own account, none of the saved official pages on the talks (the two sides' readouts, joint statements and fact sheets, and notices of a round to come) names Nvidia, the H20 or the H200, nor do the President's own posts on four calls with Xi and on two rounds. One Chinese official text sets the chip beside the talks: asked on Jul 18, 2025 about American officials' remark that approving H20 sales was part of the trade negotiations, the commerce ministry's spokesperson first described what followed the London talks and then said "美方近日又主动表示将批准对华销售英伟达H20芯片" (the US side has recently also said, of its own accord, that it will approve sales of Nvidia's H20 chips to China); the answer neither confirms nor denies that the chip was part of the talks [cn-factions-mofcom-us-initiative-20250718]. No American written readout of the rounds in London, Madrid, Kuala Lumpur, Paris and Korea, of the round of September 2026, of the two leaders' own meeting in Shenzhen or of the video calls of Dec 5, 2025 and Apr 30, 2026 was found in the Treasury's indexes of readouts and press releases, the Trade Representative's monthly lists of press releases or the White House's lists of its posts, as read on Nov 24, 2026. The foreign ministry's list of news on the United States names five calls between Xi and the President from January 2025 to November 2026; both sides' accounts of all five are saved, and none names chips.
- **Held now instead.** No Chinese document found states a motive for the unpublished guidance to companies, or ties an act against Nvidia to the trade talks; Beijing's own texts give purposes for its public acts (security, national interests, fair competition). American officials and bodies read it in more than one way. The US-China Economic and Security Review Commission, in its 2025 report to Congress, saw in the move against Nvidia of China's market regulator, the State Administration for Market Regulation, "China's willingness to both exert leverage and ease pressure as a negotiating tactic" [cn-usdocs-uscc-2025-ch1]. The President wrote on Dec 8, 2025 that he had told Xi the H200 would be allowed, and: "President Xi responded positively!" [cn-clips-followup-3-relay1] That is the President's account, not Xi's words; no Chinese readout of that exchange is among the saved pages.
