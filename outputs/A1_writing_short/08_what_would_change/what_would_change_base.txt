## The answer these would test

From 2024 to 2026 the Chinese state's declared policy, and what its regulators did, pulled China's AI companies away from Nvidia chips. The companies went on using those chips, ordering them and asking for them. Five limits apply. First, Beijing was not one actor. Second, the pressure came as public warnings and unpublished guidance, not as a published ban. Third, some purchases were approved and some chips came in. Fourth, the companies had reasons of their own, and American curbs and conditions pushed too. Fifth, what Beijing meant by it is not settled.

Each document below says which limit it bears on. None would turn the direction round: it stands on documents already held (see the last section).

**4. A Chinese text that says what Beijing meant by it**
- **What it would show.** Whether Beijing meant its acts against Nvidia as lasting policy or as leverage in trade talks. It bears on the fifth limit. It would not change the direction, which stands under either reading: what is open is what Beijing wanted from its acts, not which way they pulled the companies.
- **Where it would be.** A Chinese readout of the leaders' exchanges or of the trade talks would be on the foreign ministry's or the commerce ministry's site, or with the state news agency. The American written readouts would be on the White House's or the Trade Representative's site.
- **Held now instead.** No Chinese document found states the motive. American bodies read it two ways. The US-China commission, in its 2025 report to Congress, saw in the market regulator's move against Nvidia "China's willingness to both exert leverage and ease pressure as a negotiating tactic" [cn-usdocs-uscc-2025-ch1]. [cn-clips-followup-3-relay1] That is the President's account, not Xi's words; no Chinese readout of that exchange is among the saved pages. By the argument's own account, none of the official readouts of the talks that were saved names Nvidia, the H20 or the H200, and the American written readouts of seven rounds of talks were not found.

**6. Customs figures fine enough to show these chips**
- **What it would show.** How much of this kind of processor entered the mainland, month by month. It would test the third limit with a count that does not rest on unnamed people.
- **Where it would be.** In the customs administration's trade statistics, at the level of single tariff lines.
- **Looked for.** Public trade figures do not show how many of these chips entered China: each customs line holds every maker's goods, and no saved page says under which line the chips are declared. One line did move: China's imports of parts and accessories of computers were US$82.2 billion in January to September 2026, against US$18.9 billion a year earlier [cn-o34-gacc-t14-imports-202609] [cn-le18-gacc-t14-202509]. That line cannot count Nvidia chips.
- **Held now instead.** One press report and two statements on the record. The Financial Times, through Reuters, Aug 18, 2026: "Small batches of Nvidia's H200 chips, one of the company's most powerful AI chips, have been allowed to enter mainland China", and ByteDance and Tencent "have each received about 10,000 H200 processors in recent weeks" [cn-tolerate-reutersaug26-relay1-wayback]. Nvidia's report for the quarter to Jul 26, 2026: "we have made a fraction of the allowed shipments under the USG's H200 licensing program" [man-ms-sec-10q-2026-07]. Nvidia, on its results call for the quarter ended Oct 25, 2026: "Q3 sales of licensed Hopper Data Center products to China was $2.4 billion" [cn-o38-nvda-q3fy27-call-transcript-pdf]. None of these says how many of those chips crossed into the mainland.

**8. Nvidia's quarterly report for the quarter ended Oct 25, 2026**
- **What it would show.** Whether Nvidia's sentence on the Chinese government is kept, changed or dropped, and whether the report says why shipments stopped. Nvidia has given no reason so far.
- **Where it would be.** In the Securities and Exchange Commission's filing system, where the report for the quarter to Jul 26, 2026 is [man-ms-sec-10q-2026-07].

An application names a consignee; it is not an order, and an approval there is Washington's licence, not Beijing's.

## What is not open

These parts of the answer rest on documents already held. A new find could add to them; it would not unsay them.

- China's declared policy is to end dependence on foreign core technology and to raise the share of home-made chips.
- Its regulators opened a case against Nvidia and summoned it.
- Nvidia told securities regulators in November 2025 that the Chinese government "restricted customers from purchasing, importing, or using our data center products", and, two sentences later, that "we are already effectively foreclosed from the China market by U.S. export controls" [man-ms-sec-10q-2025-10]. Alibaba told them the cyberspace regulator asked companies to halt purchases of certain chips.
- A Shanghai government body says internet companies' clusters in the city run mainly on Nvidia.
- By May 11, 2026 Washington had approved 879,868 of Nvidia's and AMD's chips, none yet shipped, and returned 1,888,950 without action.
