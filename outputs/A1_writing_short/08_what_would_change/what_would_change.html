China chip policy · the documents that would settle it

What would change the answer

The documents that would settle the open points if they were found. For each: what it would show, where it would be, and what the project holds now instead.

Draft by Claude, Anthropic's model. Written Nov 23, 2026; brought into line with the argument on Nov 25, 2026 (UTC).

None of these documents was held when this was written, and nothing below says that any of them exists. Read every would as if found. Where a document would be kept is said from general knowledge of how such bodies publish, not from a saved page, unless a reference is given.

Several were looked for from Nov 23 to Nov 25, 2026 by other parts of this project. What was found is given in a line that begins Looked for. Those lines follow the argument's own account of what was searched and its list of what it leaves open; five notes that looked on Nov 23 are named near the end.

Words between quotation marks were found in the saved copy by a script. English given for Chinese sources is ours, not an official translation. A filled number is a document, a filing or a named person on the record. An open number is a press report citing unnamed people, or a caption track or machine transcript that nobody has checked by ear.

  • 1 a filled number: a document, a filing, a named person on the record, or a published commentary of state media or an industry body
  • 2 an open number: a press report citing unnamed people, a research firm's count, or captions and machine transcripts nobody has checked by ear

The answer these would test

From 2024 to 2026 the Chinese state's declared policy, and what its regulators did, pressed China's AI companies to move away from Nvidia chips. The companies went on using those chips, ordering them and asking for them. Five limits apply. First, Beijing was not one actor. Second, the pressure came as public warnings and unpublished guidance, not as a published ban. Third, some purchases were approved and some chips came in. Fourth, the companies had reasons of their own, and American curbs and conditions pushed too. Fifth, what Beijing meant by it is not settled.

Each document below says which limit it bears on. None would turn the direction round: it stands on documents already held (see the last section).

Documents from Beijing

1. A published Chinese rule or notice on importing these chips

  • What it would show. Whether bringing an H200 into the mainland needs a permit, and whether one was refused. It bears on the second limit. It would also test Luckey's words “China will not give them an import permit” 1 (a machine transcript of the clip, at 0:25; not checked by ear), which say more than the record shows.
  • Where it would be. On the site of the commerce ministry or of the customs administration, as a list of restricted imports or a licence notice.
  • Held now instead. No such rule was found in the 82 policy pages read, nor in China's own import lists: the lists of goods that need an import licence, an automatic import licence or a dual-use import licence, for 2024, 2025 and 2026, read whole by script, name no computer, computer part or chip (no customs number under headings 8471, 8473 or 8542), though the same search finds the computers and chips that the dual-use list controls for export 234; nor do the lists of banned imports in force 5; the sixth batch, of 2005, lists 17 kinds of asbestos and chemicals 6. The announcements of the customs administration and of the Customs Tariff Commission found for January 2024 to November 2026, read whole with their annexes by a search of Nov 25, 2026, set no licence, hold or chip-specific inspection on importing these goods, and none names Nvidia or a chip model; that search is not a census of every numbered announcement. The Foreign Trade Law does allow a restriction outside the lists: the commerce ministry, with the State Council's approval and on the grounds the law lists, may “临时决定限制或者禁止前款规定目录以外的特定货物、技术的进口或者出口” (decide for a time to restrict or ban the import or export of particular goods and technologies outside the catalogues) 7; no such decision naming these chips was found. The Customs Law says that restricted goods without licence documents are not released, “国家限制进出口的货物,没有进出口许可证件的,不予放行” 8, but none of the pages read is a list that names these chips. On Jul 30, 2026 a company asked the commerce ministry whether such an import needs a special licence, “进口是否需要办理专用进口许可证” 9. The ministry's reply of Aug 19, 2026 speaks only of exporters and export controls; it does not answer the question about imports. One listed company, 智微智能 (stock code 001339), which buys AI servers made in part by foreign makers, told the Shenzhen exchange in September 2026 that it had compared them with three Chinese catalogues, of technologies banned or restricted from import, of goods under import licence, and of dual-use items and technologies under import and export licence: “智算服务器及配套设备不涉及目录项下禁止或限制进口的产品或技术” (the servers and their equipment are not products or technologies banned or restricted from import under the catalogues); the reply names no maker of those servers or of their chips 10. A Hong Kong paper's account of a Financial Times report, Aug 19, 2026, says purchases “需通過國家發改委審批” (must pass the state planner's approval) 11: an approval step is reported, though no rule for it was found. Of another Nvidia card, the RTX PRO 5500, Reuters reported on Nov 23, 2026, from two unnamed people, that large purchases by internet companies “could require Chinese government approval” 12. Reuters's words are that Beijing “withholds approval for large-scale imports” 13. On the record, Nvidia said on its results call of May 20, 2026: “we are uncertain whether any imports will be allowed into the country” 14. That sentence names no rule and no authority.

2. The instructions to the companies, in writing

  • What it would show. Which body told which companies what, about which chips, and whether any paper exists at all. It bears on the second limit: the instructions are known from press reports citing unnamed people and from one sentence in a company filing.
  • Where it would be. With the cyberspace regulator, the industry ministry or the state planner (NDRC), the bodies the reports name. A text of this kind has been published before. In 2023 the Cybersecurity Review Office published, on the cyberspace regulator's site, its conclusion on Micron's products: operators of critical information infrastructure “应停止采购美光公司产品” (shall stop buying Micron's products) 15.
  • Looked for. By the argument's own searches (its point 11): of the latest annual reports of fifteen US-listed Chinese companies, only Alibaba's says a Chinese authority asked Chinese technology companies to stop buying certain chips; in the 76 results-call transcripts of eight Chinese companies, November 2023 to November 2026, and 42 records of four smaller model makers, no speaker and no record names a Chinese authority as restricting chip purchases, though 27 of the transcripts leave out passages spoken in another language; in Chinese records of 25 of those 27 calls no speaker names a Chinese authority beside a chip word either. For the paper on window guidance below, neither web searches for its words nor the titles on the state planner's and the finance ministry's lists of documents turned up a copy (its point 15).
  • Held now instead. One sentence in Alibaba's annual report, which does not name Nvidia: “In September 2025, the Cyberspace Administration of China requested Chinese technology companies to halt purchases of certain chips.” 16 The Financial Times, Aug 20, 2025, from unnamed people: the cyberspace regulator issued what the paper calls “window guidance”, “an informal notice”, “instructing them to halt new orders for Nvidia's H20 chips” 17. The same paper, Sept 17, 2025, by three people it did not name: the regulator “told companies, including ByteDance and Alibaba, this week to end their testing and orders of the RTX Pro 6000D” 18. A Chinese trade site, Leiphone, in an article the argument dates to Oct 31, 2025, described a newly circulating paper on such guidance: “有最新流传的‘窗口指导’相关文件写道” 19. It gives no issuer, number or date, and no copy of the paper was found.

3. Beijing's approvals of early 2026, in writing

  • What it would show. Which body approved which purchases, and on what conditions. It would move the first and third limits from press reports to the record.
  • Where it would be. It would be a document of the industry ministry, the commerce ministry or the state planner, the bodies the reports name.
  • Looked for. The commerce ministry was asked at its own press conferences, on Jan 22, 2026 whether the government had told companies it would approve H200 purchases only in special cases, “只有在特殊情况下才会批准他们购买H200芯片”, and on Mar 19, 2026 whether China had approved purchases, “中国是否已经批准了中国公司采购H200芯片”. Both times it said “我不了解你提到的情况” (I am not aware of what you mention) 2021.
  • Held now instead. Press reports citing unnamed people. Reuters, Jan 28, 2026, by four people it did not name: three companies “have been approved to purchase more than 400,000 H200 chips in total” 22. Reuters, Jan 30, 2026, reporting that DeepSeek too had been approved: “China's industry and commerce ministries have granted approvals for all four companies”, with the conditions left to the state planner 23. Reuters, Mar 17, 2026, by sources it did not name: “Nvidia has won Beijing's approval to sell its second-most powerful artificial intelligence chips to China” 24. Of a later card, the RTX PRO 5500, The Information reported, as Reuters relayed it on Sept 27, 2026 and “could not immediately verify”, that the industry ministry “told some Chinese companies the government intends to approve the purchases” 25: an intention reported, not an approval given.

4. A Chinese text that says what Beijing meant by it

  • What it would show. Whether Beijing meant its acts against Nvidia as lasting policy or as leverage in trade talks. It bears on the fifth limit. It would not change the direction, which stands under either reading: what is open is what Beijing wanted from its acts, not which way they pulled the companies.
  • Where it would be. A Chinese readout of the leaders' exchanges or of the trade talks would be on the foreign ministry's or the commerce ministry's site, or with the state news agency. The American written readouts would be on the White House's or the Trade Representative's site.
  • Looked for. By the argument's own account, none of the saved official pages on the talks (the two sides' readouts, joint statements and fact sheets, and notices of a round to come) names Nvidia, the H20 or the H200, nor do the President's own posts on four calls with Xi and on two rounds. One Chinese official text sets the chip beside the talks: asked on Jul 18, 2025 about American officials' remark that approving H20 sales was part of the trade negotiations, the commerce ministry's spokesperson first described what followed the London talks and then said “美方近日又主动表示将批准对华销售英伟达H20芯片” (the US side has recently also said, of its own accord, that it will approve sales of Nvidia's H20 chips to China); the answer neither confirms nor denies that the chip was part of the talks 26. No American written readout of the rounds in London, Madrid, Kuala Lumpur, Paris and Korea, of the round of September 2026, of the two leaders' own meeting in Shenzhen or of the video calls of Dec 5, 2025 and Apr 30, 2026 was found in the Treasury's indexes of readouts and press releases, the Trade Representative's monthly lists of press releases or the White House's lists of its posts, as read on Nov 24, 2026. The foreign ministry's list of news on the United States names five calls between Xi and the President from January 2025 to November 2026; both sides' accounts of all five are saved, and none names chips.
  • Held now instead. No Chinese document found states a motive for the unpublished guidance to companies, or ties an act against Nvidia to the trade talks; Beijing's own texts give purposes for its public acts (security, national interests, fair competition). American officials and bodies read it in more than one way. The US-China Economic and Security Review Commission, in its 2025 report to Congress, saw in the move against Nvidia of China's market regulator, the State Administration for Market Regulation, “China's willingness to both exert leverage and ease pressure as a negotiating tactic” 27. The US Trade Representative, Jamieson Greer, called buying the H200 “a sovereign decision for China” (captions, not checked by ear, at 5:59) 28. The President wrote on Dec 8, 2025 that he had told Xi the H200 would be allowed, and: “President Xi responded positively!” 29 That is the President's account, not Xi's words; no Chinese readout of that exchange is among the saved pages.

5. A notice that ends the antitrust case against Nvidia, or says that it goes on

  • What it would show. Whether the case has ended, and when. It bears on the fifth limit. It would not by itself show what the case was for.
  • Where it would be. On the market regulator's site, where its two announcements on the case are 3031, and where it posted the suspension of its case against DuPont China 32.
  • Looked for. The regulator's lists were read again on Nov 24, 2026: its antitrust bureau's list of work news has nothing on Nvidia after the notice of Sept 15, 2025 33.
  • Held now instead. No saved page says the case ended. The regulator's review of its 2025 work, posted Apr 10, 2026, still lists “对英伟达公司实施进一步调查” (a further investigation of Nvidia) 34, and its annual report on antitrust enforcement for 2025, posted Jun 5, 2026, lists the further investigation with no outcome 3536. Its news list, as read on Nov 23, 2026, has a suspension of the case against DuPont China and none for Nvidia 3237. The White House said on Nov 1, 2025: “China will terminate its various investigations targeting U.S. companies in the semiconductor supply chain, including its antitrust, anti-monopoly, and anti-dumping investigations” 38. Nvidia's report of August 2026 still lists the regulator's finding among its risks 39.

Figures

6. Customs figures fine enough to show these chips

  • What it would show. How much of this kind of processor entered the mainland, month by month. It would test the third limit with a count that does not rest on unnamed people.
  • Where it would be. In the customs administration's trade statistics, at the level of single tariff lines.
  • Looked for. Public trade figures do not show how many of these chips entered China: each customs line holds every maker's goods, and no saved page says under which line an importer in China or Hong Kong declares the chips. Nvidia's own classification list, “provided for informational purposes only”, gives the subheadings it applies itself: 8471.80 for its HGX H200 and HGX H20 parts, 8473.30 for its H200 NVL and H20 NVL16 parts and 8471.50 for its DGX H200 systems 4041. One line did move: China's imports of parts and accessories of computers were US$82.2 billion in January to September 2026, against US$18.9 billion a year earlier 4243. That line cannot count Nvidia chips.
  • Held now instead. One press report and two statements on the record. The Financial Times, through Reuters, Aug 18, 2026: “Small batches of Nvidia's H200 chips, one of the company's most powerful AI chips, have been allowed to enter mainland China”, and ByteDance and Tencent “have each received about 10,000 H200 processors in recent weeks” 44. Nvidia's report for the quarter to Jul 26, 2026: “we have made a fraction of the allowed shipments under the USG's H200 licensing program” 39. Nvidia, on its results call for the quarter ended Oct 25, 2026: “Q3 sales of licensed Hopper Data Center products to China was $2.4 billion” 45. None of these says how many of those chips crossed into the mainland.

7. A Hong Kong trade or warehouse record of the stored chips

  • What it would show. Whether about 600,000 chips are in Hong Kong, as one report says.
  • Where it would be. In Hong Kong's published trade statistics, or in a warehouse or shipping record.
  • Looked for. Another note of this project (idea 42) looked. Nothing official was found that bears out the count, and nothing that refutes it.
  • Held now instead. One Reuters report of Nov 5, 2026, citing two unnamed people: “About 600,000 of Nvidia's advanced artificial intelligence chips are stored in Hong Kong awaiting shipment to China” 13. Asked about that report on Nov 9, 2026, the foreign ministry's spokesman said to ask the departments in charge 46.

Documents from the United States

8. Nvidia's quarterly report for the quarter ended Oct 25, 2026

  • What it would show. Whether Nvidia's sentence on the Chinese government is kept, changed or dropped, and whether the report says why shipments stopped. Nvidia has given no reason so far.
  • Where it would be. In the Securities and Exchange Commission's filing system, where the report for the quarter to Jul 26, 2026 is 39.
  • Looked for. It was not yet filed when looked for on Nov 23, 2026: the commission's record of the company's filings, fetched that day, lists none for that quarter 47, nor did it when fetched again on Nov 24, 2026, with filings up to Nov 23 48, or on Nov 25, 2026 at 10:45 UTC 49. Nvidia's commentary on the quarter says: “We plan to file our 10-Q in the coming weeks.” 50
  • Held now instead. Nvidia's words to the commission changed from report to report. November 2025: the Chinese government “restricted customers from purchasing, importing, or using our data center products” 51. The annual report of February 2026: “discouraged customers from purchasing, importing, or using our data center products” 52. August 2026: the licensed sales “were restricted by the PRC government” 39. For the latest quarter there is Nvidia's commentary, “Shipments of Data Center Hopper products to China during the quarter were $2.4 billion” 50, and its results call: “we are no longer shipping licensed Hopper Data Center products to China” 45. Reuters wrote that its then interim chief executive “did not explain why Nvidia had stopped shipping the processors or whether the halt would be permanent” 53. Nvidia, in a statement carried by The Standard (page of Nov 20, 2026): “despite receiving some licenses, we have since stopped shipping H200s to China” 54. The page does not say on which day the statement was made, and the statement gives no reason either.

9. An official record of the Commerce Secretary's words at the hearing of Apr 22, 2026

  • What it would show. The exact words of the Commerce Secretary, Howard Lutnick, from the Senate itself. They are held from a commercial transcript: “my understanding is their cloud companies want to buy these chips” 55.
  • Where it would be. With the Government Publishing Office, if the hearing is printed, or on the site of the Senate appropriations committee.
  • Looked for. The committee's page for the hearing offers the video and his prepared remarks, and no transcript 56, as again on Nov 24, 2026 57. The Government Publishing Office's list of the 409 Senate hearing prints of the 119th Congress issued or changed since Apr 22, 2026 holds none of this hearing 58. A search found the same sentence in the committee video's own captions, at 1:17:28 to 1:17:47; that is a reading, with no saved copy.
  • Held now instead. That commercial transcript. And the letter Senator Chris Coons sent the Secretary on May 5, 2026, which records the answer the same way: “you responded in the negative, noting that while your department has licensed the export of these semiconductors to China, the PRC government has not allowed the sale” 59. The letter also challenges it. It says Nvidia's chief executive had told reporters that Nvidia “had received necessary approvals from both the United States and PRC governments”, and: “Your statements before the committee appear to contradict Huang's comments.” 59

10. Washington's reasons for the applications it returned without action

  • What it would show. Why Washington returned so many licence applications without action. It bears on the fourth limit: how much of what stood in the H200's way was on the American side.
  • Where it would be. With the Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security, or in the fuller papers of the House Foreign Affairs Committee, which made the table public on Nov 19, 2026 60.
  • Held now instead. The table of licence applications from the Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security, with their status on May 11, 2026 61. Of the 3,444,160 chips in it, Nvidia's H200 and AMD's MI325X together, 1,888,950 are in applications marked RWA: that is 55% of the chips in the applications the summary records (the sums are ours, by script, from the table's 54 rows). RWA is the bureau's short form for “returned without action (RWA)” 62. Another 879,868 are in applications marked “Approved, not shipped” 61. The table gives no reason; one possible reason is that “The applicant has requested the application be returned” 63. The committee says some applications are “not fully reflected in the May 11, 2026, summary document”: “For example, NVIDIA applied on December 19, 2025, to export 445,000 H200s to Tencent” 64. The table's only Tencent row of that date is an Nvidia application for 75,000 chips, approved, and neither document says whether it is that application. So the 55% is a share of the table, not of everything applied for: counted with the 445,000, it would be 49.5% if that row is the application and 48.6% if the 445,000 is a further one (our arithmetic). The bureau's rule published on Nov 19, 2026 adds a licence requirement for advanced chips bound for Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand or Brazil, “countries which present a higher-than-normal risk of diversion”, except for companies headquartered in the United States; it does not speak of the returned applications 65. An application names a data-centre operator or ultimate consignee; it is not an order, and an approval there is Washington's licence, not Beijing's.

What is not open

These parts of the answer rest on documents already held. A new find could add to them; it would not unsay them.

  • China's declared policy is to end dependence on foreign core technology and to raise the share of home-made chips.
  • Its regulators opened a case against Nvidia and summoned it.
  • Nvidia told securities regulators in November 2025 that the Chinese government “restricted customers from purchasing, importing, or using our data center products”, and, two sentences later, that “we are already effectively foreclosed from the China market by U.S. export controls” 51. Alibaba told them the cyberspace regulator asked companies to halt purchases of certain chips.
  • A Shanghai government body says internet companies' clusters in the city run mainly on Nvidia.
  • By May 11, 2026 Washington had approved 879,868 of Nvidia's and AMD's chips, none yet shipped, and returned 1,888,950 without action.
