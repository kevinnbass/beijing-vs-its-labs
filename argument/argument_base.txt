# Did Beijing's chip policy pull against China's own AI companies?
@deck Yes, on direction, with five limits: four on how, and one on what Beijing meant by it. Every statement below carries a numbered reference to a saved source.

**The answer.** From 2024 to 2026 the Chinese state's declared policy, and what its regulators did, pressed China's AI companies to move away from Nvidia chips. The companies went on using those chips, ordering them and asking for them. Five limits apply. First, Beijing was not one actor: its security and planning side pressed against the chips, its commerce and foreign-affairs side was more open to them. Second, the pressure came as public warnings and unpublished guidance, not as a published ban; the state-media warnings found came mostly in one burst, from Jul 31 to Sept 24, 2025 (the counts are in points 4 and 18). Third, some purchases were approved and some chips came in. Fourth, the companies had reasons of their own, and American curbs and conditions pushed too: Nvidia's reports since November 2025 say "we are already effectively foreclosed from the China market by U.S. export controls" [man-ms-sec-10q-2025-10], and of the 3,444,160 chips in the licence applications that Washington's summary of May 11, 2026 records, 1,888,950 (55%; the sum is mine) were in applications it returned without action; its table gives no reason, and the committee that released it says the summary does not show every application in full [cn-usdocs-hfac-decisions-20261119] [cn-usdocs-hfac-summary-20261119]. Fifth, what Beijing meant by it is not settled: the US-China commission saw in the market regulator's move against Nvidia, which came as the regulator dropped an antitrust probe into Google, "China's willingness to both exert leverage and ease pressure as a negotiating tactic" [cn-usdocs-uscc-2025-ch1], while the Trade Representative, asked whether China would let its companies buy the H200, said it was "gonna be a sovereign decision for China" (captions, not checked by ear, at 5:59 in the recording) [cn-le01-greer-bbg-20260515-subs-ttml]. The direction of the policy stands under either reading of what Beijing meant by it.

## 1. The state's declared line: use home-made chips

1. **The leader.** Xi Jinping said in 2016 that the internet's core technology is China's greatest weak point and that having core technology controlled by others is its greatest hidden danger: "互联网核心技术是我们最大的“命门”，核心技术受制于人是我们最大的隐患" [cn-xi-20160419]. In the same speech he said that an internet company, however large, whose core components depend heavily on foreign countries and whose supply chain's weak point is in others' hands is like a house built on someone else's foundation: "那就好比在别人的墙基上砌房子" [cn-policy-xi-coretech-2016]. In April 2025 he told the Politburo to concentrate forces on high-end chips: "集中力量攻克高端芯片、基础软件等核心技术" [cn-xi-round-j-6]. In October 2025 the Central Committee's recommendations for the next five-year plan called for extraordinary measures, "采取超常规措施", in integrated circuits and other fields [cn-policy-15fyp-recommendations]. The line held at the top in 2026, in the same general words: the government work report of Mar 5, 2026 calls for building integrated circuits into an emerging pillar industry, "打造集成电路、航空航天、生物医药、低空经济等新兴支柱产业" [cn-ga31-workreport-7], and the five-year plan outline asks for an independently controllable software and hardware ecosystem for computing, "加快培育自主可控、协同运行的软硬件生态" [cn-ga31-workreport-8]. Neither names a foreign firm or says what companies may buy, and the same work report promises to "积极扩大进口" (expand imports actively) [cn-ga31-workreport-7]. A search of the People's Daily database of his speeches and activities for Nvidia, its chief executive's name, the H20 and the H200 found nothing, while the same search finds American firms' names: Microsoft and Intel in his own speech of 2016 [cn-xi-20160419], and Qualcomm once, in the Xinhua report of his meeting of Mar 27, 2024 that People's Daily carried, among the American guests who spoke [cn-ga03-jhsjk-gaotong-3-20240328]; the search's totals count more than that (of its 38 items for Microsoft, 21 hold the word only in a font name; of its 8 for Qualcomm, 7 hold the two characters inside other words). The database lacks at least one of his meetings of the period: of 156 of his items of 2023 to November 2026 found in Xinhua's and Qiushi's own lists by words on chips, computing power, AI or American firms, it holds 150, and the only speech, meeting, letter or article among the six it lacks is his meeting of May 14, 2026 with American executives [cn-acts-xi-us-business-20260514], the other five being joint statements; of the 124 of those items whose only such word is AI, 21 were checked against the database, and Xinhua's own text of the other 103 names no chip, no Nvidia, no H20 or H200 and not Nvidia's chief executive. So this is a strong negative, not a complete one (the searches, with their controls, are in tables/xi_searches.tsv).

2. **The rules.** Notices of the finance and industry ministries, published on Dec 26, 2023, require Party and government bodies at township level and above, the public institutions directly under Party committees and governments at those levels, and the departments' public institutions that provide support to those bodies, to write into their purchase requirements for desktop computers and general-purpose servers that the processor and the operating system meet the security-and-reliability evaluation: "应当将CPU、操作系统符合安全可靠测评要求纳入采购需求" [cn-policy-desktop-2023] [cn-policy-server-procurement-2023]. The two evaluation centres' results of May 26, 2026, on one centre's own site, list nine AI training and inference chips that passed, every one from a maker registered in China [cn-le14-itsec-2026-2]; the evaluation guide says only an entity registered in China may apply, "送测单位应为中国境内注册的实体" [cn-le14-itsec-scope-20230727]; their results of Sept 20, 2026 add two more, HiSilicon's Ascend 950 and Kunlunxin's M100 [cn-le14-itsec-2026-3]. Firms apply of their own accord, and firms and users choose whether to use the results: "测评结果由企业和用户自主选择使用" [cn-le14-itsec-scope-20230727]. Where home-made and other products compete in one government purchase, home-made products get a 20% price preference in the evaluation: "对本国产品的报价给予20%的价格扣除" [cn-policy-domestic-product-standard-2025]. The test is where a product is made (and, once shares are set product by product, whether a set share of its components, by cost, is made in China), not who owns its maker: state, private and foreign-funded firms "平等享受对本国产品的政府采购支持政策" (enjoy the purchasing support for home-made products equally), and regions and departments may not, in government purchasing, name a brand or restrict where a brand is registered or who owns it [cn-policy-domestic-product-standard-2025]. Xi himself told representatives of international business on Mar 28, 2025 that China would see that products foreign-funded firms make in China take part equally, by law, in government purchasing, "保障外资企业在华生产的产品依法平等参与政府采购" [cn-ga03-jhsjk-xi-intl-business-20250328]; the words cover products made in China, not imported chips. A tender that allows no imported products can still be awarded to servers that carry Nvidia chips: a tender document of March 2025 for the University of the Chinese Academy of Sciences, issued by the purchasing centre for central state bodies, asks "是否允许进口产品" [cn-ga31-h20notices-19] (are imported products allowed), answers 否 (no) and gives its four GPU servers' origin as home-made; the award of Apr 24, 2025 includes four Dell servers, each with "1*NVIDIA HGX H20 8-GPU SXM 96GB 500W GPUs模组" [cn-ga31-h20notices-7] (one NVIDIA HGX H20 eight-GPU module). An award is a contract decision, not a delivery. Some central buyers' notices of November 2026 ask for home-made computing outright: the drug regulator's medical-device evaluation centre rented an "8卡国产人工智能算力环境" (an eight-card home-made AI computing environment) and asked for its existing applications to be moved "从原人工智能计算架构向国产人工智能计算架构" (from the original AI computing architecture to a home-made one), the original not named [cn-ga13-ccgp-nmpa-cmde-ai-rental-27742397]; Peking University sought eight inference cards, imports not accepted, for work "基于国产AI算力" (on home-made AI computing) [cn-ga13-ccgp-pku-inference-card-27734000].

3. **The targets, and the money.** Nine of China's 31 provincial-level governments were found to set a share for home-made computing hardware; one of the nine, Zhejiang's, is for the computing of one sector, its information and communications industry, by 2030, as the target of a project in that sector's five-year plan [cn-ga31-cndocs-52]. Two are worded as aims, with the word 力争 (strive to): Sichuan's and Guangdong's. Jiangsu's own table marks its figures as indicative, not binding [cn-factions-jiangsu-plan]. The sentences that set the shares of Shanghai, Tianjin, Hebei and Inner Mongolia carry no such word or label, and Shandong's plan is known only from a newspaper's account. Shanghai's two plans of July 2026 for the next five years set no home-made share [cn-ga05-shanghai-industry-15fyp-2026]; the digital one sets a share of green power, "新建智算中心绿电占比达80%以上" (80% or more in new intelligent-computing centres) [cn-ga05-shanghai-digital-15fyp-20260728], while a deputy director of the city's economy commission spoke in January 2026 of building a "国芯国模国云" system (home-made chips, models and clouds), with no figure [cn-ga05-shanghai-panyan-yicai-202601]. Of the other 22, 20 have a plan that was read and sets no share (Chongqing's plan of 2026 sets shares only for green power and for the uptake of new smart terminals and agents [cn-o39-chongqing-hub-plan-2026-wayback]). Beijing city's plan aims, by 2027, at an independently controllable full stack of hardware and software for intelligent computing and "具备100%自主可控智算中心建设能力" (the ability to build intelligent-computing centres that are 100% independently controllable) [cn-factions-beijing-plan-20240424]; Guizhou's aims at a national rank, "国产智算能力位居全国前列" [cn-o39-guizhou-ai-plan-2025-wayback]; Shaanxi's computing item sets no share; on building, it says it will "按需建设算力基础设施" (build computing infrastructure as needed) [cn-le16-shaanxi-ai-plus-2025-sohu]. Tibet's plan is known only from accounts of it, its issuer's own among them, whose targets name no home-made hardware [cn-ga05-tibet-xzca-compute-everest-plan-20240625], and for Xinjiang no computing plan was found. The kind of plan that holds Zhejiang's share, each provincial communications administration's five-year plan for its sector, was then searched as a class for the other 30 units: the plan for 2026 to 2030 was read in full for ten (Shanghai's and Shandong's in trade sites' copies) and as a consultation draft for two, only the plan for 2021 to 2025 for seven more, and for eleven no plan text was found; none of the texts read sets a home-made share (Beijing's, for one, asks to "加大力度适配国产算力芯片" (step up adaptation to home-made computing chips) [cn-ga31-inth2-bj15-plan-pdf], with no figure), so the nine remain a count of what was found, not a census. Cities, districts and provinces also pay part of the rent for computing through vouchers. ## 3. What Beijing did about it

Points 10 to 12 rest on documents, filings and named officials; points 13 to 16 on press reports citing unnamed people. Point 16 also sets Washington's own table of licence applications, a document, beside those reports.

**On the record**

10. **Regulators acted against Nvidia by name.** The market regulator announced on Dec 9, 2024 that it had opened an antitrust case: "市场监管总局依法对英伟达公司开展立案调查" [cn-policy-samr-open-original]. On Jul 31, 2025 the cyberspace regulator summoned Nvidia, "约谈了英伟达公司", over the security risks of vulnerabilities and backdoors in the H20 chips it sells in China [cn-policy-cac-h20-original]. On Sept 15, 2025 the market regulator announced a preliminary finding against Nvidia, and a further investigation: "市场监管总局依法决定对其实施进一步调查" [cn-policy-samr-further-original]. That notice cites the antimonopoly law and the regulator's 2020 conditions on Nvidia's purchase of Mellanox, and gives no facts. Nvidia's own account of the finding points the other way: "our compliance with applicable U.S. export controls, which required us to offer degraded products to the Chinese market, discriminated unfairly against customers in the China market" [man-ms-sec-10q-2026-07]. By that account the case faults Nvidia for offering Chinese customers degraded products, not for selling to them. The regulator's Party group, in the Party members' paper of Sept 24, 2026, counts the case as political work: "依法对英伟达、高通公司、杜邦中国集团立案调查，在重要时间节点上发布权威声音，有力维护国家利益" (it opened formal investigations into Nvidia, Qualcomm and DuPont China Group under the law, spoke with authority at important moments, and forcefully safeguarded national interests) [cn-media-party-samr-september]. Xinhua, in March 2026, had named the further investigation of Nvidia among the regulator's enforcement steps, and said the series of measures sends a clear signal of treating all kinds of companies alike: "坚持对各类企业一视同仁" [cn-media-xinhua-antitrust-equal]. One American body read the case as a lever in trade talks. The US-China commission's report of November 2025, citing Reuters and the Financial Times, says the finding came "in the midst of negotiations", as the regulator dropped an inquiry into Google, "signaling China's willingness to both exert leverage and ease pressure as a negotiating tactic" [cn-le03-uscc-2025-full]. The White House said on Nov 1, 2025 that "China will terminate its various investigations targeting U.S. companies in the semiconductor supply chain, including its antitrust, anti-monopoly, and anti-dumping investigations" [cn-usside-017]; the commerce ministry's account of the deal names none of China's investigations of American firms and, of such companies, says only "相关企业个案处理" (handling the individual cases of the companies concerned) [cn-le25-mofcom-kl-arrangement-qa-20251030]. The Trade Representative's report of March 2026 says that by the end of 2025 China "had terminated or suspended these investigations", which "were being pursued for retaliatory purposes"; it names no company [cn-le03-ustr-nte-2026]. No page read says the Nvidia case ended: the regulator's review of its 2025 work, posted Apr 10, 2026, still lists "对英伟达公司实施进一步调查" [cn-le25-samr-2025-merger-review-20260410], and its annual report on antitrust enforcement for 2025, posted Jun 5, 2026, lists the further investigation with no outcome [cn-ga15-samr-antitrust-annual-2025-pdf] [cn-ga15-samr-antitrust-annual-2025-notice]; its news list to Nov 23, 2026 has a suspension of the case against DuPont China and none for Nvidia [cn-le25-samr-news-list-p08-20261123] [cn-le25-samr-news-list-p01-20261123], and its antitrust bureau's list of work news, read on Nov 24, 2026, has nothing on Nvidia after the notice of Sept 15, 2025 [cn-ga15-samr-fldes-worknews-list-20261124]; and Nvidia's report of August 2026 still lists the finding among its risks [man-ms-sec-10q-2026-07].

## 5. Five limits on the claim

17. **Beijing was not one actor.** Vice Premier He Lifeng told Nvidia's chief executive in April 2025 that more American firms, Nvidia among them, were welcome to put down deeper roots in China: "欢迎包括英伟达公司在内的更多美资企业深耕中国市场" [cn-policy-he-welcomes]. The commerce minister told him in July 2025 that he hoped multinationals, Nvidia among them, would give Chinese customers high-quality, reliable products and services: "希望包括英伟达在内的跨国公司为中国客户提供优质可靠的产品和服务" [cn-policy-wang-nvidia]. The Financial Times, Nov 13, 2026, describes the split: the commerce ministry "favoured imports", while security officials in the cyberspace regulator and the security ministry "viewed dependence on Nvidia as a strategic vulnerability"; "Over the past year, the latter position has gained ground." [man-e2596eab15f4] No other outlet was found reporting this split from sources of its own; the paper had described the like on Aug 20, 2025 [man-cn-ft-20250821-regulators]. The US Trade Representative said in November 2026 that the Chinese side is not monolithic (point 12). In the same days as the first of the summer's instructions (point 14), People's Daily Overseas Edition (Jul 22, 2025) printed an interview in which a university scholar, Zhang Xin, advised that "国内企业的最优策略是“双线并行”" (the best strategy for Chinese firms is two lines at once): "一边积极申请、储备进口高端芯片" (on one side, actively apply for and stock imported high-end chips), on the other fill the computing gap with innovation in algorithms and the home-made ecosystem [cn-media-pd-h20-gmw]. ## 6. What can be said, and how

!! cn-o34-gacc-t14-imports-202609 | Please refer to www.customs.gov.cn for the specific coverage of each category of this table
- **Flat, on documents and filings.** China's declared policy is to end dependence on foreign core technology and to raise the share of home-made chips (points 1 to 3). Its regulators opened a case against Nvidia and summoned it (point 10). Nvidia told securities regulators in November 2025 that the Chinese government "restricted customers from purchasing, importing, or using our data center products" (the annual report says "discouraged" [man-ms-sec-10k-fy2026]), and, two sentences later, that "we are already effectively foreclosed from the China market by U.S. export controls" [man-ms-sec-10q-2025-10]. Alibaba told them the cyberspace regulator asked companies to halt purchases of certain chips (point 11). A Shanghai government body says internet companies' clusters in the city run mainly on Nvidia (point 5). By May 11, 2026 Washington had approved 879,868 of Nvidia's and AMD's chips, none yet shipped, and returned 1,888,950 without action (section 4). Nvidia's revenue from customers based in China fell by about two thirds during 2025 and was, in the quarter ended Oct 25, 2026, the highest of the nine quarters reported on that basis; that is not a count of chips entering China (section 4).
- **In public, as warnings and urging, not as orders.** The security ministry's warning, the state-media items that only warn (16 of the 19 found are dated Jul 31 to Sept 24, 2025; the items after September 2025 are what two rounds of searches found, a sample and not a full count), the state broadcaster's account saying buyers could choose not to buy the H20, and an industry body's call for caution (points 4 and 18). Nothing read ties the state-media texts to the guidance the press reports, and a Party paper also printed a scholar's advice to stock imported chips (point 17).
- **As reported, from unnamed people.** Who gave the instructions to stop buying, and when; the warning about traitors; the customs checks; the 600,000 chips in Hong Kong; every number in the first table of section 4 except the United States government's count and Nvidia's own. Some of these rest on one outlet and some on more, each with its own sources: the warning about traitors on one (the Wall Street Journal), the port checks of October 2025 on one (the Financial Times), the rule for state-funded data centres on one (Reuters), the split inside Beijing on one (the Financial Times) and the 600,000 on one (Reuters); the pressure against the H20 in August 2025 on four, though they differ on whether firms were told to stop; the instruction on the RTX Pro 6000D on two (the Financial Times and Bloomberg); the H200 held by customs in January 2026 on two (Reuters and the South China Morning Post); and the go-ahead of late January 2026 on three, which Nvidia's chief executive disputed the next day.
- **As others read it.** That Beijing's acts were leverage in trade talks, or a sovereign choice with no bargain in them (point 21).
- **Not to be said.** That China banned Nvidia chips: no published ban was found, and some chips came in. That an import permit was refused: no permit rule for these chips was found in China's published import lists of 2024 to 2026 (point 18). That Beijing spoke with one voice: its commerce side and its security and planning side pulled apart (point 17). That the companies wanted only Nvidia: they also build and buy home-made chips. That the antitrust case shows Beijing wanted no Nvidia sales: by Nvidia's account the finding is that it offered Chinese customers degraded products. That the case was a bargaining move, or that it was not: both are readings (point 21). That only Beijing stood in the H200's way: Washington's conditions and a 25% tariff did too, and Washington returned without action 55% of the chips in the applications its summary of May 11, 2026 records. That Washington refused the chips it returned: the table gives no reason.

**One sentence that survives all five limits.** From 2024 the security and planning side of the Chinese state pressed China's AI companies, through unpublished guidance that the press reported, to buy home-made chips rather than Nvidia's, and from the summer of 2025 to stop ordering them, while the security ministry warned in public of backdoors in chips made abroad and state media warned about Nvidia's chips, most of all in August and September 2025; other parts of the state welcomed Nvidia and, by the reports, approved some purchases; the companies went on ordering Nvidia chips, in the millions by the press reports, and by those reports had received a small part of them by late 2026; they were also turning to home-made chips, for reasons of their own and under American curbs; and whether Beijing meant its acts as lasting policy or as leverage in trade talks is read both ways.
