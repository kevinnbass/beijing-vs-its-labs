# Did Beijing's chip policy pull against China's own AI companies?
@deck Yes, on direction, with five limits: four on how, and one on what Beijing meant by it. Every statement below carries a numbered reference to a saved source.

@note Written Nov 23, 2026 (UTC). Made public the same day. The figure that goes with it is in the same folder: figure/beijing_vs_labs.html.

**The answer.** From 2024 to 2026 the Chinese state's declared policy, and what its regulators did, pulled China's AI companies away from Nvidia chips. The companies went on using those chips, ordering them and asking for them. Five limits apply. First, Beijing was not one actor: its security and planning side pressed against the chips, its commerce and foreign-affairs side was more open to them. Second, the pressure came as public warnings and unpublished guidance, not as a published ban, and the seven state-media items found that only warn about Nvidia's chips all fall between Aug 1 and Sept 24, 2025; three later ones mix caution with openness to the H200. Third, some purchases were approved and some chips came in. Fourth, the companies had reasons of their own, and American curbs and conditions pushed too: Nvidia's reports since November 2025 say "we are already effectively foreclosed from the China market by U.S. export controls" [man-ms-sec-10q-2025-10], and of the 3,444,160 chips in the licence applications that Washington's summary of May 11, 2026 records, 1,888,950 (55%; the sum is mine) were in applications it returned without action; its table gives no reason, and the committee that released it says the summary does not show every application in full [cn-usdocs-hfac-decisions-20261119] [cn-usdocs-hfac-summary-20261119]. Fifth, what Beijing meant by it is not settled: the US-China commission saw in the market regulator's move against Nvidia, which came as the regulator dropped an antitrust probe into Google, "China's willingness to both exert leverage and ease pressure as a negotiating tactic" [cn-usdocs-uscc-2025-ch1], while the Trade Representative, asked whether China would let its companies buy the H200, said it was "gonna be a sovereign decision for China" (captions, not checked by ear, at 5:59 in the recording) [cn-le01-greer-bbg-20260515-subs-ttml]. The direction of the policy stands under either reading.

## 1. The state's declared line: use home-made chips

1. **The leader.** Xi Jinping said in 2016 that the internet's core technology is China's greatest weak point and that having core technology controlled by others is its greatest hidden danger: "互联网核心技术是我们最大的“命门”，核心技术受制于人是我们最大的隐患" [cn-xi-20160419]. In the same speech he said that an internet company, however large, whose core components depend heavily on foreign countries is like a house built on someone else's foundation: "那就好比在别人的墙基上砌房子" [cn-policy-xi-coretech-2016]. In April 2025 he told the Politburo to concentrate forces on high-end chips: "集中力量攻克高端芯片、基础软件等核心技术" [cn-xi-round-j-6]. In October 2025 the Central Committee's recommendations for the next five-year plan called for extraordinary measures, "采取超常规措施", in integrated circuits and other fields [cn-policy-15fyp-recommendations]. A search of the People's Daily database of his speeches and activities for Nvidia, its chief executive's name, the H20 and the H200 found nothing, while the same search finds Qualcomm, Microsoft and Intel in his texts; the database lacks at least one of his meetings of the period, so this is a strong negative, not a complete one (the searches, with their controls, are in tables/xi_searches.tsv).

2. **The rules.** Notices of the finance and industry ministries, published on Dec 26, 2023, require Party and government bodies at township level and above, and the public institutions attached to them, to write into their purchase requirements for desktop computers and servers that the processor and the operating system meet the security-and-reliability evaluation: "应当将CPU、操作系统符合安全可靠测评要求纳入采购需求" [cn-policy-desktop-2023] [cn-policy-server-procurement-2023]. The two evaluation centres' results of May 26, 2026, on one centre's own site, list nine AI training and inference chips that passed, every one from a Chinese maker [cn-le14-itsec-2026-2]; their results of Sept 20, 2026 add two more, HiSilicon's Ascend 950 and Kunlunxin's M100 [cn-le14-itsec-2026-3]. Firms apply of their own accord, and firms and users choose whether to use the results: "测评结果由企业和用户自主选择使用" [cn-le14-itsec-scope-20230727]. Home-made products get a 20% price preference in government purchasing: "对本国产品的报价给予20%的价格扣除" [cn-policy-domestic-product-standard-2025]. The test is where a product is made, not who owns its maker: state, private and foreign-funded firms "平等享受对本国产品的政府采购支持政策" (enjoy the purchasing support for home-made products equally), and regions and departments may not, in government purchasing, name a brand or restrict where a brand is registered or who owns it [cn-policy-domestic-product-standard-2025].

Cities, districts and provinces also pay part of the rent for computing through vouchers. Some pay more for home-made computing; every such rule found that is in force still pays for other computing too, at a lower rate, though Binjiang district, by its bureau's account, began paying for other computing only in May 2025 [cn-cnpress-binjiang-rental-reply-20260720]. Others pay one rate whatever the chip: Shenzhen's training voucher up to 50% of the contract [cn-le28-sz-gxj-2024-13hao], Wuhan's 50% [cn-le28-wuhan-2025-3hao], Qingdao's up to 20% [cn-le28-qingdao-2025-15hao]; Shanghai's pays the same rent share for any chip and supports building only home-made facilities [cn-le15-sheitc-8cea38]. One zone dropped its tier: Beijing's E-Town paid 40% and 30% under its rule of March 2024, and a later rule repeals that one and pays 30% whatever the chip, keeping up to 10% of the cost of home-made AI chip servers that a cloud firm buys [cn-le28-etown-29hao-20241126]. Counting one rule in force for each local government whose text is saved in a form that could be checked by script and which states a share of the bill, 4 of 15 pay more for home-made computing and 11 pay one rate; these are the rules found, not a census, and the figure lists the 15. Beijing city's own notes, held only as Word files, pay 20% whatever the chip and are not among the 15. The local rules found are in tables/local_vouchers.tsv and tables/local_vouchers_more.tsv. Two provinces reward data centres whose basic hardware and software are 90% or more home-made: a one-off reward of up to 5 million yuan for new or rebuilt centres in Shanxi [cn-o39b-shanxi-measures-2024-jincheng], and a reward of up to 10 million in Ningxia, by its industry department's account of a regional policy [cn-o39-ningxia-reply-2023].

4. **The agencies and the state's media, in public.** The Ministry of State Security warned in July 2025 that some chips, smart devices and software made abroad may have had backdoors planted on purpose when they were designed and made: "一些境外生产的芯片、智能设备或者软件可能在设计制造阶段就被故意预埋了“后门”" [cn-acts-mss-xinhua-20250721]. In May 2026 Li Chao, of the state planner's policy research office, said at its press conference that it was guiding home-made large models to do more to adapt to home-made computing chips: "指导国产大模型加大力度适配国产算力芯片" [cn-policy-ndrc-chip-adaptation-2026]. State media went further and named the chip. After the cyberspace regulator summoned Nvidia, a People's Daily commentary of Aug 1, 2025 said that 'sick' chips must never be allowed on duty: "绝不能让“带病”的芯片上岗" [cn-acts-peoplesdaily-bjd-20250801]. Yuyuan Tantian, an account of the state broadcaster [cn-le13-cja36-winners-xinhuanet-20261108], wrote on Aug 10, 2025 that the H20 was neither green, nor advanced, nor safe, and: "作为消费者，我们当然可以选择，不买" (as consumers we can of course choose not to buy) [cn-le13-yuyuan-qq-20250810b]. Of 42 state and Party media items on Nvidia chips found from July 2025 to November 2026, the seven that only warn about them all date from Aug 1 to Sept 24, 2025 [cn-acts-peoplesdaily-bjd-20250801] [cn-media-stdaily-security-forum]. No later item found is a warning alone; three, of Dec 13 and 14, 2025 and Jan 7, 2026, mix caution with openness to the H200, the Economic Daily piece quoted below among them (the count and the reading of each item are in tables/state_media_items.tsv). ## 3. What Beijing did about it

Points 10 to 12 rest on documents, filings and named officials; points 13 to 16 on press reports citing unnamed people. Point 16 also sets Washington's own table of licence applications, a document, beside those reports.

**On the record**

10. **Regulators acted against Nvidia by name.** The market regulator announced on Dec 9, 2024 that it had opened an antitrust case: "市场监管总局依法对英伟达公司开展立案调查" [cn-policy-samr-open-original]. On Jul 31, 2025 the cyberspace regulator summoned Nvidia, "约谈了英伟达公司", over the security risks of vulnerabilities and backdoors in the H20 chips it sells in China [cn-policy-cac-h20-original]. On Sept 15, 2025 the market regulator announced a preliminary finding against Nvidia, and a further investigation: "市场监管总局依法决定对其实施进一步调查" [cn-policy-samr-further-original]. That notice cites the antimonopoly law and the regulator's 2020 conditions on Nvidia's purchase of Mellanox, and gives no facts. Nvidia's own account of the finding points the other way: "our compliance with applicable U.S. export controls, which required us to offer degraded products to the Chinese market, discriminated unfairly against customers in the China market" [man-ms-sec-10q-2026-07]. By that account the case faults Nvidia for what it held back from Chinese customers, not for selling to them. The regulator's Party group, in the Party members' paper of Sept 24, 2026, counts the case as political work: "依法对英伟达、高通公司、杜邦中国集团立案调查，在重要时间节点上发布权威声音" (it opened cases against Nvidia, Qualcomm and DuPont China under the law, and spoke with authority at important moments) [cn-media-party-samr-september]. Xinhua, in March 2026, had called the same enforcement even-handed: "坚持对各类企业一视同仁" [cn-media-xinhua-antitrust-equal]. American bodies read the case as a lever in trade talks. The US-China commission's report of November 2025, citing Reuters and the Financial Times, says the finding came "in the midst of negotiations", as the regulator dropped an inquiry into Google, "signaling China's willingness to both exert leverage and ease pressure as a negotiating tactic" [cn-le03-uscc-2025-full]. The White House said on Nov 1, 2025 that "China will terminate its various investigations targeting U.S. companies in the semiconductor supply chain, including its antitrust, anti-monopoly, and anti-dumping investigations" [cn-usside-017]; the commerce ministry's account of the deal names none of China's investigations of American firms and, of such companies, says only "相关企业个案处理" (handling the cases of the companies concerned) [cn-le25-mofcom-kl-arrangement-qa-20251030]. The Trade Representative's report of 2026 says that by the end of 2025 China "had terminated or suspended these investigations", which "were being pursued for retaliatory purposes"; it names no company [cn-le03-ustr-nte-2026]. No page read says the Nvidia case ended: the regulator's review of its 2025 work, posted Apr 10, 2026, still lists "对英伟达公司实施进一步调查" [cn-le25-samr-2025-merger-review-20260410]; its news list to Nov 23, 2026 has a suspension of the case against DuPont China and none for Nvidia [cn-le25-samr-news-list-p08-20261123] [cn-le25-samr-news-list-p01-20261123]; and Nvidia's report of August 2026 still lists the finding among its risks [man-ms-sec-10q-2026-07].

## 5. Five limits on the claim

17. **Beijing was not one actor.** Vice Premier He Lifeng told Nvidia's chief executive in April 2025 that more American firms, Nvidia among them, were welcome to put down deeper roots in China: "欢迎包括英伟达公司在内的更多美资企业深耕中国市场" [cn-policy-he-welcomes]. The commerce minister told him in July 2025 that he hoped multinationals, Nvidia among them, would give Chinese customers high-quality, reliable products and services: "希望包括英伟达在内的跨国公司为中国客户提供优质可靠的产品和服务" [cn-policy-wang-nvidia]. The Financial Times, Nov 13, 2026, describes the split: the commerce ministry "favoured imports", while security officials in the cyberspace regulator and the security ministry "viewed dependence on Nvidia as a strategic vulnerability"; "Over the past year, the latter position has gained ground." [man-e2596eab15f4] The US Trade Representative said in November 2026 that the Chinese side is not monolithic (point 12). In the same days as the first of the summer's instructions (point 14), People's Daily Overseas Edition (Jul 22, 2025) printed an interview in which a university scholar, Zhang Xin, advised that "国内企业的最优策略是“双线并行”" (the best strategy for Chinese firms is two lines at once): "一边积极申请、储备进口高端芯片" (on one side, actively apply for and stock imported high-end chips), on the other fill the gap with algorithms and home-made chips [cn-media-pd-h20-gmw]. That is a scholar's printed advice, not an act of the state. On May 14, 2026, meeting the American executives who came with the President, Xi said: "中方欢迎美国对华加强互利合作，相信美国企业在华将拥有更广阔前景" (China welcomes more mutually beneficial American cooperation and believes American firms will have broader prospects in China) [cn-acts-xi-us-business-20260514]. Premier Li Qiang told them, Nvidia's among them, that a stable and open policy environment is the Chinese government's long-standing commitment: "一个稳定开放的政策环境是中国政府长期不变的承诺" [cn-xi-round-f-6]. Reuters, Jan 30, 2026, reporting that DeepSeek too had been approved: "China's industry and commerce ministries have granted approvals for all four companies" (DeepSeek, ByteDance, Alibaba and Tencent), with the conditions left to the state planner [cn-acts-reuters-deepseek-20260130]. The commerce ministry also eased Chinese firms' computing abroad: presenting on Oct 16, 2026 a draft that adds 23 AI technologies, computing clusters among them, to the catalogue of technologies restricted from export, it said exports of technology for "中国企业在境外自建自营算力中心" (Chinese firms' own computing centres abroad) would get "适用自动许可等便利措施" (automatic licences and other facilitation) [cn-tolerate-mof], and on Nov 17, 2026 it called the new American curbs on remote access interference in the "正常贸易合作" (normal trade cooperation) of Chinese firms with firms of other countries [cn-acts-mofcom-20261117]. Local voucher rules paid for computing that is not home-made too: of the 15 counted, 11 pay one rate whatever the chip and 4 pay a lower rate than for home-made (point 3). So the conflict ran through the state itself: its security and planning side against the companies, with the commerce side nearer to the companies.

18. **Guidance and public warnings, not a published ban.** No published Chinese order against buying Nvidia chips was found in the 103 policy pages read. Such an order could have been found: the 2023 review conclusion telling operators of critical information infrastructure to stop buying Micron's products is among those pages, "应停止采购美光公司产品" [cn-policy-micron-decision]. And when the finance ministry published a purchase bar by name, on Jun 22, 2026, "不得采购46家美国企业（不包括在华美资企业）生产的产品" (government buyers may not buy products made by 46 American firms, leaving out American-funded firms in China), its list, of defence and aerospace firms, named no chip maker [cn-le14-mof-us-firms-measures-20260622] [cn-le14-mof-us-firms-list-20260622]. The purchase rules in point 2 bind Party and government bodies, and the same sentence adds "其他单位可不在采购需求中提出此项要求" (other units may leave this requirement out) [cn-policy-desktop-2023]. Every home-made percentage found in a published text is a province's or a city's; the national computing-network opinions of December 2023 set none [cn-policy-computing-network-1779]. ## 6. What can be said, and how

- **Flat, on documents and filings.** China's declared policy is to end dependence on foreign core technology and to raise the share of home-made chips (points 1 to 3). Its regulators opened a case against Nvidia and summoned it (point 10). Nvidia told securities regulators in November 2025 that the Chinese government "restricted customers from purchasing, importing, or using our data center products" (the annual report says "discouraged" [man-ms-sec-10k-fy2026]), and, two sentences later, that "we are already effectively foreclosed from the China market by U.S. export controls" [man-ms-sec-10q-2025-10]. Alibaba told them the cyberspace regulator asked companies to halt purchases of certain chips (point 11). A Shanghai government body says internet companies' clusters in the city run mainly on Nvidia (point 5). By May 11, 2026 Washington had approved 879,868 of Nvidia's and AMD's chips, none yet shipped, and returned 1,888,950 without action (section 4). Nvidia's revenue from customers based in China fell by about two thirds during 2025 and was, in the quarter ended Oct 25, 2026, the highest of the nine quarters reported on that basis; that is not a count of chips entering China (section 4).
- **In public, as warnings and urging, not as orders.** The security ministry's warning, the state-media commentaries (found from August to September 2025), the state broadcaster's account saying buyers could choose not to buy the H20, and an industry body's call for caution (points 4 and 18). Nothing read ties the state-media texts to the guidance the press reports, and a Party paper also printed a scholar's advice to stock imported chips (point 17).
- **As reported, from unnamed people.** Who gave the instructions to stop buying, and when; the warning about traitors; the customs checks; the 600,000 chips in Hong Kong; every number in the first table of section 4 except the United States government's count and Nvidia's own.
- **As others read it.** That Beijing's acts were leverage in trade talks, or a sovereign choice with no bargain in them (point 21).
- **Not to be said.** That China banned Nvidia chips: no published ban was found, and some chips came in. That an import permit was refused: no permit rule was found. That Beijing spoke with one voice: its commerce side and its security and planning side pulled apart (point 17). That the companies wanted only Nvidia: they also build and buy home-made chips. That the antitrust case shows Beijing wanted no Nvidia sales: by Nvidia's account the finding is that it offered Chinese customers degraded products. That the case was a bargaining move, or that it was not: both are readings (point 21). That only Beijing stood in the H200's way: Washington's conditions and a 25% tariff did too, and Washington returned without action 55% of the chips in the applications its summary of May 11, 2026 records. That Washington refused the chips it returned: the table gives no reason.

**One sentence that survives all five limits.** From 2024 the security and planning side of the Chinese state pressed China's AI companies, through unpublished guidance that the press reported, to buy home-made chips rather than Nvidia's, and from the summer of 2025 to stop ordering them, while the security ministry warned in public of backdoors in chips made abroad and state media warned about Nvidia's chips, most of all in August and September 2025; other parts of the state welcomed Nvidia and approved some purchases; the companies went on ordering Nvidia chips in the millions and, by the reports, had received a small part of them by late 2026; they were also turning to home-made chips, for reasons of their own and under American curbs; and whether Beijing meant its acts as lasting policy or as leverage in trade talks is read both ways.
