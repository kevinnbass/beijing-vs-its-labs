# The short version: did Beijing's chip policy pull against China's own AI companies?

@note For the top of a post or a reply. The numbers are this text's own; the full argument is ARGUMENT_referenced.html in the same folder. Made public Nov 23, 2026.

China's declared chip policy pulled against what its own AI companies were buying. A Shanghai government body wrote in 2025 that the city's internet companies run their own clusters mainly on Nvidia cards [cn-counter-b11-5]. Nvidia told the SEC in a report signed Nov 19, 2025: "The Chinese government has encouraged customers to purchase from our China-based competitors and restricted customers from purchasing, importing, or using our data center products" [man-ms-sec-10q-2025-10]. Five cautions. No published ban was found in the policy pages read: state media warned in public, in a burst from Aug 1 to Sept 24, 2025 [cn-acts-peoplesdaily-bjd-20250801] [cn-media-stdaily-security-forum], and the guidance against buying is known from press reports citing unnamed people [cn-factions-bloomberg-flexibility-202409-relay1]. Beijing was split: by Reuters, from unnamed people, its industry and commerce ministries approved some purchases [cn-acts-reuters-deepseek-20260130]. Some chips were sold: $2.4 billion of Nvidia's licensed Hopper products to China in the quarter to Oct 25, 2026 [cn-o38-nvda-q3fy27-call-transcript-pdf]. The companies had reasons of their own, and American curbs pushed too: the same Nvidia passage says "we are already effectively foreclosed from the China market by U.S. export controls" [man-ms-sec-10q-2025-10]. And what Beijing meant is open: the US-China commission read the antitrust finding as leverage in trade talks [cn-le03-uscc-2025-full]; the Trade Representative (captions at 5:59, not checked by ear) calls whether China lets its companies buy "a sovereign decision for China" [cn-le01-greer-bbg-20260515-subs-ttml].
