# The short version: did Beijing's chip policy pull against China's own AI companies?

@note The argument in one paragraph, with its own reference numbers; the full argument is ARGUMENT_referenced.html in the same folder. Made public Nov 23, 2026.

China's declared chip policy pulled against the Nvidia chips its own AI companies were buying. A Shanghai government body wrote in 2025 that the city's internet companies run their own clusters mainly on Nvidia cards [cn-counter-b11-5]. Nvidia told the SEC in a report signed Nov 19, 2025: "The Chinese government has encouraged customers to purchase from our China-based competitors and restricted customers from purchasing, importing, or using our data center products" [man-ms-sec-10q-2025-10]. Five limits. No published ban was found in the policy pages read: state media warned in public (23 of the 34 items found that only warn are dated Jul 31 to Sept 24, 2025, and six more Dec 9, 2025 to Jan 14, 2026) [cn-ga02-gt-cac-summons-news-202507] [cn-acts-peoplesdaily-bjd-20250801] [cn-media-stdaily-security-forum], and the guidance against buying is known from press reports citing unnamed people [cn-factions-bloomberg-flexibility-202409-relay1]. Beijing was split: by Reuters, from unnamed people, its industry and commerce ministries approved some purchases [cn-ga31-sv1-reuters-deepseek-yahoo]. Some chips were sold: $2.4 billion of Nvidia's licensed Hopper products (the chip family that includes the H200) to China in the quarter to Oct 25, 2026 [cn-o38-nvda-q3fy27-call-transcript-pdf] [cn-acts-reuters-halt-20261118-relay2]. The companies had reasons of their own and used home-made chips as well: the president of ByteDance's cloud arm said in June 2026 "关于国产算力，我们用得非常多" (we use home-made computing a great deal) [cn-ga31-labsbytep3-11]. American curbs pushed too: the same Nvidia passage says "we are already effectively foreclosed from the China market by U.S. export controls" [man-ms-sec-10q-2025-10], and a Chinese listed company's board wrote in April 2026 that servers with Nvidia chips grew scarce "受美国人工智能芯片对华出口管制等因素影响" (under American export controls on AI chips to China, among other factors) [cn-ga31-dcstatewest-13]. And what Beijing meant is open: the US-China Economic and Security Review Commission read China's market regulator's accusation of antitrust violations against Nvidia, in September 2025, as leverage in trade talks [cn-le03-uscc-2025-full]; the US Trade Representative, Jamieson Greer, in a Bloomberg Television interview (captions at 5:59, not checked by ear) calls whether China lets its companies buy the H200 "a sovereign decision for China" [cn-le01-greer-bbg-20260515-subs-ttml]; and Kevin Hassett, on Fox News on Sept 25, 2025, as Taiwan's Central News Agency reports him, said China wanted "讓華為壟斷所有在華晶片銷售" (to let Huawei monopolise all chip sales in China) [cn-ga31-usoffwhitehouse-14].
