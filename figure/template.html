China · Nvidia chips · 2023 to November 2026

Beijing pushed for home-made chips. China’s AI companies still sought Nvidia.

%%N_EVENTS%% dated records show how Chinese authorities acted on Nvidia chips and how companies bought, used or sought them. The companies also bought and used home-made chips. Parts of Beijing, some local governments and some units of state-owned telecom carriers were more open to Nvidia.

  • Blue · Toward Nvidia: seeking, buying, using or welcoming it

%%SHARE%%

Nvidia’s own figures: revenue from customers based in China

Billions of dollars by quarter, from Nvidia’s reports to the SEC; Hong Kong included. Counted by where the direct customer has its head office, for every product. It is not a count of chips entering China. Nvidia’s note: “The end customer and shipping location may be different from our direct customers’ headquarters location.”

Revenue fell by about two thirds between the quarters ended April and October 2025. The latest quarter was the highest of the nine.

%%LEDGER%%

Read with care

  • Open marks rest on unnamed people. In the answers found, no Chinese authority confirmed any of them: asked about such reports, the foreign ministry said it was not aware of the matter (Aug 13, 2025), that the question should go to the competent departments (Jan 29, 2026), and that its position on US chips was consistent (Nov 9, 2026).
  • No published Chinese order against buying Nvidia chips was found in the policy pages and national document lists read. The published acts against Nvidia that were found were a summons and an antitrust case. Of %%SM_N%% state and Party media items on Nvidia and its chips found from %%SM_FROM%% to %%SM_TO%%, %%SM_W%% only warn. %%SM_WIN%% date from %%SM_W0%% to %%SM_W1%%. Of the %%SM_OUTSIDE%% outside those weeks, one is dated %%SM_EARLY%%, one %%SM_NOV%%, and %%SM_H200%% date from %%SM_H0%% to %%SM_H1%% in reports on the American easing for the H200. The last %%SM_LAST%% are in Global Times in 2026. %%SM_MIX%% items mix a warning with a welcome, %%SM_MIX_LATER%% after September 2025. These warnings are not orders. The searches and the scan of saved pages yielded a sample of what the outlets printed.
  • By subject, %%SM_AI%% of the later items concern chips for AI computing, including %%SM_AI_W%% that only warn. Another %%SM_PRODUCT%% concern products with an Nvidia chip. %%SM_COMPANY%% concern Nvidia as a company, and %%SM_OTHER%% concern other subjects. A separate January 2024 to June 2025 sample holds %%SM_BASE%% items, %%SM_COMP%% from outlets covered by the later sample and %%SM_GROUP%% from three People’s Daily group papers outside it. One baseline item warns against reliance on the H20. None of the %%SM_COMP%% comparable items was found to tell buyers not to buy Nvidia chips. Across both periods, %%SM_WIN%% of the %%SM_ALL_W%% warnings fall in those eight weeks. On Dec 3, 2024, the Internet Society of China called on companies to be careful in choosing to buy American chips, citing US export controls, and asked them to use chips made in China by domestic and foreign-funded firms alike.
  • Some purchases were reported approved by outlets citing people they did not name. Nvidia’s own filings report licensed sales to China. The filings and other records cite American curbs as causes far more often than they cite Chinese acts.
  • Records %%REC:cn-policy-samr-open-original%% and %%REC:cn-policy-samr-further-original%% are an antitrust case against Nvidia the company. By Nvidia’s own reports the preliminary finding concerns the degraded products that US export controls required it to offer the Chinese market. They are drawn orange as pressure on Nvidia, not as a wish for fewer Nvidia chips. The White House said on Nov 1, 2025 that China would end its investigations of US firms in the chip supply chain, naming none; neither the regulator’s own news list (read to Nov 23, 2026) nor Nvidia’s reports say the Nvidia case has ended, and the regulator’s review of its 2025 work, posted Apr 10, 2026, lists the further investigation.
  • What Beijing meant is not settled. American bodies read its antitrust moves as leverage or pressure: the US-China commission (2025), “exert leverage and ease pressure as a negotiating tactic”; the Congressional Research Service (Sept 2025), “to pressure Nvidia to provide its advanced chips to China”. Yet asked whether China would let its companies buy the H200, the Trade Representative said “that’s gonna be a sovereign decision for China” (May 15, 2026, Bloomberg Television; captions, not checked by ear, at 05:59). Either reading leaves the direction of the acts drawn here as it is.
  • The H200 also had to pass American conditions: testing in the United States, shipments to China and Macau of no more than half of the same product shipped for end use in the United States, and a 25% tariff (Commerce Department rule of Jan 15, 2026; Nvidia’s report for the quarter ended Jul 26, 2026). By May 11, 2026 Washington had returned without action the applications for %%HF_RWA%% of the %%HF_ALL%% chips listed, more than half of that list (the bars above). The committee that released the table says the summary does not show every application in full; its example is an Nvidia application of Dec 19, 2025 for 445,000 chips for Tencent; the table’s only Tencent row of that date is for 75,000, approved, and neither document says whether it is that application.
  • The chip at issue changed over time: the H20 (2024 to 2025), the RTX PRO 6000D (2025), the H200 (from December 2025), the RTX PRO 5500 (2026).
  • The records are a selection made to show both sides, not a census. The count of marks in a row is not a measure.
  • Records %%REC:cn-policy-micron-decision%% and %%REC:cn-policy-desktop-2023%% are not about Nvidia. Record %%REC:cn-policy-micron-decision%% is the 2023 review conclusion that failed Micron’s products, a published instruction to operators of critical information infrastructure to stop buying one foreign chip maker’s products; record %%REC:cn-policy-desktop-2023%% is a purchase rule for the desktop computers of Party and state bodies and of the institutions directly under them or supporting them. They are drawn in orange as steps away from foreign chips. In record %%REC:cn-le17-whuf-134391%% the universities issued their own procurement notices and held suppliers to the government procurement law’s conditions. This was not an act of the central government. Records %%REC:cn-tolerate-mof%% and %%REC:cn-acts-mofcom-20261117%% do not name Nvidia. They are drawn blue because they back Chinese firms’ computing abroad. The Financial Times, citing two unnamed people on Nov 27, 2025, said firms were training abroad “to access Nvidia’s chips” (record %%REC:cn-tolerate-ft-offshore%%). Records %%REC:cn-counter-b11-5%% and %%REC:cn-policy-qiushi-cuda-gap%% are a city body’s and a Party journal’s documents about what the companies use; they are drawn in the companies’ row.
