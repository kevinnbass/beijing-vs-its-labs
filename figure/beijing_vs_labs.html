Acted against Nvidia the company

  • Market regulator: an antitrust case against Nvidia the company416

The Financial Times (Nov 13, 2026, citing unnamed people): security-focused officials within the cyberspace regulator and the security ministry “viewed dependence on Nvidia as a strategic vulnerability”, the commerce ministry “favoured imports”, and the 2025 push to restrict “was led by the National Development and Reform Commission”. The US Trade Representative, Nov 20, 2026 (Bloomberg Television; machine captions, not checked by ear, 01:06 to 01:35): it is “not as monolithic as people would have you believe”; in his “own analysis” there are some who think “we’d like to have access” and “others who think, well, we don’t want American chips anyway”. The two caption tracks break the sentence differently, so whether he puts the split inside the government is not settled.

The market regulator’s Party group lists the Nvidia case, with its cases on Qualcomm and DuPont China, under “坚决落实党中央重大决策部署” (carrying out the Party centre’s major decisions) and “敢于斗争” (daring to struggle) (Party members’ paper, Sept 24, 2026). Nvidia’s reports say the preliminary finding concerns the “degraded products” US export controls required it to offer the Chinese market. A standing rule cuts the other way: the regulator’s 2020 approval of Nvidia’s Mellanox purchase obliges Nvidia to keep supplying its accelerators to the Chinese market, unless lifted since (the parties may apply to lift the conditions six years after they took effect) (decision).

Read with care

  • Open marks rest on unnamed people. In the answers found, no Chinese authority confirmed any of them: asked about such reports, the foreign ministry said it was not aware of the matter (Aug 13, 2025), that the question should go to the competent departments (Jan 29, 2026), and that its position on US chips was consistent (Nov 9, 2026).
  • No published Chinese order against buying Nvidia chips was found in the policy pages and national document lists read. The published acts against Nvidia that were found are a summons and an antitrust case. The public warnings found came in one burst: of 42 state and Party media items on Nvidia chips found from Jul 2025 to Nov 2026, the 7 that only warn about Nvidia’s chips all date from Aug 1, 2025 to Sept 24, 2025 (4 others mix a warning with a welcome), and none of them is an order. Earlier, on Dec 3, 2024, an industry body, the Internet Society of China, had called on companies to be careful in choosing to buy American chips, citing US export controls, and in the same sentence to use chips made in China by domestic and foreign-funded firms alike.
  • Records 4 and 16 are an antitrust case against Nvidia the company. By Nvidia’s own reports the preliminary finding concerns the degraded products that US export controls required it to offer the Chinese market. They are drawn orange as pressure on Nvidia, not as a wish for fewer Nvidia chips. The White House said on Nov 1, 2025 that China would end its investigations of US firms in the chip supply chain, naming none; neither the regulator’s own news list (read to Nov 23, 2026) nor Nvidia’s reports say the Nvidia case has ended, and the regulator’s review of its 2025 work, posted Apr 10, 2026, lists the further investigation.
  • What Beijing meant is not settled. American bodies read its antitrust moves as leverage or pressure: the US-China commission (2025), “exert leverage and ease pressure as a negotiating tactic”; the Congressional Research Service (Sept 2025), “to pressure Nvidia to provide its advanced chips to China”. Yet asked whether China would let its companies buy the H200, the Trade Representative said “that’s gonna be a sovereign decision for China” (May 15, 2026, Bloomberg Television; captions, not checked by ear, at 05:59). Either reading leaves the direction of the acts drawn here as it is.
  • The H200 also had to pass American conditions: testing in the United States, a cap at half of the shipments to American customers, and a 25% tariff (Commerce Department rule of Jan 15, 2026; Nvidia’s report for the quarter ended Jul 26, 2026). By May 11, 2026 Washington had returned without action 1,888,950 of the 3,444,160 chips in the applications listed, more than half of that list (the bars above). The committee that released the table says the summary does not show every application in full; its example is an Nvidia application of Dec 19, 2025 for 445,000 chips for Tencent, which the table shows at 75,000.
  • The chip at issue changed over time: the H20 (2024 to 2025), the RTX Pro 6000D (2025), the H200 (from December 2025).
  • The records are a selection made to show both sides, not a census. The count of marks in a row is not a measure.
  • Records 1 and 2 are not about Nvidia. Record 1 is the 2023 review conclusion that failed Micron’s products, a published instruction to operators of critical information infrastructure to stop buying one foreign chip maker’s products; record 2 is a purchase rule for Party and state bodies’ desktop computers. They are drawn in orange as steps away from foreign chips. Record 30 is two universities’ own buying, in notices that hold suppliers to the government procurement law’s conditions; it is not an act of the central government. Records 35 and 37 do not name Nvidia; they are drawn blue because they back Chinese firms’ computing abroad, where by record 47 Nvidia chips are used. Records 42 and 55 are a city body’s and a Party journal’s documents about what the companies use; they are drawn in the companies’ row.
  • English renderings of Chinese are mine, not official translations.
