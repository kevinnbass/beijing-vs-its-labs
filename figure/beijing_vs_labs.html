Other published texts favour home-made computing. Inner Mongolia offers land, green power and energy-use quotas to firms building very large centres on home-made computing (regional measures). The Suzhou High-tech Zone management committee in Anhui says in a reply on the city’s site that the science ministry’s trial guide requires at least 60% of nominal computing power to come from independently developed chips for designation as a national AI computing platform (the committee’s reply). The ministry’s own copy was not found.

In its practice guide for AI accelerator chips, the secretariat of the national cybersecurity standards committee advises against functions for remote shutdown, location or data exchange with undeclared communications entities. The provision names no maker. The guide is guidance, not a binding standard.

Acted against Nvidia the company

  • Market regulator: an antitrust case against Nvidia the company416

The Financial Times (Nov 13, 2026): security-focused officials within the cyberspace regulator and the security ministry “viewed dependence on Nvidia as a strategic vulnerability”, the commerce ministry “favoured imports”, and, by people the paper does not name, the push later in 2025 to restrict the import and use of less advanced Nvidia chips “was led by the National Development and Reform Commission”. The US Trade Representative, Nov 20, 2026 (Bloomberg Television; machine captions, not checked by ear, 01:06 to 01:35): it is “not as monolithic as people would have you believe”; in his “own analysis” there are some who think “we’d like to have access” and “others who think, well, we don’t want American chips anyway”. The two caption tracks break the sentence differently, so whether he puts the split inside the government is not settled.

An unsigned article on the work of the market regulator’s Party group lists the Nvidia case, with its cases on Qualcomm and DuPont China, under “坚决落实党中央重大决策部署” (carrying out the Party centre’s major decisions) and “敢于斗争” (daring to struggle) (Party members’ paper, Sept 24, 2026). Nvidia’s reports say the preliminary finding concerns the “degraded products” US export controls required it to offer the Chinese market. A standing rule cuts the other way: the regulator’s 2020 approval of Nvidia’s Mellanox purchase obliges Nvidia to keep supplying its accelerators to the Chinese market, unless lifted since (the parties may apply to lift the conditions six years after they took effect) (decision).

Read with care

  • Open marks rest on unnamed people. In the answers found, no Chinese authority confirmed any of them. Asked about such reports, the foreign ministry said it was not aware of the matter (Aug 13, 2025), that the question should go to the competent departments (Jan 29, 2026), and that its position on US chips was consistent (Nov 9, 2026). The ministry’s 47 regular press conferences of Sept 14 to Nov 25, 2026 were read in full under the census’s rule for chip-related questions. No answer in them confirms or denies a restriction, an instruction or an approval. At the Nov 12, 2026 press conference, an Agence France-Presse reporter said that the procurement records of Northwestern Polytechnical University in Xi’an showed that the university had bought Nvidia H200 chips. The ministry said it was unfamiliar with the specifics the reporter mentioned and that it opposed American discriminatory lists against Chinese institutions.
  • No published Chinese order against buying Nvidia chips was found in the policy pages and national document lists read. The published acts against Nvidia that were found were a summons and an antitrust case. Of 297 state and Party media items on Nvidia and its chips found from Jul 2025 to Nov 2026, 34 only warn. Of those, 33 concern chips for AI computing. The other is a Science and Technology Daily report of a forum about home-made software and hardware at large. 23 of the warnings date from Jul 31, 2025 to Sept 24, 2025, including 22 about chips for AI computing. Of the 11 outside those weeks, one is dated Jul 24, 2025, one Nov 1, 2025, and 6 date from Dec 9, 2025 to Jan 14, 2026 in reports on the American easing for the H200. The last 3 are in Global Times in 2026. 14 items mix a warning with a welcome, 10 after September 2025. These warnings are not orders. The searches and the scan of saved pages yielded a sample of what the outlets printed.
  • By subject, 219 of the later items concern chips for AI computing. Another 40 concern products with an Nvidia chip. 8 concern Nvidia as a company, and 30 concern other subjects. A separate January 2024 to June 2025 sample holds 174 items, 160 from outlets covered by the later sample and 14 from three People’s Daily group papers outside it. One baseline item warns against reliance on the H20. None of the 160 comparable items was found to tell buyers not to buy Nvidia chips. Across both periods, 23 of the 35 warnings fall in those eight weeks. On Dec 3, 2024, the Internet Society of China called on companies to be careful in choosing to buy American chips, citing US export controls, and asked them to use chips made in China by domestic and foreign-funded firms alike.
  • Nvidia’s own filings report licensed sales to China, and Reuters reported, citing people it did not name, that some purchases were approved (record 22). The filings and other records cite American curbs as causes far more often than they cite Chinese acts.
  • Records 4 and 16 are an antitrust case against Nvidia the company. By Nvidia’s own reports the preliminary finding concerns the degraded products that US export controls required it to offer the Chinese market. They are drawn orange as pressure on Nvidia, not as a wish for fewer Nvidia chips. The White House said on Nov 1, 2025 that China would end its investigations of US firms in the chip supply chain, naming none; neither the regulator’s own news list (read to Nov 23, 2026) nor Nvidia’s reports say the Nvidia case has ended, and the regulator’s review of its 2025 work, posted Apr 10, 2026, lists the further investigation.
  • What Beijing meant is not settled. American bodies read its antitrust moves as leverage or pressure: the US-China commission (2025), “exert leverage and ease pressure as a negotiating tactic”; the Congressional Research Service (Sept 2025), “to pressure Nvidia to provide its advanced chips to China”. Yet asked whether China would let its companies buy the H200, the Trade Representative said “that’s gonna be a sovereign decision for China” (May 15, 2026, Bloomberg Television; captions, not checked by ear, at 05:59). The acts can be read as lasting policy, leverage in trade talks or protection of the home market. The direction of the acts drawn here stands under each of the three readings.
  • The H200 also had to pass American conditions: testing in the United States, shipments to China and Macau of no more than half of the same product shipped for end use in the United States, and a 25% tariff (Commerce Department rule of Jan 15, 2026; Nvidia’s report for the quarter ended Jul 26, 2026). By May 11, 2026 Washington had returned without action the applications for 1,888,950 of the 3,444,160 chips listed, more than half of that list (the bars above). The committee that released the table says the summary does not show every application in full; its example is an Nvidia application of Dec 19, 2025 for 445,000 chips for Tencent; the table’s only Tencent row of that date is for 75,000, approved, and neither document says whether it is that application. Reuters reported in May 2026, citing two people it did not name, that “each approved customer can purchase up to 75,000 chips”. In August 2026, the Financial Times reported that the United States “cleared the companies to purchase up to 100,000 H200 chips each” (ByteDance and Tencent). Reuters relayed the Financial Times report, which cited two people it did not name (record 33).
  • The chip at issue changed over time: the H20 (2024 to 2025), the RTX PRO 6000D (2025), the H200 (from December 2025), the RTX PRO 5500 (2026).
  • The records are a selection made to show both sides, not a census. The count of marks in a row is not a measure.
  • Records 1 and 2 are not about Nvidia. Record 1 is the 2023 review conclusion that failed Micron’s products, a published instruction to operators of critical information infrastructure to stop buying one foreign chip maker’s products; record 2 is a purchase rule for the desktop computers of Party and state bodies and of the institutions directly under them or supporting them. They are drawn in orange as steps away from foreign chips. In record 30 the universities issued their own procurement notices and held suppliers to the government procurement law’s conditions. This was not an act of the central government. Records 35 and 37 do not name Nvidia. They are drawn blue because they back Chinese firms’ computing abroad. The Financial Times, citing two unnamed people on Nov 27, 2025, said firms were training abroad “to access Nvidia’s chips” (record 47). Records 42 and 55 are a city body’s and a Party journal’s documents about what the companies use; they are drawn in the companies’ row.
