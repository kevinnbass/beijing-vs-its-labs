# The short version: did Beijing's chip policy pull against China's own AI companies?

The argument in one paragraph, with its own reference numbers; the full argument is ARGUMENT_referenced.html in the same folder. Made public Nov 23, 2026.

China's declared chip policy pulled against what its own AI companies were buying. A Shanghai government body wrote in 2025 that the city's internet companies run their own clusters mainly on Nvidia cards [1]. Nvidia told the SEC in a report signed Nov 19, 2025: "The Chinese government has encouraged customers to purchase from our China-based competitors and restricted customers from purchasing, importing, or using our data center products" [2]. Five limits. No published ban was found in the policy pages read: state media warned in public (16 of the 19 items found that only warn are dated Jul 31 to Sept 24, 2025) [3] [4] [5], and the guidance against buying is known from press reports citing unnamed people [6]. Beijing was split: by Reuters, from unnamed people, its industry and commerce ministries approved some purchases [7]. Some chips were sold: $2.4 billion of Nvidia's licensed Hopper products to China in the quarter to Oct 25, 2026 [8]. The companies had reasons of their own, and American curbs pushed too: the same Nvidia passage says "we are already effectively foreclosed from the China market by U.S. export controls" [2]. And what Beijing meant is open: the US-China Economic and Security Review Commission read China's market regulator's accusation of antitrust violations against Nvidia, in September 2025, as leverage in trade talks [9]; the US Trade Representative, Jamieson Greer, in a Bloomberg Television interview (captions at 5:59, not checked by ear) calls whether China lets its companies buy the H200 "a sovereign decision for China" [10].
